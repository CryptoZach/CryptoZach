    Operational Risk in Token Economies: How Infrastructure Failures Strengthen Adaptive Controllers and Break Reputation Systems

    What happens to token mechanisms when the underlying infrastructure fails? Using a 624-run simulation calibrated to observed failure rates from Ethereum, Helium, Geodnet, and Filecoin, this paper finds a split result: adaptive emission controllers stabilize rewards after infrastructure shocks, while reputation-weighted governance shifts power toward the largest holders as smaller operators absorb more downtime and lose influence. In plain terms, the same shock can steady one part of the system while pushing governance toward plutocracy, or rule by wealth, in another.

  • SSRN ~20,900 words Mar 1, 2026

    Adaptive Tokenomics: A Systems Engineering Approach to Programmable Incentive Design

    This paper builds a complete token economy for a fictional DePIN network, or decentralized physical infrastructure network, called MeshNet and stress-tests it across 240 simulations under four adverse scenarios. It uses PID-controlled emissions, meaning an automatic feedback loop that reacts to how far the network is from target, how long it has been off target, and whether it is moving back toward target. The main finding is that penalties for failures or rule violations drive supply dynamics more than emission policy under ordinary shocks. The feedback loop usually smooths volatility, but in a long downturn it can store up too much correction and then overshoot when conditions change. That problem, called integral wind-up, is delayed overcorrection by the controller, not a death spiral or self-reinforcing collapse in the network itself.

  • Final draft ~13,200 words Mar 2026

    GeoDePIN: Design Patterns for Decentralized Geographic Infrastructure

    Geo-dependent DePIN networks face problems that purely digital systems do not: they must verify physical coverage, respond to locally heterogeneous demand, and support operators whose economics depend on place-specific hardware deployment. This paper develops a taxonomy for those design problems, tracks Helium's subsidy-to-revenue ratio from 0.013 to 2.06 across 34 months of on-chain data, and decomposes the transition from subsidy dependence to net-deflationary operation. Twelve tables tie each design claim to validated data sources (29 of 29 paper claims verified).

  • Final draft ~11,500 words Mar 2026

    Umbrella Thesis: Tokenomics as Distributed Institutional Design

    Token design borrows from economics and mechanism design, but it is rarely judged by standards of legitimacy. This paper operationalizes five normative traditions into testable hypotheses, then grounds them in a governance concentration cross-section of twelve protocols (seven DeFi, five DePIN) using on-chain holder data. The empirical result: DePIN protocols concentrate governance power roughly twice as much as comparable DeFi systems, with near-universal token inequality across the twelve-protocol sample.
