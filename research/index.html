RESEARCH

Seven-paper research program across two tracks

Overview defines the objects. Frameworks defines the tools. Research shows the evidence.

Track A studies digital-dollar products under stress: gateways, reserve chains, settlement dependencies, and the control layer that determines who bears the obligations and who absorbs the losses.

Track B studies token networks under stress: reward schedules, governance thresholds, infrastructure reliability, and the operating economics that determine whether participation stays productive or concentrates into extraction.

Both tracks converge on the same conclusion: the policy-relevant unit is the operator, not the instrument. Classify the token, but regulate and audit the operator.

Routing the Dollar

The same stablecoin becomes a different policy object depending on who routes it.

How the gateway that routes a dollar stablecoin changes its regulatory exposure, its response to Fed policy, and its behavior under stress.

What it is: A data-driven study of 19 gateway entities and 51 wallet addresses showing that the same dollar token inherits different regulatory obligations, different Fed-policy sensitivity, and different crisis behavior depending on who routes it. Introduces the CLII, which measures how heavily regulated each gateway is across five dimensions (licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions). A high CLII score means a gateway faces more regulatory obligations; stress performance depends on entity-specific conditions the CLII does not measure. Circle and Coinbase had near-identical CLII scores but showed sharply different stress retention during SVB, consistent with entity-specific banking exposure driving outcomes. These are descriptive findings, not causal estimates.

Why it matters: Moves stablecoin analysis from token labels to operators, reserve chains, and routing networks. SVB provides the richest stress variation; BUSD (where Paxos guaranteed 1:1 redemption and there was no peg pressure) is the cleaner routing test. In both episodes, capital rotated across dollar gateways, staying within dollar-pegged instruments.

Who it's for: Policy, central bank, market-structure, and tokenization-strategy readers.

Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack

If equivalence is not proven under stress, it is not ready for committee.

What tokenized products must prove before institutions should treat them as equivalent.

What it is: Ten diligence categories, each with a required artifact and pass/fail test, applied in v9 to nine major products across three regulatory tiers. The manuscript adds collateral interoperability analysis (Binance Portfolio Margin, Ceffu custody), independent corroboration from three scholarly sources, and an OCC GENIUS Act implementing rule readout with a submitted comment letter. SVB and follow-on stablecoin contagion anchor the framework; Tier 1 products under banking law and the 1940 Act satisfy every category, while no stablecoin product does.

Why it matters: Turns tokenized-product diligence from pitch-deck review into a repeatable committee standard with named evidence requirements and explicit pass/fail tests. v9 shows how three Treasury products under three regimes can receive comparable collateral treatment on one exchange through one custodian without supervisory coverage of that boundary. The governance section documents that on-chain verification of the four largest tokenized Treasury products reveals no timelocks in any admin chain.

Who it's for: Asset managers, risk committees, tokenization platforms, custodians, legal and compliance teams, exchanges evaluating collateral acceptance, and policy designers and regulators.

    Evidence type: Simulation + systems engineering

    Adaptive Tokenomics

    Adaptive emissions are conditional insurance with a defined boundary.

    Token design as systems engineering.

    What it is: A complete token economy (MeshNet) built as a systems-engineering design laboratory and stress-tested across a 240-run Monte Carlo simulation plus additional sensitivity checks covering prolonged bear markets, competitor entry, regulatory shocks, and combined stress. Input parameters are drawn from real hardware costs across twelve DePIN protocols. Finds that adaptive emissions function as downside insurance (compressing worst-case outcomes by roughly 6x), slashing dominates token burns during the early growth phase, and the most important results depend on market conditions and parameter settings. Running the model once hides rare but severe downside scenarios that only appear when you run it hundreds of times with different starting conditions.

    Open paper summary → SSRN
    More on relevance and audience

    Why it matters: Shows which token mechanisms earn their complexity and where they break. PID emission control helps under demand contraction but can overcorrect in tail scenarios, applying a supply-side lever to a demand-side problem.

    Who it's for: Protocol designers, DePIN teams, mechanism designers, and governance researchers.

    Evidence type: 624-run simulation + operational-risk standards

    Operational Risk in Token Economies

    Infrastructure failure can stabilize controllers and break governance at the same time.

    What happens when infrastructure fails while operators stay.

    What it is: A 624-configuration simulation study using real outage and failure rates from Ethereum, Helium, Geodnet, and Filecoin. Finds that adaptive emission controllers become more resilient under operational risk (the counterintuitive result: infrastructure shocks strengthen the controller's advantage over static policy). Reputation-weighted governance moves in the opposite direction, and the outcome depends entirely on one design parameter: the uptime threshold operators must clear to recover their standing. When the designer sets that bar at 99%, real-world outage rates make recovery nearly impossible, and reputation scores collapse to an average of 0.18 on a 5.0 scale by year five. When the designer lowers the bar to 95%, the system stabilizes.

    Open paper summary → SSRN
    More on relevance and audience

    Why it matters: The collapse traces to a single design parameter: the recovery threshold was set above what operators can reliably achieve. The fix is a single parameter change (0.99 to 0.95). Risk is assigned at the infrastructure-group level; whether smaller operators face systematically higher risk is explicitly future work.

    Who it's for: Protocol designers, infrastructure operators, operator-risk teams, and governance researchers.

Same thesis, different laboratories

Track A shows that the same dollar token changes when routed through different gateways, reserve chains, and control layers.

Track B shows that the same network changes when run under different reward schedules, governance gates, and infrastructure conditions.

Cross-track comparison

Dimension Track A (stablecoins) Track B (token networks)
Where control concentrates The intermediary layer, not the issuance layer. Three gateways (Binance, Circle, Tether Treasury) account for 84% of identified network volume. A single market maker, Wintermute, grew from 1.4% to 19.9% of counterparty volume over 35 months while Cumberland declined from 3.3% to 0.1%. The share of counterparties connecting multiple gateways fell from 5.8% to 2.6% even as total volume doubled. Entity-level HHI averages 2,742, straddling the DOJ "highly concentrated" threshold. More value is flowing through fewer intermediaries with fewer cross-connections. Governance voting power, through secondary market dynamics that design choices do not reliably predict. Across 40 protocol-token systems (February 2026 snapshot), holding inequality is universal (Gini 0.72 to 0.98) while concentration varies by an order of magnitude (HHI 0.009 to 0.593). Neither initial allocation (r = +0.08, p = 0.64), revenue, subsidy intensity, nor token maturity predicts where a protocol lands. Insider-heavy protocols have concentrated governance ecosystems, not merely concentrated insider positions: non-insider HHI correlates with insider presence (rho = 0.54, p = 0.001). Delegation amplifies concentration in DePIN (DIMO 6.0x, WXM 3.3x) but compresses it in some infrastructure protocols (OP 0.79x). A separate measurement finding: naive holder lists inflate concentration by up to 5x when staking contracts, exchange custodians, and vesting locks are counted as governance participants. Across 17 protocols, 56 such addresses required exclusion.
What happens under stress Capital rotates across dollar gateways rather than exiting dollar-pegged instruments. During SVB, Circle (1.39x retention) and Coinbase (2.63x) diverged sharply despite near-identical CLII scores; the gap traced to banking exposure, not regulatory tier. Curve 3pool, a permissionless Tier 3 protocol, absorbed the most displaced volume at 12.35x. During the BUSD wind-down, where Paxos guaranteed 1:1 redemption and no peg pressure existed, users chose exchange continuity over regulatory protection: Wintermute's volume surged from $4.7B to $24.4B mediating the BUSD-to-USDT migration into Binance, while Paxos as a Binance counterparty declined 88%. In both events the salient variation was entity-level, not tier-level. CLII captures compliance posture, not resilience. Adaptive emission control raises the floor under worst-case outcomes rather than improving averages. An adaptive controller calibrated to Helium parameters (240-run simulation) reduces the spread of network size outcomes by sixfold compared to fixed emission, raising the worst-case from roughly 1,000 to roughly 9,000 nodes. The compression is strongest under bear markets (CV 0.219 vs. 0.958) but inverts under regulatory shock, where fixed emission produces marginally lower price variance. Adaptive control does not dominate uniformly; the contribution is identifying where, on which dimension, and under which conditions each mechanism provides value.
Where the economics sit On the operator balance sheet, transmitted through reserve yield. Stablecoin supply cointegrates with Fed balance-sheet variables, but the relationship is regime-dependent: the trace statistic rejects strongly during tightening (48.18 vs. cv95 of 29.80) and fails during easing (17.71). After the September 2024 rate cut, supply nearly doubled from $162B to $305B. The relationship is dollar-specific; Bitcoin and Ethereum produce a null. The transmission operates primarily on the issuer side through a T-bill-minus-DeFi-rate spread, and the yield prohibition in recent U.S. stablecoin legislation reinforces this channel. In the burn mechanism and service delivery layer. Helium's S2R rose from 0.013 to 1.84 over 34 months. Fee growth drove 58% of the improvement (weekly DC burns surged 20x, from $10.7K to $217K), with price decline contributing 25% and the emission halving 17%. The crossover is fragile on two dimensions: roughly five purchasers account for 90% of token burns (burn-signer HHI = 0.22), and WiFi offload pricing may overstate steady-state equilibrium by 2 to 5x. Across DePIN more broadly, subsidy dependence varies enormously: DIMO earns three times more in revenue than it pays in token rewards (0.33x), while Livepeer pays 88 times more in rewards than it earns (88.5x). Revenue-price decoupling (8x revenue growth while HNT declined 77%) is consistent with S2R capturing demand-side fundamentals independently of token price dynamics.

Two research tracks, one conclusion: the token is the surface; control, failure, and revenue resolve at the operator layer.
