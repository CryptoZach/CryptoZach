RESEARCH

Eight-paper research program across two tracks

Overview defines the objects. Frameworks defines the tools. Research shows the evidence.

Track A studies digital-dollar products under stress: gateways, reserve chains, settlement dependencies, and the control layer that determines who bears the obligations and who absorbs the losses.

Track B studies token networks under stress: reward schedules, governance thresholds, infrastructure reliability, and the operating economics that determine whether participation stays productive or concentrates into extraction.

Both tracks converge on the same conclusion: the policy-relevant unit is the operator, not the instrument. Classify the token, but regulate and audit the operator.

Routing the Dollar

The same stablecoin becomes a different policy object depending on who routes it.

How the gateway that routes a dollar stablecoin changes its regulatory exposure, its response to Fed policy, and its behavior under stress.

What it is: A data-driven study of 19 gateway entities and 51 wallet addresses showing that the same dollar token inherits different regulatory obligations, different Fed-policy sensitivity, and different crisis behavior depending on who routes it. Introduces the CLII, which measures how heavily regulated each gateway is across five dimensions (licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions). A high CLII score means a gateway faces more regulatory obligations; stress performance depends on entity-specific conditions the CLII does not measure. Circle and Coinbase had near-identical CLII scores but showed sharply different stress retention during SVB, consistent with entity-specific banking exposure driving outcomes. These are descriptive findings, not causal estimates.

Why it matters: Moves stablecoin analysis from token labels to operators, reserve chains, and routing networks. SVB provides the richest stress variation; BUSD (where Paxos guaranteed 1:1 redemption and there was no peg pressure) is the cleaner routing test. In both episodes, capital rotated across dollar gateways, staying within dollar-pegged instruments.

Who it's for: Policy, central bank, market-structure, and tokenization-strategy readers.

Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack

If equivalence is not proven under stress, it is not ready for committee.

What tokenized products must prove before institutions should treat them as equivalent.

What it is: Ten diligence categories, each with a required artifact and pass or fail test, applied to nine major products across three regulatory tiers. The manuscript adds collateral interoperability analysis (Binance Portfolio Margin, Ceffu custody), independent corroboration from three scholarly sources, and an OCC GENIUS Act implementing rule readout with a submitted comment letter. SVB and follow-on stablecoin contagion anchor the framework; Tier 1 products under banking law and the 1940 Act satisfy every category, while no stablecoin product does.

    Treasury's New Tokenized-Cash Principles Already Map onto MVEP's Operator-Layer Diagnostic

    The Tokenized Cash Management Advisory Group (TCMAG) launched twelve buyer-side principles April 21, 2026. MVEP supplies the supply-side diagnostic. The frameworks are complementary, not competing.

    Mapping twelve TCMAG treasury-practitioner principles to the program's ten-category MVEP framework, with three gap categories surfaced for any future MVEP v2.

    What it is: Comparative briefing covering the twelve TCMAG principles published April 21, 2026, mapped against MVEP's ten supply-side categories. Eight of twelve principles map directly or partially to MVEP; three (Multi-Issuer architecture, Interoperability, Confidentiality) surface genuine gap candidates for any future MVEP v2 cycle. The briefing shows why the Multi-Bank principle (treasurers able to hold and move tokenized cash across many banks rather than being locked into one issuer) depends on neutral cross-bank clearing infrastructure (Ubyx is the canonical example). Without such a clearing layer, the program's analysis projects market consolidation around one or two dominant deposit-token issuers within roughly two years of launch, at which point multi-bank freedom becomes aspirational rather than operational. The briefing also maps the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) standard maintained by GLEIF onto two MVEP requirements it sharpens: establishing what legal claim a tokenholder actually has against a named issuer, and keeping an auditable record of who is authorized to change a tokenized product's rules.

The Tokenization Systems research program has been building the complementary diagnostic layer (MVEP) and the supporting market-structure analysis (Three Tokenization Strategies, Routing the Dollar). This briefing positions all four frames as one narrative: requirements, diagnostic, competitive dynamics, transmission channels.

    Three Tokenization Strategies

    Institutional tokenization of US securities is proceeding through three distinct strategies, each synthesizing infrastructure from different combinations of regulated entities.

    Securitize is transfer agent across all three strategies. A 72-hour operational failure would degrade all three simultaneously, a system-level risk that entity-level scoring does not capture.

    What it is: Infrastructure map of the eight operating entities supplying institutional-tokenization building blocks (DTCC, Kinexys, Nasdaq, NYSE, Securitize, BNY, JPMorgan, Ubyx), dated March 27, 2026. Three strategies analyzed against the MVEP's ten diligence categories: a Regulated Collateral Circuit (DTC-tokenized Treasuries on Canton Network with bank-issued deposit tokens cleared at par through Ubyx), a Multi-Issuer Yield Stack (five tokenized Treasury products with a smart redemption router), and Tokenized Equity Rails (Nasdaq and NYSE). Securitize is the SEC-registered transfer agent in all three. The corporate-actions reconciliation gap (24/7 trading on a business-day register clock) requires an explicit architecture choice. The deposit-token market is control-layer competition applied to institutional settlement; Ubyx's multi-issuer clearing depth determines monopoly versus competitive outcome.

    Open paper summary → Request preprint →
    More on relevance and audience

    Why it matters: System-level risks live in the entities that connect the strategies, not in the strategies themselves. Entity-level regulatory scoring rates Securitize high; system-effective regulatory strength is lower because the same entity operates across all three with no documented fallback. The corporate-actions gap in tokenized-equity rails is the most novel operational risk on the institutional roadmap; the deposit-token market outcome turns on whether multi-issuer clearing achieves depth before any one bank achieves dominant volume.

    Who it's for: Central banks, policy designers, securities regulators, exchange operators, transfer agents, prime brokers, clearinghouses, asset managers, tokenization issuers, and risk committees.

    Evidence type: Simulation + systems engineering

    Adaptive Tokenomics

    Adaptive emissions are conditional insurance with a defined boundary.

    Token design as systems engineering.

    What it is: A complete token economy (MeshNet) built as a systems-engineering design laboratory and stress-tested across a 240-run Monte Carlo simulation plus additional sensitivity checks covering prolonged bear markets, competitor entry, regulatory shocks, and combined stress. Input parameters are drawn from real hardware costs across twelve DePIN protocols. Finds that adaptive emissions function as downside insurance (compressing worst-case outcomes by roughly 6x), slashing dominates token burns during the early growth phase, and the most important results depend on market conditions and parameter settings. Running the model once hides rare but severe downside scenarios that only appear when you run it hundreds of times with different starting conditions.

    Open paper summary →
    More on relevance and audience

    Why it matters: Shows which token mechanisms earn their complexity and where they break. PID emission control helps under demand contraction but can overcorrect in tail scenarios, applying a supply-side lever to a demand-side problem.

    Who it's for: Protocol designers, DePIN teams, mechanism designers, and governance researchers.

    Mar 2026 · SSRN · Protocol Design / DePIN

    Evidence type: 624-run simulation + operational-risk standards

    Operational Risk in Token Economies

    Infrastructure failure can stabilize controllers and break governance at the same time.

    What happens when infrastructure fails while operators stay.

    What it is: A 624-configuration simulation study using real outage and failure rates from Ethereum, Helium, GEODNET, and Filecoin. Finds that adaptive emission controllers become more resilient under operational risk (the counterintuitive result: infrastructure shocks strengthen the controller's advantage over static policy). Reputation-weighted governance moves in the opposite direction, and the outcome depends entirely on one design parameter: the uptime threshold operators must clear to recover their standing. When the designer sets that bar at 99%, real-world outage rates make recovery nearly impossible, and reputation scores collapse to an average of 0.18 on a 5.0 scale by year five. When the designer lowers the bar to 95%, the system stabilizes.

    Open paper summary →
    More on relevance and audience

    Why it matters: The collapse traces to a single design parameter: the recovery threshold was set above what operators can reliably achieve. The fix is a single parameter change (0.99 to 0.95). Risk is assigned at the infrastructure-group level; whether smaller operators face systematically higher risk is explicitly future work.

    Who it's for: Protocol designers, infrastructure operators, operator-risk teams, and governance researchers.

    Evidence type: 40-protocol cross-section + 5-lens institutional-design framework

    Governance Concentration Beyond Token Allocation

    Initial allocation does not predict governance concentration; sector membership does.

    A 40-protocol cross-section: allocation null, sector significant, delegation amplifies.

    What it is: Cross-sectional analysis of 40 protocols (15 DePIN, 15 DeFi, 8 L1/L2 infrastructure, 2 social or stagnated). Lead correlation between initial allocation design and observed governance concentration is null (Pearson r = 0.19, p = 0.25, N = 37). Sector membership predicts concentration (Mann-Whitney p = 0.031, Cohen's d = 0.96 between DePIN and DeFi); insider retention is the mechanism (Spearman rho = 0.54); subsidy correlation is entirely Livepeer-driven; delegation amplifies token concentration into voting concentration by 3 to 6 times in DePIN. Five-lens institutional-design framework (Kantian publicity, Rawlsian legitimacy, Pettit non-domination, Ostromian polycentricity, Hayekian knowledge-use) and a Blockscout-verified exclusion of 69 protocol-controlled addresses across 20 protocols.

Same thesis, different laboratories

Track A shows that the same dollar token changes when routed through different gateways, reserve chains, and control layers.

Track B shows that the same network changes when run under different reward schedules, governance gates, and infrastructure conditions.

Cross-track comparison

Dimension Track A (stablecoins) Track B (token networks)
Where control concentrates Three gateways account for 84% of identified network volume. A single market maker (Wintermute) grew from 1.4% to 19.9% of counterparty volume over 35 months; Cumberland fell from 3.3% to 0.1%. Counterparties connecting multiple gateways fell from 5.8% to 2.6% while total volume doubled. Entity-level HHI averages 2,742, straddling the DOJ highly-concentrated threshold. Control concentrates at the intermediary layer, not the issuance layer. Governance concentration varies by an order of magnitude without a predictive design lever. Holding inequality is universal (Gini 0.72 to 0.98) across 40 protocol-token systems. DeFi concentration spans HHI 0.003 (BAL) to 0.174 (CRV raw); DePIN spans HHI 0.013 (Morpheus AI) to 0.199 (Livepeer). Allocation design (r = +0.08, p = 0.64), revenue, subsidy intensity, and token maturity all fail to predict where a protocol lands. Insider-heavy protocols show concentrated non-insider governance (rho = 0.54, p = 0.001); concentration is a secondary-market outcome, not a design outcome. Control concentrates in governance voting power through dynamics that token design does not reliably predict.
What happens under stress Entity-level variation dominates tier-level variation during stress. During SVB, Circle (1.39x retention) and Coinbase (2.63x) diverged sharply despite near-identical CLII scores; banking exposure, not regulatory tier, drove the gap. Curve 3pool, a permissionless Tier 3 protocol, absorbed 12.35x displaced volume. During the BUSD wind-down, Wintermute's role in the BUSD-to-USDT migration expanded materially while Paxos as a Binance counterparty declined 88%. CLII captures compliance posture, not resilience. Adaptive emission control raises the worst-case floor without improving averages. A controller calibrated to Helium parameters (240-run simulation) reduces network-size outcome spread sixfold compared to fixed emission, raising the worst case from ~1,000 to ~9,000 nodes. Compression is strongest under bear markets (CV 0.219 vs 0.958) but inverts under regulatory shock, where fixed emission produces marginally lower price variance. Adaptive control does not dominate uniformly; the value is identifying where each mechanism applies.
Where the economics sit Stablecoin supply cointegrates with Fed balance-sheet variables during tightening regimes only. Trace statistic rejects at 48.18 (cv95 = 29.80) during tightening; fails at 17.71 during easing. After the September 2024 rate cut, supply nearly doubled from $162B to $305B. The relationship is dollar-specific: Bitcoin and Ethereum produce a null. The yield prohibition in recent U.S. stablecoin legislation reinforces the issuer-side transmission channel. Economics sit on the operator balance sheet, transmitted through reserve yield. Subsidy-to-revenue ratios vary 266x across DePIN protocols; S2R captures demand-side fundamentals independently of token price. Helium's S2R rose from 0.013 to 1.84 over 34 months (fee growth 58%, price decline 25%, emission halving 17%). Revenue grew 8x while HNT declined 77%. Across DePIN, DIMO earns 3x more in revenue than rewards paid (0.33x); Livepeer pays 88.5x more in rewards than earned. Helium's crossover is fragile: ~5 purchasers account for 90% of burns (burn-signer HHI = 0.22). Economics sit in the burn mechanism and service delivery layer.

Two research tracks, one conclusion: the token is the surface; control, failure, and revenue resolve at the operator layer.
