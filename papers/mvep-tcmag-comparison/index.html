Tokenized Cash Management Principles and the Operator-Layer Diagnostic

Briefing · April 2026 · Tokenized Cash · Treasury Operations · Operator Diligence

Briefing version 1, current as of April 2026.

On April 21, 2026, the Tokenized Cash Management Advisory Group (TCMAG) published twelve principles articulating what corporate treasurers require from tokenized cash solutions. The principles operate at the requirement-articulation level: they define what corporates expect, not how to evaluate whether a specific operator delivers. This briefing maps TCMAG's twelve buyer-side requirements against the Tokenization Systems research program's Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack (MVEP), the ten-category supply-side diagnostic framework, and identifies three TCMAG-unique principles as gap candidates for any future MVEP v2 cycle. The frameworks are complementary, not competing: treasurers who adopt TCMAG as their requirements framework need MVEP-style scoring to evaluate candidate operators; operators who score well on MVEP can demonstrate compliance against TCMAG's principles with measurable evidence rather than self-declaration.

1

Eight of twelve TCMAG principles map directly or partially to MVEP categories. Compliance, accounting standards, security, functional equivalence, 24/7 operations, controls, settlement finality, and operational resilience all have direct or partial coverage in MVEP's ten-category framework. The two frameworks are operating on the same problem from different sides.

2

Three TCMAG principles surface genuine gap candidates for MVEP v2. Multi-Bank / Multi-Issuer architecture, Interoperability, and Confidentiality have no clean MVEP equivalents and represent operator-structural dimensions a future MVEP revision could add. The fourth TCMAG-unique principle (TMS / ERP integration) is buyer-side and appropriately outside an operator-structural diagnostic.

3

Deposit-token competition explains why Multi-Bank / Multi-Issuer is load-bearing. The entity issuing the dominant deposit token captures settlement-layer rent (Three Tokenization Strategies, SSRN 6483198). Without cross-institution clearing infrastructure (Ubyx-style, where Ubyx is a TCMAG sponsor), the deposit-token market consolidates around one or two issuers within twenty-four months of launch. TCMAG's Multi-Bank principle is operationally enforceable only when supported by a clearing layer with the right structural properties.

4

GLEIF and the LEI standard provide the natural identifier layer. MVEP Categories 1 (Rights Parity) and 8 (Governance) anchor on operator entity identification. The Legal Entity Identifier per ISO 17442 is the canonical scheme for legal entities globally. GLEIF being a TCMAG sponsor affirms market acceptance; explicit LEI alignment in any future MVEP v2 strengthens the framework's interoperability with global financial-system standards without paradigmatic change.

What this briefing changes

TCMAG launched as a corporate-treasury-side voice in the tokenized cash conversation, articulating eleven principles that define what treasurers expect from tokenized solutions. The Tokenization Systems research program has been building the complementary supply-side layer: MVEP, a ten-category diagnostic framework that scores whether a specific operator clears the threshold institutional users require. This briefing maps the two frameworks and frames their relationship as complementary rather than competing. Treasurers, operators, and regulators are working different sides of the same problem; the frameworks are designed to interoperate.

Why "Multi-Bank / Multi-Issuer" is load-bearing

TCMAG's second principle explicitly rejects single-provider dependency. The Tokenization Systems research program has an empirical anchor for why this matters. The deposit-token competition finding from Three Tokenization Strategies (SSRN 6483198) shows that the entity issuing the dominant deposit token captures settlement-layer rent. If JPMorgan's JPMD becomes the dominant deposit token, JPMorgan owns the settlement layer for tokenized corporate cash; competing banks become rent-payers rather than peers. The market structure outcome is determined by clearing infrastructure: with no cross-institution clearing layer, the deposit-token market consolidates around one or two issuers within twenty-four months of launch; with cross-institution clearing infrastructure such as Ubyx's pre-funding clearing model, the deposit-token market preserves a competitive equilibrium.

This is not a theoretical concern. Ubyx is a TCMAG sponsor, and Ubyx's CEO Tony McLaughlin (former Citi executive, Regulated Liability Network designer) has engaged the deposit-token clearing thesis directly. The clearing layer is the structural mechanism that makes TCMAG's Multi-Bank principle operational rather than aspirational. Without it, the principle is a corporate preference that the market cannot enforce.

Implication for MVEP: a future MVEP v2 should add a Multi-Issuer Compatibility category that scores whether an operator's technical and governance architecture supports corporates holding balances across multiple issuers without operational penalty. This becomes the supply-side measurement that gives the buyer-side requirement teeth.

Structural alignment with the LEI standard

The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF) is a TCMAG sponsor. The Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) per ISO 17442 is the canonical legal-entity identifier in global financial systems. MVEP Categories 1 (Rights Parity) and 8 (Governance) anchor on operator entity identification: who holds the obligation, who can change parameters, who is liable in stress.

MVEP currently treats entity identification as implicit. The framework refers to "the issuer," "the custodian," and "the intermediary" without specifying which canonical identifier scheme bounds those entities. In practice, the LEI standard already provides the answer. A future MVEP v2 would benefit from explicit alignment: "operator entity identification follows the LEI standard per ISO 17442; category scoring anchors on LEI-canonical entity boundaries." This is a one-paragraph addition that strengthens MVEP's interoperability with global financial-system standards without paradigmatic change.

The operator-layer thesis underneath both frameworks

Both TCMAG's principles and MVEP's categories operationalize a deeper claim: that the appropriate locus of regulatory and diligence attention is the operator and router layer, not the token itself. TCMAG's twelve principles do not score the token; they score whether the operator behind the token can demonstrate compliance, security, interoperability, controls, finality, and resilience. The token is the carrier; the operator is the unit of accountability.

This is the same orientation Routing the Dollar (SSRN 6267698) develops at the regulatory-policy level. When an institutional stablecoin failure cascaded through Dai's Peg Stability Module to USDP and GUSD during the SVB weekend in March 2023, the operator-layer transmission channels (PSM mechanics, reserve-bank concentration, redemption-pathway design) determined the contagion path. TCMAG's principles, MVEP's categories, and the program's broader operator-layer regulatory thesis converge on the same locus.

MVEP v2 expansion candidates

Three TCMAG principles without clean MVEP equivalents are worth treating as candidate categories for a future MVEP v2 cycle:

  • Multi-Issuer Compatibility: does the operator's technical and governance architecture support corporates holding balances across multiple issuers without operational penalty? Scores operators on walled-garden versus open-architecture dimensions.
  • Interoperability Surface: does the operator's infrastructure produce vendor lock-in or open-standard compatibility? Distinct from multi-issuer compatibility (which concerns single-corporate multi-counterparty relationships); interoperability concerns cross-chain and cross-protocol message flow.
  • Privacy Architecture: does the operator support balance and transaction privacy while preserving audit-trail access for regulators and counterparty verification? Maps to different primitives across operators (off-chain orchestration at Partior; zero-knowledge approaches at Aztec-style infrastructure; permissioned ledgers at consortium chains).

These are flagged as analytical fuel for any future MVEP revision cycle, not immediate cause to revise the published MVEP framework. The current MVEP (v9, SSRN 6363138) is live and defensible against TCMAG's principles for the seven categories that map directly or partially. The four-category gap is the analytical opportunity, not a flaw in the current framework.

The TMS / ERP Integration principle is appropriately TCMAG-side: it concerns buyer-side integration with corporate treasury management systems and enterprise resource planning platforms, not operator-structural properties.

Engagement context

The TCMAG sponsor list overlaps with several entities the Tokenization Systems research program has analyzed or engaged. Ubyx (Tony McLaughlin) is in active dialogue regarding the deposit-token clearing thesis. HSBC, Barclays, and Lloyds Bank are tokenized deposit issuers whose architectures fit naturally within MVEP scoring. Partior is a candidate Tier 1 case study for CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index) scoring. GLEIF anchors the identifier layer underlying MVEP Categories 1 and 8. SAP carries the ERP integration relevance that TCMAG's fourth principle requires.

Ecosystem participants engaging with TCMAG can use the MVEP framework as the operator-side measurement layer that turns TCMAG's qualitative principles into measurable diligence outcomes. Issuers of tokenized deposits, stablecoins, and tokenized money market funds can self-assess against the ten MVEP categories alongside TCMAG's twelve principles to identify structural gaps and engagement opportunities.

Limitations

  • This is a comparative briefing, not a formal academic paper. The MVEP framework is the canonical academic publication (SSRN 6363138); this briefing applies it against TCMAG's principles as a positioning analysis.
  • TCMAG's principles are the published initial set as of April 21, 2026. TCMAG has indicated the principles will be refined through ongoing engagement; this briefing reflects the initial publication.
  • The four-category MVEP v2 expansion candidates are analytical proposals, not committed framework revisions. Any MVEP revision cycle will evaluate them on their own merits alongside other candidates.
  • The LEI alignment note is a structural observation; it does not modify the canonical MVEP v9 framework or its category definitions.
