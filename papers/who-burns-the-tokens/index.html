Who Burns the Tokens? Fiscal Sustainability and Demand Concentration in GeoDePIN Networks

~25 min read · SSRN 6483619 · DOI 10.2139/ssrn.6483619 · Targeting Blockchain: Research and Applications (Elsevier)

Description current as of June 9, 2026. Manuscript and SSRN abstract are public; journal submission in progress at Blockchain: Research and Applications (Elsevier).

Payment architecture determines whether a GeoDePIN network's demand concentration can be observed on-chain at all. Demand concentration is measurable only for direct-payment protocols, where customers pay the protocol directly on-chain by burning tokens or paying fees: among the networks studied, two qualify, Helium (Data Credit burns, burn-signer HHI 0.27) and Livepeer (transcoding fees, fee-payer HHI 0.31), and in both the demand side is highly concentrated. GEODNET runs a Foundation buy-and-burn, so its on-chain burn signer is the treasury, not the customer; its demand base is off-chain and not measurable from burn data. The applicability boundary is itself the finding. The paper measures fiscal sustainability through the Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio (S2R) and shows that trajectory matters more than level: Helium's monthly S2R rose from 0.013 (May 2023) to a peak of 1.84 (February 2026), crossing monthly fiscal parity in October 2025, driven by the August 2025 emission halving and the full carrier-revenue burn policy; on a trailing-twelve-month basis the network remained net inflationary (S2R approximately 0.51).

At a glance

Question
In GeoDePIN networks, when can outsiders even observe who pays for the network on-chain, and what does the path to fiscal sustainability look like where they can?

Answer
Payment architecture determines whether demand concentration is observable on-chain. Helium's direct-burn model exposes it (burn-signer HHI 0.27, Top-5 roughly 90% of burns) and Livepeer's direct-fee model does the same (fee-payer HHI 0.31); both are highly concentrated. GEODNET's Foundation buy-and-burn relocates demand off-chain, where it is not measurable. Helium's monthly S2R crossed fiscal parity in October 2025, driven by the August 2025 emission halving and the full carrier-revenue burn policy. These are descriptive, within-sector findings, not causal estimates.

Evidence base
The analysis rests on Helium's 34-month monthly S2R series (May 2023 to February 2026, rising from 0.013 to a peak of 1.84 in the final month) alongside demand-concentration measurements for the two direct-payment protocols (Helium, Livepeer) and governance-concentration measurements across the GeoDePIN cross-section, with post-exclusion corrections drawn from the companion cross-sectional study.

Five findings

1

Fiscal sustainability is dynamic, not static

The Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio (S2R) measures demand-side burns against supply-side issuance (tokens burned divided by tokens issued): values above 1.0 indicate net deflationary pressure, below 1.0 indicate subsidy dependence. The S2R trajectory beats S2R level for assessing fiscal sustainability. A network can be subsidy-dependent for years and still be on a defensible trajectory; a network at temporary parity can be on a deteriorating one. (Important methodology disclosure: S2R measures burns versus mining emissions only and excludes vesting dilution; see Methods below.)

What this means: Judging a network's fiscal health requires watching where its subsidy ratio is heading over time, not snapshotting a single S2R reading.

2

Payment architecture determines whether demand concentration is observable

Demand concentration is measurable only where customers pay the protocol directly on-chain. Helium's Data Credit purchasers sign their own burns: Top-5 roughly 90% of burn volume, burn-signer HHI 0.27. Livepeer's broadcasters pay transcoding fees directly in ETH: the top two broadcasters fund 75% of fees, fee-payer HHI 0.31. GEODNET runs a Foundation buy-and-burn: the burn signer is the treasury, not the customer, so its demand concentration is not measurable on-chain.

What this means: Whether outsiders can see who funds a network's token demand is itself a design property: direct-payment architectures expose the customer base on-chain, while buy-and-burn, single-relayer, and credit-intermediated architectures relocate it off-chain.

3

GEODNET governance concentration is measurable on-chain even though its demand base is not

While GEODNET's demand base sits off-chain, its Polygon-side token distribution is observable and concentrated: holdings HHI 0.14, with the team wallet alone holding 25.3% of non-burned supply. Both figures are required: HHI alone undersells the team-wallet concentration. The paper cautions that this distribution-level reading overstates true governance concentration where a bridge contract aggregates many individual holders into a single address.

What this means: Governance concentration and demand concentration are independent dimensions that have to be measured separately, and for buy-and-burn architectures the token-distribution side is the one on-chain data can actually reach.

4

SuperHex is a novel design primitive with no analog in digital-only token economies

GEODNET's spatial staking mechanism: token holders stake GEOD on specific hexagonal geographic cells (H3 cells) before stations are deployed, tying token incentives to coverage geography. Spatial staking has no clean analog in DeFi or DePIN networks where the unit of work is purely digital. The paper treats SuperHex as worth instrumenting as a separate design dimension for GeoDePIN networks.

What this means: Networks whose work is tied to physical geography need their own tokenomics dimension, since incentive tools borrowed from digital-only economies do not capture spatial staking.

5

DePIN tokenomics is a design space, not a single architecture

The 13-protocol sample separates into a three-tier sustainability hierarchy: Helium posted the first net-deflationary monthly readings (peak S2R 1.84) and DIMO a single elevated month (4.24); GEODNET (0.219) and Hivemapper (0.183) occupy a growth-phase middle tier with confirmed burn activity; the remaining protocols cluster near S2R zero. Payment architectures split further into direct-payment, buy-and-burn, single-relayer, and credit-intermediated designs with different on-chain visibility. The result counters monolithic-sector framings of "DePIN tokenomics" as a single thing. The relevant level of analysis is design choice, not sector membership.

What this means: Evaluating a DePIN token economy means looking at its specific mechanism design rather than treating every network in the sector as interchangeable.

What this paper changes

Most discussion of DePIN tokenomics evaluates whether a network is fiscally sustainable by asking whether token revenue covers token emissions today. This paper reframes the question: it is not the level that matters but the trajectory, and trajectory is determined more by burn-mechanism design than by the absolute size of current revenue. The paper is a study of 13 DePIN protocols (10 GeoDePIN plus 3 DeVIN comparison cases) anchored by a 34-month Helium longitudinal case, and its distinctive empirical contribution is a measurability boundary: payment architecture determines whether a network's demand concentration can be observed on-chain at all.

The paper inherits the protocol-controlled-address exclusion methodology developed in Auditing Governance Concentration Beyond Token Allocation: its own GeoDePIN governance readings are reported pre-exclusion (HHI 0.034 to 0.593), with post-exclusion corrections drawn from that companion cross-section where available (most prominently for DIMO). It shares that paper's grounding in Ostromian self-governance and republican non-domination, though the full normative apparatus is developed in companion theory papers rather than in either empirical study.

Core findings

1. Helium's monthly S2R rose from 0.013 to a peak of 1.84 across the 34-month window; monthly parity crossed in October 2025

The Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio measures demand-side burns against supply-side issuance: monthly S2R = tokens burned divided by tokens issued. Across the 34-month window (May 2023 to February 2026), Helium's monthly S2R rose from 0.013 to a peak of 1.84 in the final month, posting net-deflationary monthly readings in four of the five months from October 2025 (November 2025 the lone exception).

S2R operationalizes the burn-and-mint equilibrium: readings above 1.0 mean net token supply contraction. Three fiscal regimes emerge over the study window:

  • Post-migration baseline: S2R 0.01 to 0.04 (May 2023 to early 2024); burns minimal, issuance dominant
  • Demand ramp: S2R 0.15 to 0.67 (mid-2025), as Mobile subscriber growth and IoT data transfer increased burn volume; full carrier-revenue burns lifted the ratio to 0.38 by June 2025, before the halving
  • Fiscal parity and beyond: monthly S2R 0.88 to 1.84 (late 2025 to February 2026), with burns exceeding issuance in four of five months

Because monthly readings are volatile, the paper also sums burns and issuance over trailing windows: the trailing-three-month ratio crossed 1.0 in December 2025 and reached 1.50 by February 2026; the trailing-six-month ratio crossed in January 2026 (1.24 by February); the trailing-twelve-month ratio remained below parity throughout, at approximately 0.51 as of February 2026.

2. Two drivers explain the parity crossover (not separable on-chain without a formal event study)

Two drivers moved the system across fiscal parity: the August 2025 emission halving (issuance dropped roughly 58%) and the full carrier-revenue burn policy. The on-chain data cannot separate their contributions without a formal event study, but the pre-halving jump in the Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio to 0.38 in June 2025 (carrier burn only, no halving) shows that demand-side burns alone moved S2R substantially.

Demand-coupled fee growth is large in absolute terms: by December 2025, annualized Data Credit burn reached an approximately $20.9M run-rate (trailing three-month annualized, October to December 2025; industry-reported and consistent with on-chain data). The same industry data report a roughly eightfold year-over-year revenue increase even as the HNT price declined 77%. The related governance reform, HIP-147 (approved Q3 2025), shifted reward distribution from coverage-based proof-of-coverage to service-based metrics weighted by actual data transfer and subscriber usage.

3. Payment architecture determines whether demand concentration can be observed on-chain

Helium's customers pay the protocol directly: Data Credit purchasers sign their own HNT burns, so the demand side is visible on-chain. Result: Top-5 demand sources account for ~90% of burns; burn-signer HHI 0.27, with the top two addresses at 70.1%. Livepeer's broadcasters pay transcoding fees directly in ETH and are visible the same way: the top two broadcasters fund 75% of fees; fee-payer HHI 0.31 across 28 paying broadcasters.

GEODNET collects subscription revenue off-chain and runs a Foundation buy-and-burn: the Foundation buys and burns GEOD, so the burn signer is the treasury, not the customer, and the demand base is not measurable from burn data. Hivemapper routes roughly 96% of burns through a single credit-purchase relayer. DIMO's burns come from roughly one hundred distinct purchaser wallets, but each burn is a prepaid-credit purchase one step removed from end-customer consumption, so the burn-wallet distribution does not reliably map to end-customer concentration.

Demand concentration is measurable precisely for the direct-payment protocols, and wherever it is measurable it is high. The framing is: payment architecture determines whether a network's customer base is visible on-chain at all; the applicability boundary is itself the finding. This is a measurability result resting on two observed protocols, not a claim that all GeoDePIN demand is concentrated.

4. Governance concentration is measurable where demand concentration is not

GEODNET's Polygon-side token distribution concentrates: holdings HHI 0.14, with the team wallet alone holding 25.3% of non-burned supply. Both figures are needed to read the result correctly: HHI alone undersells the team-wallet concentration; team-wallet share alone misses the rest of the distribution. The paper cautions that this distribution-level reading overstates true governance concentration where a bridge contract aggregates many individual holders into a single address.

Beyond GEODNET, DIMO governance HHI is 0.025 post-exclusion: excluding the DIMO Foundation address reduces the measured HHI from 0.305, roughly an order of magnitude, per the inherited exclusion methodology.

5. SuperHex as a novel design primitive

GEODNET's SuperHex mechanism: token holders stake GEOD to designate high-value geographic hexagons (H3 cells) before stations are deployed, creating a market signal for where coverage is most valuable so capital flows to frontier regions before hardware does. There is no clean analog in digital-only token economies: in DeFi, the unit of work is software state changes; in non-spatial DePIN, work is bandwidth, storage, or compute. SuperHex bundles the work unit (high-precision GPS reference signal) with a geographic location proof and a stake commitment. The paper treats SuperHex as a separate design dimension worth instrumenting in other GeoDePIN networks; it is absent from all other protocols in the 13-protocol sample.

Methods and data

Definitions (first-use)

DePIN: Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Network. GeoDePIN: the DePIN subset whose value depends on where hardware is deployed: geographic distribution across many unique locations is essential to the service. The paper studies GeoDePIN specifically, with three DeVIN (virtual-infrastructure) comparison cases.

HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index): the standard market-concentration measure; the sum of squared participant shares, ranging from near 0 (dispersed) to 1.0 (a single entity).

Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio (S2R): demand-side burns divided by supply-side issuance within a time window (monthly S2R = tokens burned / tokens issued). Values above 1.0 indicate net deflationary pressure (burns exceed issuance); below 1.0 indicate subsidy dependence.

Burn-and-mint equilibrium: the condition where token burns offset token issuance (parity at S2R = 1.0). The paper's S2R metric operationalizes it; because monthly readings are volatile, trailing three-, six-, and twelve-month window ratios are reported alongside.

HIP-147: Helium Improvement Proposal approved Q3 2025 that shifted reward distribution from coverage-based proof-of-coverage to service-based metrics weighted by actual data transfer and subscriber usage. Distinct from the August 2025 emission halving, which cut issuance roughly 58%.

SuperHex: GEODNET's spatial staking mechanism. Token holders stake GEOD on specific hexagonal cells (H3 cells) before stations are deployed, tying token incentives to coverage geography.

Fiscal sustainability: protocol revenue sufficient to sustain token emissions without external capital injection. The paper-specific term is narrower than fiscal sustainability in public finance, where the analogous concept involves sovereign budget and debt dynamics.

Demand concentration: the distribution of protocol revenue sources, measuring who pays for the network's services. Distinct from governance concentration (who controls the protocol) and supply concentration (who holds the tokens). The paper's distinctive empirical contribution is the demand-concentration measurability boundary: the measure is valid only for direct-payment protocols, where customers pay the protocol directly on-chain.

Service verification: the protocol-specific mechanism for confirming that a node delivered the physical service it claims (location proof, coverage proof, data integrity proof). Critical for GeoDePIN because services are physical and not directly observable on-chain without an oracle bridge.

HNT versus Data Credits (Helium architecture)

HNT (the Helium Network Token) is the speculative and tradeable token. Data Credits are non-speculative, USD-pegged utility credits that customers burn to pay for network services. Data Credits are produced by burning HNT at the oracle-reported HNT/USD price. This separation is critical for burn-and-mint intuition: the burn side is denominated in Data Credit demand (real network usage); the mint side is denominated in HNT emissions (operator subsidies).

Methodology blind spot (non-negotiable disclosure)

S2R measures burns versus mining emissions only, excluding vesting dilution. This is a first-order caveat: vesting unlocks from team, investor, and ecosystem allocations add to circulating supply without passing through emission accounting, so S2R can read healthy while vesting silently expands the float. The disclosure is surfaced at first mention of S2R in the main text, not only in the limitations section.

Exclusion methodology (inherited from Governance Concentration)

Protocol-controlled addresses (staking contracts, bridges, custodians, vesting locks, team multisigs, treasuries) are excluded before concentration calculations. The methodology is developed in Auditing Governance Concentration Beyond Token Allocation. Applied to DIMO, exclusion of the DIMO Foundation address reduces governance HHI from 0.305 (pre-exclusion) to 0.025 (post-exclusion, per the companion cross-section); skipping the exclusion overstates DIMO's governance concentration by roughly an order of magnitude.

Philosophy disposition

This is not a philosophy paper. It uses Ostromian self-governance as the framing for governance distribution and draws its measurement methodology from Auditing Governance Concentration Beyond Token Allocation (reference [34]); the broader normative apparatus is developed in companion theory papers, not in either empirical study. Scope is narrowed to the empirical Helium/GEODNET focus.

Limitations

  • S2R excludes vesting dilution; vesting unlocks can expand circulating supply without changing emissions or revenue. Surfaced at first S2R mention; treated as a first-order caveat.
  • Helium's monthly S2R peak (1.84, February 2026) reflects a temporary post-HIP-147 Mobile burn allocation and WiFi-offload pricing effects, and overstates steady-state equilibrium by an estimated 2-5x; on a trailing-twelve-month basis the network remained net inflationary (approximately 0.51).
  • The direct-payment measurement rests on two protocols (Helium and Livepeer), so the finding that demand concentration is high wherever observable is conditional on a small observed set; it is a measurability result about on-chain visibility, not a population claim across all DePIN networks.
  • Filecoin S2R is included for cross-section reference only. The paper de-emphasizes Filecoin in favor of Helium and GEODNET, where on-chain data is authoritative.
  • SuperHex is a novel design primitive; the paper documents it but does not evaluate it against alternative spatial-staking designs (because none currently exist at comparable scale).
  • Demand-concentration measurements are a snapshot of the study window and may shift as the demand mix evolves; the dominant burn signer itself changed during the window (the February 2026 leader was not active before November 2025).
