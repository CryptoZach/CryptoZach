The Control Layer of Agentic Payments

Research memo · Facilitator Concentration · Subsidy Pipelines · Settlement Accountability · x402

Research memo, version 1.1 (July 3, 2026 citation-layer corrections; original version 1 published June 11, 2026). All on-chain figures measured June 11, 2026 (UTC) and unchanged in version 1.1; the x402 ecosystem moves weekly. Full title: "The Control Layer of Agentic Payments: Facilitator Concentration, Subsidy Pipelines, and Settlement Accountability in x402."

Corrections (v1.1, July 3, 2026): Six citation-layer corrections surfaced by the companion working paper's references-verification pass. The merger-guidelines threshold now cites both editions (1,800 under the current 2023 Merger Guidelines; 2,500 under the superseded 2010 edition); the ERC-7760 seller is labeled a minimal-upgradeable-proxy contract, not a vault; the Coinbase CDP Polygon launch date reads March 11, 2026; the published wash estimates are attributed to Artemis (strict filter) and Allium (on-chain), both as published by a16z crypto; the eight-day Polygon-over-Base run is dated to January 2026 (Artemis dashboard data via crypto press); and the Verda formation figures are re-pinned to the store-reproducible reading. No measured finding, HHI, dollar figure, or cardinality count changed.

Agentic payments have a measurement literature and a leaderboard culture, but no one measures the control layer: the small set of facilitator operators through whom x402 settlement actually routes, and the accountability structure of the composition chain those operators sit in. This memo reports the first such measurement, across the three chains that carry materially all x402 activity (Base, Polygon, Solana), using the same gateway-concentration method the research program applied to stablecoin routing in Routing the Dollar. The settlement layer measures more concentrated than the stablecoin gateway layer it sits on; the count layer and the value layer have different monopolists on two chains independently; the largest count and value contributors are subsidized and proxy pipelines that buyer cardinality exposes without any wash-trading heuristic; and the composition chain that delivers an agentic payment has no layer at which accountability currently composes.

At a glance

Question
Who controls agentic payments: how concentrated is the facilitator layer that settles them, how much of the measured activity is organic, and where does accountability sit in the chain that delivers an agent-initiated payment?

Answer
Settlement routes majority-through one operator on every chain measured, and the operator differs by layer. May 2026 facilitator concentration measures at HHI 6,009 by payment count and 4,040 by corrected dollar volume on Base, 4,203 and 4,915 on Solana, and effective single-operator service on Polygon: every reading above both merger-guidelines thresholds for a highly concentrated market (1,800 current, 2,500 superseded). The dominant count pipeline is a chain operator's official facilitator under a public gas-rebate program (5.04 million May payments from 35 buyer wallets); the dominant value pipeline is a proxy facilitator with 17 active buyers and a directly observable 0.99 percent fee. Measured at the value layer, the market is tiny (940,323 US dollars cross-chain in May, 0.0000376 percent of USDC transfer value on the same rails). Settlement is irreversible at the protocol layer, the facilitator function carries no licensing or registration requirements, and the top sell-side endpoints are pseudonymous even to full-data analysis.

Evidence base
Registry-merged facilitator attribution (138 addresses across 29 facilitators and three networks), settlement-fingerprint queries against USDC transfer and authorization events on Base, Polygon, and Solana from May 2025 through June 2026, payment-level validation of the dominant value-layer facilitator, and reconciliation against the published figures of four external measurement providers. Every number reproduces from committed SQL and recompute scripts; the dataset and queries are available on request.

Six findings

1

The settlement layer is more concentrated than the stablecoin gateway layer it sits on

May 2026 facilitator concentration: Base HHI 6,009 by count and 4,040 by corrected dollar volume; Solana 4,203 and 4,915; Polygon effectively single-operator. Every reading clears both merger-guidelines thresholds for a highly concentrated market (1,800 current; 2,500 superseded) and exceeds the Tier 1 stablecoin gateway duopoly (56/44) measured in Routing the Dollar, at a market age of under 14 months.

What this means: The natural supervisory question about agentic payments is not the token or the agent; it is the small set of operators every payment routes through.

2

The count layer and the value layer have different monopolists, on two chains independently

On Base, Coinbase's CDP facilitator carries 75.6 percent of May payment counts while Meridian, a proxy-settlement facilitator, carries 57.2 percent of corrected May dollars. On Solana the flip replicates with different operators: Dexter carries 51.8 percent of counts while Coinbase carries 66.5 percent of dollars.

What this means: Any single-metric concentration claim about this market is structurally fragile; the control-layer reading requires both metrics.

3

Transaction counts under subsidy are not adoption, and buyer cardinality exposes the difference

The official Polygon Facilitator, a 24-address fleet operating under a public 1 million US dollar gas-rebate program, produced 5.04 million May payments (56.8 percent of cross-chain counts) from 35 buyer wallets that recirculate USDC seeded by two treasury wallets. Payments per distinct buyer: 26.7 on Base versus roughly 144,000 on Polygon.

What this means: Agents make volume arbitrarily cheap to manufacture, so unweighted count metrics select for whoever has the strongest incentive to manufacture them. Buyer cardinality reads the difference directly from settlement structure, with no wash-trading heuristic.

4

Transfer-sum measurement double-counts proxy settlement; the corrected value layer is 36.6 percent smaller

Payment-level validation of Meridian's two-hop proxy architecture (buyer to facilitator, facilitator to seller, in the same transaction) corrects Base May volume from 1,030,242 to 652,882 US dollars and the volume HHI from 5,621 to 4,040. The retained spread is directly observable on-chain: 0.99 percent, against roughly 14 basis points on a settlement-router path and a posted 0.001 US dollars per transaction elsewhere.

What this means: Facilitation pricing currently spans nearly two orders of magnitude, and dashboards that sum transfers inherit a proxy-settlement overcount in proportion to proxy-settled share.

5

Agentic payments are message-significant and value-pre-material

Corrected cross-chain May 2026 volume is 940,323 US dollars across 8.87 million payments: 2.83 percent of all USDC transfer events on the three chains, and 0.0000376 percent of USDC transfer value. On Polygon the subsidized fleet alone is 15.9 percent of all USDC transfer events, large enough to distort chain-level activity statistics.

What this means: The activity profile is real and growing at the message layer; the dollar layer remains pre-material. Both calibrations belong in any claim about this market.

6

The composition chain has no accountable layer

Settlement is push-payment and irreversible at the protocol layer (no chargeback primitive exists). The facilitator function carries no licensing, registration, capital, or operational requirements, and on two of three chains the dominant facilitator has a direct economic interest in the measured volume. None of the top 25 May sellers on Base resolves to a named operator from public surfaces.

What this means: Every operator in the chain can be individually defensible while the composed product delivers an irreversible payment, on a non-human instruction, through an unlicensed intermediary, to an unidentifiable counterparty. The binding gap sits at a layer neither AI-governance frameworks nor payments regulation currently sees.

What this memo changes

Existing x402 measurement (x402scan, Chainalysis, Artemis, Allium, the community Dune dashboards) counts transactions, estimates volume, and filters wash activity. That work answers "how much," with answers that disagree by 10x or more depending on filters: published 30-day estimates span 24 million US dollars raw (x402.org), roughly 3 million of on-chain volume (Allium data, as published by a16z crypto), and 1.6 million after wash filtering (Artemis). None of it answers "through whom," and none of it applies an accountability framework to the operators it counts.

The research program's position is that "through whom" is the question that matters for financial-stability and consumer-protection purposes, for the same reason it mattered for stablecoins in Routing the Dollar: the token is not the control point; the routing operator is. In x402, the routing operator is the facilitator, the service that receives a buyer's signed payment authorization off-chain, broadcasts the settlement transaction on-chain, and pays the gas. Facilitators decide what settles, when, on which chain, and at what fee. They are the natural locus of concentration, the natural single point of failure, and the natural supervisory perimeter question, and they were unmeasured.

The category context sharpens the question. Venture-tracking data documents an agentic-payments formation surge: the Verda Ventures Stablescape database tracked more than 600 agentic-payments companies as of May 2026, 96 percent of venture-staged entries at Pre-seed, and the provider's live readings through mid-2026 show the founded-in-2026 cohort running at roughly five times the full-year-2025 count. (The provider re-curates continuously and its live figures shift between reads.) Formation is outpacing every accountability framework in the space. This memo measures the settlement infrastructure those 600-plus entities will route through.

Measurement design

Settlement fingerprint. x402's dominant exact-EVM scheme settles via transferWithAuthorization() on EIP-3009 tokens (USDC): the buyer signs an EIP-712 authorization off-chain; the facilitator broadcasts it and pays gas; the token contract emits a Transfer and an AuthorizationUsed event. On Solana (exact-SVM), settlement is an SPL TransferChecked in a structured transaction with a memo nonce, with the facilitator as fee payer. Attribution rule: transaction sender (or fee payer) in the facilitator set, on the USDC token, with the authorization fingerprint present. AuthorizationUsed alone is not sufficient; other EIP-3009 relayer populations exist and are large.

Census frame. Three merged sources: the x402scan facilitator registry (machine-readable; 138 address entries across 29 facilitators and 3 networks) plus the two curated attribution maps behind the principal community dashboard (96 EVM rows across 12 chains; 24 Solana fee payers), merged and normalized. The registry is community-maintained and treated as best-available, not exhaustive.

Coverage. On Base, the registry set accounts for 92.3 percent of all AuthorizationUsed events in the trailing 30 days; this is a conservative lower bound on x402 coverage because the denominator includes consumer gasless-wallet relayer traffic that is knowably not x402. On Polygon, after a transaction-target discriminator separates direct USDC settlement from app-mediated meta-transactions (26.1 percent of raw events), the merged set covers 99.5 percent of the x402-pattern population. Residual unattributed machine-shaped submitters on Base are estimated at 2 to 3 points of coverage.

The buyer-cardinality classifier. The measurement contributes one methodological tool: events per distinct buyer per month, computed at submitter, facilitator, or chain level, separates organic usage from pipeline activity without any wash heuristic. May 2026 values: Base 26.7; Polygon roughly 144,000; Solana history swings from 11,900 (December 2025, the leaderboard-grind era) to 10.1 (May 2026). The classifier requires no labeling, no funding-graph traversal, and no judgment calls about self-dealing; it reads structure directly from settlement data.

The value layer is a proxy pipeline

Every Meridian May transaction (11,088 of 11,088) carries exactly one AuthorizationUsed event and exactly two USDC transfers: the buyer's authorized payment into Meridian's own submitter address (377,360.50 US dollars total), and an onward transfer from that address to the end seller in the same transaction (373,610.22 US dollars total to 15 external sellers). This matches Meridian's published architecture: its documentation describes a proxy-facilitator flow ("Payer Wallet, Meridian Facilitator, Settlement / Bridge, Recipient") with built-in fee management. No misconduct claim attaches to any of this; the design is public and has legitimate functions (isolated receiver balances, cross-chain execution via bridge, fee accounting).

Three measurement consequences follow. First, transfer-sum methods double-count proxy settlement: Meridian's May volume is 373,610 US dollars, not 750,971; Base May corrects from 1,030,242 to 652,882; the volume HHI corrects from 5,621 to 4,040; and the cumulative Base series corrects by 1.42 million US dollars lifetime. Second, the implied facilitation fee is directly observable: the retained spread between buyer leg and seller leg is 3,750.28 US dollars in May, 0.99 percent of the buyer leg; lifetime, 14,084 US dollars on 1.419 million, the same 0.99 percent. Third, the value layer is small-cardinality: 17 buyers and 15 end sellers, each seller receiving from exactly one counterparty at tight payment sizes (average 33.86 US dollars; median 31.02; maximum 2,000) and roughly 720 to 1,950 payments per seller-month. Whether these flows are external customers, affiliated services, or incentive-driven recirculation is not determinable from on-chain structure alone and is flagged as an open verification item, not asserted.

The headline consequence: 57.2 percent of the corrected May Base value layer, and 39.7 percent of cross-chain dollars, route through one proxy facilitator with 17 active buyers. The market's "value" is currently a pipeline, just as the market's "counts" are; they are different pipelines run by different operators under different economics.

The count layer is a subsidy pipeline

The single largest contributor to cross-chain transaction counts is a 24-address fleet on Polygon, identified by discovery and resolved to a named operator with primary sourcing: Polygon's own documentation describes the official Polygon Facilitator and lists the fleet's 24 wallet addresses in three rotating sets. The fleet's mechanics, closed on-chain:

  • Gas: one treasury supplied 687,453 POL across 352 transfers to the 24 addresses since September 2025. Polygon's public 1 million US dollar gas-rebate program for qualified x402 facilitator transactions (100 percent of gas refunded until pool exhaustion) activated in early March 2026; fleet counts rose from 3.0 million (March) to 5.04 million (May).
  • Buyers: the fleet served 35 to 43 distinct buyer wallets per month across its entire history. The 38 buyers active in the trailing 60 days were seeded by two treasury-shaped wallets (171,444 and 167,770 US dollars) and otherwise recirculate USDC among themselves in micro-transfers. Top buyers each produced approximately 330,000 events in 60 days, six of them within 0.3 percent of each other: scripted workers at uniform workloads.
  • Dollars: fleet lifetime volume is approximately 514,000 US dollars across 20.6 million transfers (0.025 US dollars average); May is 152,956 US dollars across 5.04 million transfers.

The leaderboard compression these counts feed is on the record: in late January 2026, community dashboards and crypto press reported Polygon "out-pacing Base" in daily agentic payments for eight consecutive days (Artemis dashboard data, as carried by crypto press), before the rebate program had activated. The counts are real, correctly attributed, and produced by a chain operator's official facilitator from three dozen self-recirculating wallets, under a public subsidy since March 2026. The calibrated finding: transaction counts under subsidy are not adoption, and buyer cardinality exposes the difference (26.7 events per buyer on Base versus roughly 144,000 on Polygon in May). The episode is a clean case study in why leaderboard metrics fail as adoption measures in agent-driven markets: agents make volume arbitrarily cheap to manufacture, so any unweighted count metric selects for whoever has the strongest incentive to manufacture it.

Settlement properties and fingerprint decay

Push-payment irreversibility. Exact-EVM settlement is a one-way authorized transfer. EIP-3009 provides no chargeback primitive, no holding period, and no protocol-layer dispute mechanism; finality is the chain's finality. Refunds, where they exist, are a voluntary off-protocol action by the seller or facilitator. This stands in descriptive contrast to the error-resolution architecture consumer payments law assumes (Regulation E's investigation and provisional-recredit obligations presuppose an operator with the power to reverse). In the x402 chain, no layer holds that power post-broadcast: not the agent runtime, not the authorization protocol, not the facilitator (whose role ends at broadcast), not the token issuer (USDC freeze authority exists but is not a payment-reversal mechanism), not the chain. Whether Regulation E would even attach to an agent-initiated USDC payment is a live legal question outside this memo's scope; the measured point is that the settlement layer is architecturally finality-only.

Observable facilitation economics. The x402x SettlementRouter on Base (a decoded on-chain settlement path used by 6 facilitators) emitted per-payment facilitatorFee totaling 25.68 US dollars on 18,408 across 13,423 payments in 30 days: approximately 14 basis points. With Meridian's 99-basis-point proxy spread and Coinbase's posted 0.001 US dollars per transaction beyond its free tier, the market's observable pricing spans 0.001 US dollars to roughly 1 percent per payment. At current corrected volumes, total facilitation revenue on Base is on the order of hundreds of dollars per month outside Meridian's spread: the layer is being operated at a loss or as a strategic position, not as a business, which is itself evidence consistent with the concentration readings (operators are buying position in a layer whose value is prospective).

Fingerprint decay (the measurement half-life). The router path is 0.3 percent of Base counts today, and the announced batch-settlement scheme (pre-funded escrow, off-chain vouchers, bulk claims) would remove per-payment on-chain observability entirely where adopted. The Base series tracks community counts closely, which bounds current batch migration as low, but the structural point stands: this market is measurable today at per-payment granularity, and the protocol roadmap reduces that granularity. The window in which public-record measurement of agentic payments is possible at this fidelity may be temporary; that is an argument for standing measurement now, not after the perimeter question becomes acute.

The meta-finding: in a market this young, measurement infrastructure is itself concentrated and unaudited. The dominant community dashboard's attribution map carries a typo that silently drops a chain's rows from per-chain joins, misses the largest value-layer operator, and is nonetheless the de facto public record. Divergent published figures are not competing estimates of one quantity; they are different quantities wearing the same name.

The composition chain and the weakest layer

An agentic payment is a multi-vendor composition: an agent runtime (operated by an AI platform or the buyer's developer) invokes a paid external resource; an authorization or checkout protocol (Google's AP2 mandates, OpenAI and Stripe's ACP, or bare x402 client libraries) structures the payment intent; a facilitator settles it; a token issuer (Circle) provides the settlement asset; a chain finalizes. Five operator classes, none of which sees the whole transaction.

At the facilitator layer the census is complete (that is the concentration result: 29 registered facilitators, of which one per layer-metric pair dominates each chain). At the sell-side endpoint layer, the census result is negative in an informative way: of the top 25 Base sellers by May dollars, after removing Meridian's internal hop, the top organic seller (an unlabeled address receiving 86,968 US dollars across 218,370 payments from 76,291 distinct buyers through 5 facilitator groups) resolves to no named operator from any public surface; eight of the next ranks are Meridian's one-to-one pipeline sellers; the high-count micro-metering sellers (2,700 to 5,900 US dollars on 84,000 to 145,000 payments each) include one verified smart-contract vault and otherwise unlabeled addresses. Accountability mapping fails at the seller layer: the entities receiving the majority of agentic-payment dollars are not identifiable from public records, including by a motivated analyst with full on-chain data.

Accountability degrades to the weakest operator in the composition chain. Three category readings follow. The transaction record: the on-chain settlement record is excellent (it is the basis of this memo) but ends at addresses; the authorization context (what the agent was instructed to buy, under what mandate, with what spend limits) lives with the agent runtime and the authorization protocol, none of it on the public record, and no operator commits to producing it. The record that exists is the one no party chose to keep; the record that matters for disputes is the one no party is obligated to keep. Response finality: absolute at the settlement layer and uncommitted everywhere else; a buyer's recourse chain terminates at whichever upstream operator first declines responsibility, and every operator in the chain can credibly point elsewhere (the runtime executed the mandate, the protocol transported it, the facilitator broadcast what was signed, the issuer transferred per a valid authorization, the chain finalized a valid transaction). Incident handling and dependency operability: the facilitator is a hard runtime dependency with no licensing, no registration, no capital or operational requirements, and (on two of three chains) majority operation by parties with direct economic interest in measured volume; facilitator exit, key compromise, or discretionary censorship has no committed remediation path at any layer.

The composition-level gap is invisible at single-operator level: every operator in this chain can be individually defensible while the composed product delivers an irreversible payment, on the instruction of a non-human agent, through an unlicensed intermediary, to an unidentifiable counterparty. Existing AI-governance frameworks evaluate the model operator; existing payments regulation evaluates licensed money transmitters and banks. The facilitator sits in neither perimeter, and the measured market routes majority-through it.

Limitations

  • Attribution is registry-bounded. Community-maintained sets; estimated 2 to 3 points of unattributed machine-shaped Base coverage; at least one facilitator settles from per-customer wallets with no public submitter set and is unmeasured.
  • Proxy-settlement correction is Meridian-complete only. Aggregate excess of transfers over authorization events is 2.8 percent on Base, but one other facilitator shows a 15 percent excess that is unexplained; per-facilitator pairing across the full set is queued before any per-facilitator value series ships beyond May.
  • Meridian's 17-buyer flow composition (customers versus affiliates versus incentive recirculation) is not determinable on-chain; flagged, not asserted.
  • Solana per-facilitator history beyond May, and EURC and multi-token schemes, are unmeasured.
  • June figures are 11-day partials and excluded from all concentration claims.
  • Denominators are raw transfer events, an upper-bound activity base including DEX and contract flows.

Reproducibility and data access

Every number above reproduces from committed SQL queries, raw result sets, a merged facilitator-attribution table, and a concentration recompute script that reproduces the raw readings before applying the documented proxy-settlement correction. External anchors (operator documentation, vendor dashboards, published estimates) were verified at the measurement date and move weekly; re-verify before re-use.
