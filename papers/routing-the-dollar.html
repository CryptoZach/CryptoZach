Routing the Dollar
Gateway Infrastructure, Monetary Policy Transmission, and the Dollar's International Functions

~3 min brief · ~25 min full read · March 2026 · SSRN · FRBNY Conference

Description current as of March 2026.

Whether stablecoins strengthen or weaken the dollar's international functions depends not on the tokens but on the gateways that route them.

Read on SSRN (PDF) GitHub (data + replication)
Request deeper memo · Thread

If you only read one thing

  • The gateway (the issuer, exchange, or protocol that routes the token) matters more for regulation and supervision than the token itself.
  • Stablecoin supply and Federal Reserve balance-sheet variables move in opposite directions, with supply nearly doubling after the September 2024 rate cut while the Fed continued shrinking its balance sheet. This pattern appears only for dollar stablecoins, not for Bitcoin or Ethereum, which means the relationship is tied to the dollar system specifically, not to crypto markets in general.
  • During the SVB crisis, three gateways that looked equally regulated (MakerDAO, Paxos, Gemini) produced opposite results because they used different banks to hold their reserves.

Evidence base: Three years of on-chain gateway data (19 entities, 51 addresses), regime-dependent cointegration with Federal Reserve balance-sheet variables, and stress-episode analysis of the SVB crisis and BUSD regulatory action.

What this paper changes

Most stablecoin analysis focuses on the token: classification, reserve composition, market capitalization. This paper shifts the unit of analysis to the gateway: the issuer, exchange, or protocol that actually routes the token.

Once you measure at that layer, the same dollar token produces different regulatory exposure, different monetary-policy transmission, and different stress behavior.

1. Stablecoin supply moves in the opposite direction from the Federal Reserve's balance sheet, and only for dollar stablecoins

Stablecoin supply and Federal Reserve balance-sheet variables move in opposite directions. During tightening, supply grew 123% even as the Fed drained reserves. After the September 2024 rate cut, supply nearly doubled from $162 billion to $305 billion while the Fed continued shrinking its balance sheet. The same relationship does not appear for Bitcoin or Ethereum, which means the connection is tied to the dollar system specifically, not to crypto markets in general.

The yield spread between Treasury bill rates and on-chain lending rates provides supporting evidence. The connection works primarily through issuer economics: issuers hold reserves in Treasury bills and earn that yield while paying holders nothing. As the spread between what issuers earn and what holders receive shifts with Fed policy, the profitability of each dollar of stablecoin issued changes, and supply responds. Current U.S. stablecoin legislation reinforces this channel by prohibiting issuers from paying yield to holders.

What this means: stablecoin supply moves with Federal Reserve policy through reserve structures and banking relationships, not through markets reacting to Fed announcements. The relationship changes character across monetary regimes but persists through both tightening and easing.

2. Gateway choice produces materially different regulatory and concentration outcomes

The Control Layer Intensity Index scores 19 gateway entities across licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions. Over three years, the total volume moving through gateways doubled, but the number of distinct entities transacting with each other dropped by 25%, and the share of transactions crossing between different gateways fell from 5.8% to 2.6%. More money, fewer participants, less interconnection.

Usage expanded. The routing network concentrated.

What this means: watching total stablecoin supply misses the concentration happening underneath. Gateway-level transfer data reveal which entities are gaining share, which connections are disappearing, and where exposure is building.

3. SVB stress rotated capital across gateways rather than out of dollar instruments

During the SVB episode, capital rotated across dollar gateways rather than exiting dollar-pegged instruments. Tier 1 (the most heavily regulated) gateway share dropped from 63% to 13%, then recovered to 57% after banking-sector interventions. Three gateways with zero direct SVB exposure (MakerDAO, Paxos, Gemini) saw their instruments depeg because their reserves included USDC, which had direct exposure to SVB.

Stress transmitted through the gateway layer, not through token properties.

What this means: How heavily a gateway is regulated does not predict how it performs under stress. Three gateways that scored almost identically on regulatory intensity (0.82 to 0.92 out of 1.0) produced opposite results during SVB because they used different banks to hold their reserves. An index that measures compliance should not be expected to predict which gateways fail under stress; those are separate questions that require separate monitoring.

Who should care

Policymakers: If the goal is understanding how Fed policy affects stablecoin markets, measuring transfers at the gateway level (which issuer, exchange, or protocol processed each transaction) provides better visibility than watching total stablecoin supply alone. The CLII classifies where regulatory obligations attach across 19 gateways. The automatic rules that move money when no one actively chooses, such as default sweep destinations, routing preferences, and hold triggers, are where concentrated exposure builds up unnoticed.

Issuers and operators: Where reserves are held, which banks hold them, and how the automatic routing rules behave under stress determine how a product performs, not the token's label or brand. The broader monetary impact of stablecoin growth depends on which issuer absorbs it: different issuers hold reserves at different banks, and those deposits re-enter the banking system as funds that each bank lends and manages differently.

Investors and market-structure teams: How heavily a gateway is regulated does not predict how it performs under stress. Assessing both requires measuring transfers at the gateway level. Looking only at token-level totals cannot distinguish between gateways that meet the same compliance standards but use different banks, different custody arrangements, and different reserve structures.

Who routes the dollar matters more than which dollar is routed.

Full technical summary

Abstract

This paper introduces a gateway-level analytical framework for understanding how dollar-denominated stablecoins interact with Federal Reserve monetary policy and the dollar's international functions. Using a 157-week panel across nine blockchains, a 51-address Ethereum counterparty registry, and Johansen cointegration on a three-variable system (Fed total assets, ON RRP, stablecoin supply), the analysis identifies regime-dependent cointegration consistent with money-market transmission. Cointegration is strongest during quantitative tightening (trace = 48.18) and weaker during easing (trace = 17.71), while level correlations are strongest during easing (r = -0.98). Direct policy-rate channels play a weaker role; no FOMC event study result survives Bonferroni correction.

The Control Layer Intensity Index (CLII) scores regulatory intensity at the gateway layer across five dimensions: Regulatory License 25%, Reserve Transparency 20%, Freeze/Blacklist Capability 20%, Compliance Infrastructure 20%, Geographic Restrictions/Sanctions 15%. Tier 1 above 0.75; Tier 2 from 0.30 to 0.75; Tier 3 below 0.30. The CLII measures who is regulated, not who is fragile: three Tier 1 gateways with near-identical scores (0.82 to 0.92) produced opposite outcomes during SVB based on entity-specific banking exposure. Regulatory posture and stress resilience are distinct properties; conflating them misframes both the compliance question and the stability question.

The SVB stress episode traces vertical contagion through MakerDAO's Peg Stability Module; three gateways with zero direct SVB exposure (MakerDAO, Paxos, Gemini) saw their issued instruments (DAI, USDP, GUSD) depeg through code-level reserve dependencies. Application-level flow decomposition covers Ethereum, Solana, and Base (64% of volume), with selection-bias robustness across five checks including Nansen cross-validation (Spearman rho = 0.94).
