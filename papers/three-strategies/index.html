Three Tokenization Strategies: Infrastructure Competition, Settlement Architecture, and Operational Risk in Institutional Digital Asset Markets

10 min read·March 2026·Zach Zukowski

~12 min read · 2026 · Working paper

Infrastructure map dated March 27, 2026. Institutional tokenization is evolving rapidly; events after this date may alter sequencing and competitive dynamics.

Institutional tokenization of US securities is proceeding through three distinct strategies: a regulated collateral circuit using tokenized US Treasuries and bank-issued deposit tokens, a multi-issuer yield stack composing tokenized Treasuries from five fund issuers with a smart redemption router, and tokenized equity rails on Nasdaq and NYSE. Each strategy synthesizes infrastructure from different combinations of regulated entities, but the same small set of entities recurs. Securitize, an SEC-registered transfer agent, sits in all three. The system-level risks live in the entities that connect the strategies, not in the strategies themselves.

1

Three distinct strategies are now under construction. Strategy 1 is a regulated collateral circuit using tokenized US Treasuries and bank-issued deposit tokens, cleared at par through Ubyx (a new clearing layer that allows different banks' deposit tokens to settle against each other). Strategy 2 is a multi-issuer yield stack composing tokenized Treasuries from five fund issuers with a smart redemption router. Strategy 3 is tokenized equity rails on Nasdaq and NYSE. The MVEP category profile across the three is a complementarity map, not a competitive ranking.

2

Securitize, an SEC-registered transfer agent (the regulated entity that maintains the authoritative holder register for a security), is transfer agent across all three strategies. Entity-level regulatory scores rate Securitize high. System-effective regulatory strength is lower, because the same entity operates across all three strategies with no documented fallback. A 72-hour Securitize operational failure would degrade all three simultaneously.

When the Depository Trust Company (DTC) operates on business-day cycles and tokens trade 24/7, record dates, ex-dates, and stock splits produce different holders on-chain and on the DTC register at the same instant. Three architectures can close the gap (trading halt, blockchain-as-authoritative, dual-record synchronization); each has distinct operational-risk implications.

What this paper changes

The risk framework most institutions use for tokenization treats each participant as an independent node: the issuer, the transfer agent, the custodian, the clearinghouse, the venue. Each entity is assessed against its own operational and regulatory profile. The three strategies now under construction all route through a small number of the same entities. Securitize is the transfer agent in every strategy. DTC is the authoritative register in two of the three. Ubyx clears the cash leg in two of the three. These shared dependencies concentrate system-level risk in places where entity-level assessments produce a clean bill of health.

The paper maps each strategy against the MVEP's ten diligence categories, develops nine stress scenarios traced through the category failure chains, and identifies the four cross-cutting findings that shape the institutional-tokenization roadmap. The analysis is prospective: the collateral circuit is live in controlled production, the yield stack is composed from operating products, and the tokenized equity rails went from SEC approval (Nasdaq, March 18, 2026) to a signed MoU (NYSE with Securitize, March 24, 2026) in the week before the paper went to draft.

Finding 1. Each strategy is strongest where the others are weakest

Strategy 1 (Regulated Collateral Circuit) is strongest on custody and settlement finality, because it uses DTC and Canton Network's atomic settlement, a design in which the asset leg and cash leg of a transaction either both succeed or both fail. Strategy 2 (Multi-Issuer Yield Stack) is strongest on collateral operability, because the redemption router spreads demand across multiple underlying pools, avoiding concentration in any single one. Strategy 3 (Tokenized Equity Rails) is strongest on rights parity, because SEC rules mandate identical economic and voting rights for tokenized and non-tokenized shares, and weakest on reconciliation, because 24/7 trading runs on a different clock than business-day corporate actions.

Finding 2. Securitize concentration is a system-level risk that entity-level scoring does not capture

Securitize is the transfer agent for BUIDL (BlackRock's tokenized US Treasury money-market fund and the largest on-chain institutional tokenized product), for the BUIDL layer of Strategy 2's yield stack, and for NYSE's digital-asset trading platform. It is also the first digital transfer agent approved to mint on NYSE's Digital Trading Platform and an expected broker-dealer participant there. The same entity therefore connects the collateral asset layer, the yield-stack underlying layer, and the equity-venue register.

A 72-hour Securitize operational failure, from any cause, would freeze the authoritative register in all three strategies simultaneously. No fallback digital transfer agent is documented in the BUIDL operating documents or in the public NYSE Digital Trading Platform materials. Entity-level regulatory scoring rates Securitize high, consistent with its SEC registration and institutional client base. System-effective regulatory strength, measured as the degree of independent redundancy across the three strategies, is lower.

Finding 3. Strategy 3's corporate-actions gap requires an explicit architecture choice

Corporate actions are the business-day events that change share counts, ownership, or economic rights: stock splits, dividends, mergers, and the record dates and ex-dates that govern them. A stock split announced Friday at 4pm with a Monday record date (the date on which a holder must be on the register to receive the corresponding adjustment) illustrates the gap. Tokenized shares of the same CUSIP trade all weekend. If a tokenized share changes hands six times between Friday close and Monday open, the DTC register (updated Monday) and the blockchain ledger (updated continuously) show different holders at the same timestamp. This is the corporate actions reconciliation gap, and it is the most novel failure mode in the tokenized-equities strategy.

Three architectures close the gap. The first halts tokenized trading during corporate-action processing windows, which removes the 24/7 trading feature that motivated tokenization. The second designates the blockchain ledger as authoritative, which requires DTC to change its record-keeping role. The third runs a dual-record synchronization protocol with a named adjudication procedure for divergences at the record date. Each architecture satisfies different MVEP categories and imposes different costs on issuers, exchanges, and holders.

Finding 4. The deposit-token market is control-layer competition applied to institutional settlement

JPMorgan, BNY, and Citi are each building bank-issued deposit tokens. Ubyx is building a clearing layer that allows these tokens to settle against each other at par. If a single issuer wins, settlement-layer rent concentrates with that issuer; if multi-issuer clearing operates, the settlement rent is competed away. This is the same control-layer competition the research program identifies across the dollar stack: who routes the token matters more than what the token is called.

The binding constraint on multi-issuer clearing is not regulatory. It is infrastructure: the clearing function must verify funding and settle in a single atomic operation using pre-funded Delivery versus Payment (DvP, where the cash and asset legs settle simultaneously only after both sides are verified funded), each issuer must expose a settlement account visible exclusively to the clearing layer, and a named procedure must govern cascading settlement failure when an upstream payment bounces. A pre-funded architecture sidesteps the insolvency-posture divergence across issuers (a working-paper extension; see Forthcoming below).

Forthcoming. The pre-funded-clearing architecture extension is drafted and will be added after author verification with Ubyx. The extension is not a revision; it is additional analysis of how pre-funded DvP reshapes the binding constraints.

Who should care

Policymakers. If the goal is to prevent the tokenized-securities buildout from repeating the concentrated-custody failure modes of 2022 to 2023, the supervisory instrument most likely to catch it is a system-level diligence pass that names the shared dependencies across strategies, not an entity-level regulatory rating.

Issuers, operators, and transfer agents. What determines whether a strategy clears its MVEP profile is the independence of its shared-entity dependencies. A strategy strong on custody but weak on transfer-agent redundancy is operationally fragile in ways that entity-level scores do not reveal.

Investors and market-structure teams. The three strategies produce complementary MVEP profiles; the corporate-actions gap in Strategy 3 is the most novel operational risk on the roadmap; the deposit-token competition is the control-layer question for institutional settlement.

Dollar-infrastructure context

Three Tokenization Strategies is the fourth and most forward-looking paper in the Track A dollar-infrastructure sequence. Routing the Dollar establishes the CLII and the gateway-level variation in stablecoin transmission. MVEP develops the ten-category diligence framework from the SVB case. The Seven Dollars work applies both frameworks to seven dollar-denominated digital products. This paper extends the same control-layer logic to institutional settlement infrastructure: tokenized Treasuries, tokenized equities, and bank-issued deposit tokens.

How the paper is built

  • Infrastructure map: eight operating entities supplying institutional-tokenization building blocks (DTCC, Kinexys, Nasdaq, NYSE, Securitize, BNY, JPMorgan, Ubyx), dated March 27, 2026.
  • MVEP mapping: each of the three strategies scored across all ten diligence categories.
  • Stress scenarios: nine strategy-specific scenarios traced through MVEP category failure chains (three per strategy).
  • Cross-strategy concentration analysis: Securitize as transfer agent across all three strategies; DTC and Ubyx as shared dependencies in two of three.
  • Corporate-actions gap: three architectures specified (trading halt, blockchain-as-authoritative, dual-record synchronization) with MVEP implications.
  • Deposit-token competition framework: JPMD, BNY deposits, Citi deposits; Ubyx clearing function as the determinant of monopoly versus competitive outcome.
  • Phase-gate structure: quantitative thresholds gating each strategy transition ($100M minted, 30 consecutive days, 2% daily volume-to-outstanding ratio).

Abstract

Opening and claim boundary. Institutional tokenization of US securities is proceeding through three distinct strategies. The analysis is prospective and descriptive; the stress scenarios are hypothetical, no DTC-tokenized Treasury has been used as margin collateral in production, no smart redemption router operates at institutional scale, and no tokenized equity has processed a stock split on a 24/7 trading rail. None of the findings should be read as causal estimates.

Data. Public regulatory filings and announcements from March 2025 through March 2026; SEC approvals (Nasdaq March 18, 2026; NYSE-Securitize MoU March 24, 2026; BNY tokenized deposits January 9, 2026; DTCC SEC no-action authorization for Canton-based Russell 1000 pilot); public technical specifications for Canton Network, Kinexys JPMD, and Ubyx clearing; author-assessed CLII scores for participant entities based on public information.

Strategy 1 (Section 3.1). Regulated Collateral Circuit. DTC-tokenized Treasuries as 24/7 margin collateral, asset leg on Canton, cash leg in bank-issued deposit tokens (JPMD primary; BNY, Citi optional), clearing through Ubyx. Three liquidation paths specified: secondary market, redemption, bilateral swap. Satisfies all ten MVEP categories by construction.

Strategy 2 (Section 3.2). Multi-Issuer Yield Stack. Composed product holding five tokenized Treasury products (BUIDL, BENJI, FDIT, WTGXX, ULTRA) with a smart redemption router that queries available liquidity and routes to the deepest available pool. Five layers: underlying, router, transfer-agent aggregation, yield-mechanism normalization, multi-chain deployment (Canton, Ethereum, Solana). The Ondo-Oasis Pro acquisition closes the Category 2 gap the MVEP identifies.

Strategy 3 (Section 3.3). Tokenized Equity Rails. Issuance-to-trading pipeline for US equities on SEC-approved exchange infrastructure. Nasdaq-Kraken partnership for global distribution; NYSE-Securitize MoU for digital transfer-agent services. Same CUSIP, same rights, same order book.

Cross-cutting findings (Section 5). Securitize concentration risk: entity is transfer agent in all three strategies; no documented fallback. Corporate-actions reconciliation gap: three architectures specified. Deposit-token competition: Ubyx clearing function determines monopoly versus multi-issuer outcome.

Phase gates (Section 8). Quantitative thresholds for each strategy transition. Phase 1 to Phase 2: $100M minted on Canton circuit, 30 days of JPMD 24/7 settlement without material downtime, two-issuer Ubyx clearing, one end-to-end off-hours margin call. Phase 2 to Phase 3: 90 consecutive days of exchange tokenized-equity trading, one corporate action processed with documented reconciliation, Securitize demonstrating SEC-registered TA functions for fund shares and equities, 2% daily volume-to-outstanding ratio for 30 consecutive days.

Scope boundary. CLII scores for strategy participants are author-assessed from public information; Securitize's score in particular would require internal compliance documentation to validate. Phase-gate thresholds reflect the author's assessment of institutional-adoption minimums; different institutional contexts may require different thresholds. The corporate-actions analysis is forward-looking; no jurisdiction has finalized a regulatory framework for 24/7 corporate-action processing.
