Who Controls Agentic Payments? Facilitator Concentration, Subsidy Pipelines, and Settlement Accountability in x402

Working paper · SSRN 7396241 · DOI 10.2139/ssrn.7396241

Posted to SSRN on September 5, 2026. Concentration figures are anchored to the July 2026 snapshot, with May 2026 retained throughout as the payment-level-validated baseline. The companion control-layer measurement memo is the empirical core and carries the reproduction detail; this paper is the separate scholarly treatment.

Agentic payments are usually described by their tokens and their throughput. The more consequential question is who routes them. When an agent pays over x402, the value moves as a familiar stablecoin, but the payment clears through a facilitator: the operator that verifies the authorization, submits settlement on chain, and pays the gas. The buyer never broadcasts.

That makes a small set of operators the locus of three questions token-level measurement cannot reach: how concentrated the settlement layer is, what happens to the agents depending on a facilitator when it fails or withdraws, and which supervisor holds it to account. This paper measures all three across the chains that carry materially all x402 activity.

At a glance

Question
Who controls agentic payments: how concentrated is the facilitator layer that settles them, how much of the measured activity is organic, and where does accountability sit in the chain that delivers an agent-initiated payment?

Answer
The settlement layer is more concentrated than the stablecoin gateway layer beneath it, the count layer and the value layer have different monopolists, most headline activity is subsidized or self-referential, and no layer of the composition chain currently composes accountability.

Evidence base
A settlement census across Base, Polygon and Solana, built from the x402scan facilitator registry (138 address entries across 29 facilitators and 3 networks) merged with two curated attribution maps, validated at payment level. On Base the registry set accounts for 92.3 percent of trailing-30-day authorization events, a conservative lower bound on coverage.

Five findings

1

Settlement is more concentrated than the gateway layer it sits on

July 2026 facilitator concentration measures at HHI 6,512 by payment count and 4,700 by corrected dollar volume on Base, 4,071 and 3,831 on Solana, and effective single-operator service on Polygon. Every reading clears both merger-guidelines thresholds for a highly concentrated market.

What this means: the layer that actually routes agentic payments is more concentrated than the stablecoin gateway infrastructure beneath it, which is already a supervised concern.

2

The count layer and the value layer have different monopolists

On Base, Coinbase's CDP facilitator carries 78.5 percent of July payment counts while Meridian, a proxy-settlement facilitator, carries 62.6 percent of corrected July dollars. On Solana the split repeats independently: Dexter 52.2 percent of counts, payAI 56.2 percent of dollars. The cast is not fixed across months.

What this means: a measurement that reports one layer names the wrong dominant operator. Supervisory attention aimed at the count leader misses where the money concentrates.

3

Buyer cardinality exposes subsidized pipelines without a wash-trading heuristic

The paper's methodological contribution is events per distinct buyer per month, computable at submitter, facilitator or chain level from settlement data alone. Applied to July, the official Polygon Facilitator, a 24-address fleet under a public $1 million gas-rebate programme, produced 5.61 million payments, 55.8 percent of cross-chain counts, from 52 buyer wallets recirculating seeded USDC.

What this means: headline transaction counts in this market are dominated by subsidized and self-referential flow, and the separation needs no judgment call about intent.

4

At the value layer the market is currently small

Corrected cross-chain July volume is $583,430 across 10.06 million payments; counts rose while dollars fell against May. Measured against its own settlement rails in the validated May baseline, x402 activity was 2.83 percent of all USDC transfer events on the three chains that carry materially all of it.

What this means: the policy question is about the structure forming now, not about present systemic scale. Structure set at this size is what persists if the category grows.

5

No layer of the composition chain composes accountability

Settlement is push-payment and irreversible at the protocol layer, so no chargeback path exists. The facilitator layer is unlicensed and unregistered, and on two of three chains is dominated by an operator with a direct economic interest in the measured volume. The sell-side endpoints are pseudonymous. In May, 99.66 percent of the 110,727 distinct buyers authorizing on Base depended on a single facilitator.

What this means: stranding exposure is measured rather than notional, and there is currently no layer at which a harmed agent's principal can seek redress.

What this paper changes

Existing x402 measurement counts transactions, estimates volume, and filters wash activity. That work answers "how much," and its answers disagree by an order of magnitude depending on filters. This paper asks "through whom," which is the question that determines financial-stability and consumer-protection exposure, for the same reason it mattered for stablecoins in the program's earlier gateway work.

Two structural properties of the protocol make the question answerable. The buyer never broadcasts, so every settled payment passes through a facilitator and the facilitator layer is fully observable at per-payment granularity from public chain data. And settlement is a one-way authorization, so the payment is irreversible once submitted. The first property is what makes the measurement possible today; the paper discusses why that observability is likely temporary.

Core findings

Five results organize the paper: concentration above both antitrust thresholds on every chain measured; different monopolists at the count and value layers, replicated independently on two chains; subsidized and proxy pipelines dominating headline counts, exposed by buyer cardinality; a value layer that is small measured against its own rails; and a composition chain with no accountable layer.

A sixth, methodological result conditions the rest: vendor and community measurements of this market diverge by an order of magnitude, and the divergences reduce to attribution-set and filter choices. The widely cited May 2026 community dashboard volume of $179,793 reflects an address set that misses Meridian entirely; the same month recomputes to $1,030,242 raw on Base. Reconciling those readings is itself a contribution, because it shows that measurement infrastructure rather than underlying activity explains most of the disagreement.

Methods and data

Settlement fingerprint. The dominant exact-EVM scheme settles via transferWithAuthorization() on EIP-3009 tokens, in practice USDC: the buyer signs an EIP-712 authorization off chain and the facilitator submits it, which leaves a per-payment record attributable to the submitting operator.

Census frame. Three merged sources: the machine-readable x402scan facilitator registry (138 address entries across 29 facilitators and 3 networks) plus two curated attribution maps. On Base the registry set accounts for 92.3 percent of trailing-30-day authorization events, which is a conservative lower bound on coverage because the denominator includes non-x402 EIP-3009 traffic.

The buyer-cardinality classifier. Events per distinct buyer per month, computed at submitter, facilitator or chain level, separates organic usage from pipeline activity using settlement data only.

Denominator discipline. Concentration claims are anchored to the July 2026 snapshot, with the payment-level-validated May 2026 snapshot retained throughout as the baseline, and every number reproduces from the committed measurement store described in the paper's reproducibility appendix.

Limitations

The measurement rests on the current settlement fingerprint, and that fingerprint is not permanent: newer transfer methods and batching would reduce per-payment observability. Attribution depends on registry and curated address sets, which is precisely the dependency the reconciliation section quantifies. The concentration series is anchored to one snapshot month with a validated baseline, and external anchors in this market move weekly. The paper carries its own verification queue rather than treating any of these as closed.
