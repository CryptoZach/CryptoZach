Letters

Federal Comment Letters: eight letters, six agencies, ten days.

April 10 to April 20, 2026 · GENIUS Act and related rulemakings.

Six dockets, seven rulemakings, three frameworks applied. The Federal Comment Letter Program used CLII (operator regulatory intensity), MVEP (institutional product diligence), and the control-layer view across the GENIUS Act implementation cycle. Fifty-two recommendations across the eight letters. Each letter summarizes what the proposed rule does, identifies gaps, and proposes specific fixes.

1

Structural thesis: regulate the operator, audit the product. The program's central analytical move separates two questions that rulemaking practice has been collapsing. The CLII scores regulatory intensity at the operator level. The MVEP scores institutional diligence at the product level. Applied across eight rulemakings, this separation surfaces gaps that neither frame alone would catch: collateral interoperability on Binance; the interface-layer asymmetry exposed by the SEC CUIP statement; the custody-concentration channel documented during SVB.

2

First statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation. The joint FinCEN-OFAC rulemaking (FINCEN-2026-0100) operationalizes the GENIUS Act Section 1033.240 block/freeze/reject technical capability, applied to both primary and secondary market activity. The program's comment on that docket is the most framework-intensive letter in the set, applying all three frameworks in depth and engaging 50 of 58 questions in the Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (86% coverage).

3

Principal substantive contribution: MVEP-gated conditional deposit insurance pass-through. Recommendation 7 of the closing AG19 letter proposes that the FDIC replace §330.11(a)(3)'s categorical foreclosure of pass-through insurance with a conditional framework gated on MVEP qualification across six pass-through-critical categories. Pass-through attaches to the reserve deposit at the IDI, not to the payment stablecoin itself; consistent with 12 U.S.C. 5903(e)(1) and (e)(2). Makes pass-through harder to achieve than the current market state, rewarding only operators whose architectures provide genuine structural equivalents of deposit protection.

The eight letters

Each card leads with the substantive finding. Click through for the full rule summary, gaps, and proposed fixes.

1. OCC PPSI MVEP (anchor) Three tokenized U.S. Treasury products under three regulatory regimes receive comparable collateral treatment on Binance through Ceffu, with no counterparty disclosure about which instrument backs any given trade. Gaps: collateral interoperability is unregulated; MSB capital runs roughly 22-to-1 below the banking-law equivalent for a $40B issuer. 2026-04-10 · OCC · MVEP · 3 fixes 2. OCC Supplemental (control-layer) A user transacting in a PPSI-issued stablecoin through an SEC-CUIP-covered interface receives none of the twelve interface-conduct conditions; the protection gradient is inverted. Gaps: interface-layer asymmetry between crypto-asset-securities and payment-stablecoin transactions on the same interface; no third-party diligence framework attaches. 2026-04-13 · OCC · Control-layer + CLII · 3 recs 3. Treasury GENIUS (state-similarity) Treasury must evaluate fifty state regulatory frameworks as substantially similar to the federal GENIUS Act framework; the proposed principles frame this as section-by-section assessment against statutory text. Gaps: text-matching cannot detect functional-dimension drift across reserve transparency, capital adequacy, activity scope, insolvency, and interface-layer conduct. 2026-04-15 · Treasury · Control-layer + CLII + MVEP · 6 fixes 4. FDIC AG20 (application) The AG20 application is the FDIC's first and foundational opportunity to map a prospective PPSI's control-layer topology, shaping both the approval decision and the baseline for ongoing supervision. Gaps: §303.252 filing content does not surface the structural features (operator, custodian, interface, sweep) that are consequential for either decision. 2026-04-18 · FDIC · MVEP + CLII · 4 recs 5. FinCEN AML/CFT NPRM AML regulation has always been control-layer regulation: the Bank Secrecy Act attaches obligations to intermediaries, not to transactions or instruments. Gaps: tokenized-finance intermediation disaggregates across issuer, custodian, interface, router, sweep, and hold-and-freeze layers; the proposed risk-based directive does not articulate this compatibility. 2026-04-18 · FinCEN · Control-layer + CLII · 7 recs 6. FinCEN-OFAC PPSI (sanctions) The joint NPRM operationalizes the first statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation: GENIUS Act §1033.240's block, freeze, and reject technical capability across primary and secondary market activity. Gaps: implementing technical capability without explicit MVEP-mapped diligence categories leaves both compliance evidence and supervisory observability under-specified. 2026-04-18 · FinCEN + OFAC · CLII + MVEP + Control-layer · 10 recs, 50 of 58 NPRM Qs 7. Banking agencies AML (joint) Banks increasingly perform control-layer functions in tokenized finance: PPSI reserve custody, tokenized deposit issuance, tokenized securities custody, Federal qualified PPSI operation, and correspondent services to issuers and interfaces. Gaps: 2026 re-issuance dropped the Federal Reserve Board from the 2024 proposal; the resulting consistency gap is addressed by Recommendation 6. 2026-04-18 · OCC + FDIC + NCUA · CLII · 7 recs 8. FDIC AG19 PPSI activities (closing) Recommendation 7 proposes an MVEP-gated conditional deposit insurance pass-through replacing §330.11(a)(3)'s categorical foreclosure, with pass-through attaching to the reserve deposit at the IDI rather than the payment stablecoin itself. Gaps: categorical foreclosure forecloses incentive-aligned design; current market-state pass-through claims would not qualify under MVEP-gated assessment. 2026-04-20 · FDIC · MVEP + CLII · 12 recs

Program context

Six dockets opened simultaneously in the GENIUS Act implementation cycle: three AML rulemakings published April 10, 2026 (FinCEN 0034, FinCEN-OFAC 0100, banking joint 91 FR 18304), two FDIC PPSI rulemakings (AG20, AG19), the OCC PPSI rule already in comment period, and Treasury's state-similarity rulemaking. Filing as one coordinated program let the recommendations cross-reference: the OCC MVEP anchor (cited by five subsequent filings per DEC-089) seeded the diligence vocabulary; the OCC Supplemental introduced the control-layer frame; later letters extended both to new domains. Four comment periods remain open through June 9, 2026; the GENIUS Act statutory rulemaking deadline of July 18, 2026 governs the PPSI implementing rules.

Framework deployment. CLII applied in 7 of 8 letters; MVEP in 5; control-layer five-element in 4. Per Frameworks: CLII is the continuous-monitoring drift detector, MVEP is the entry gate, the control-layer framework is the structural spine both operate on.

Related research. The letter program is the applied-regulatory expression of the Track A papers: Routing the Dollar (CLII), MVEP, Seven Dollars (independence of the two frameworks), Three Tokenization Strategies. See also Overview: Federal Comment Letter Program.
