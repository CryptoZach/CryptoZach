Program summary

Twenty-one dockets, nine agencies, three frameworks applied. The Federal Comment Letter Program used CLII (operator regulatory intensity), MVEP (institutional product diligence), and the control-layer view across the GENIUS Act implementation cycle; the SEC's draft strategic plan, Regulation Crypto Assets proposal, tokenized NMS stock exemptions, and transfer agent rules; the Board of Governors' anti-money-laundering program rule; the CFTC's clearing-organization information collection; the FDIC's resolution, assessments, and disclosure-of-information proposals; Treasury's GENIUS Act proposal on who may issue payment stablecoins; and FinCEN's proposed A7 Network special measure. The Regulation Crypto Assets and FDIC disclosure-of-information letters apply none of the three. Each letter summarizes what the proposed rule or plan does, identifies gaps, and proposes specific recommendations.

Program context

Six dockets opened simultaneously in April 2026 in the GENIUS Act implementation cycle, and filing as one coordinated program let the recommendations cross-reference: the OCC MVEP anchor seeded the diligence vocabulary, the OCC supplemental introduced the control-layer frame, and the later letters extended both to the SEC (Draft Strategic Plan, Regulation Crypto Assets, tokenized NMS stock exemptions, and transfer agent rules), the Board of Governors, the CFTC, the FDIC's resolution, assessments, and disclosure proposals, Treasury's proposal on payment stablecoin issuance, offer, and sale, including by foreign issuers, and FinCEN's proposed A7 Network special measure. The letters below carry the chronology; each letter page records its docket, its comment period, and what has happened since.

Related research. The letter program is the applied-regulatory expression of the Track A papers: Routing the Dollar (CLII), MVEP, Seven Dollars (independence of the two frameworks), Three Tokenization Strategies. See also Overview: Federal Comment Letter Program, the recommendation index, and the Atom feed of this series.

What the program established

1

Structural thesis: regulate the operator, audit the product. The program's central analytical move separates two questions that rulemaking practice has been collapsing. The CLII scores regulatory intensity at the operator level. The MVEP scores institutional diligence at the product level. Applied across the program's rulemakings, this separation surfaces gaps that neither frame alone would catch: collateral interoperability on Binance; the interface-layer asymmetry exposed by the SEC staff statement on Covered User Interface Providers; the custody-concentration channel documented during the failure of Silicon Valley Bank.

2

First statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation. The joint FinCEN-OFAC rulemaking on anti-money-laundering and sanctions programs for stablecoin issuers operationalizes the GENIUS Act block, freeze and reject technical capability, applied to both primary and secondary market activity. The program's comment on that docket engages 50 of the 58 questions in the notice.

3

Principal substantive contribution: MVEP-gated conditional deposit insurance pass-through. Recommendation 7 of the letter on the FDIC's stablecoin issuer standards proposes that the FDIC replace its proposed categorical bar on pass-through insurance for stablecoin reserve deposits with a conditional framework gated on MVEP qualification across six pass-through-critical categories. Pass-through attaches to the reserve deposit at the insured bank, not to the payment stablecoin itself; consistent with the GENIUS Act's provisions that payment stablecoins are not federally insured and may not be represented as insured. Makes pass-through harder to achieve than the current market state, rewarding only operators whose architectures provide genuine structural equivalents of deposit protection.

No. 27 FinCEN A7 Network Special Measure The proposal places nearly all of its work on convertible virtual currency (CVC) on the receiving institution, although FinCEN itself assesses that there are "few, if any, readily available ways" for that institution to reject an incoming transfer before receipt, and it does not say what the rule would require of the payment stablecoin issuers that are about to become covered by it. Gaps: the proposed list identifies entities, while a blockchain transfer carries addresses, not names; payment stablecoin issuers become covered institutions by cross-reference, and neither proposal says what this rule then requires of them; the preamble's rule that OFAC blocking counts as compliance is not in the regulatory text; the safe harbor does not separate blocking from returning; the notice and records do not identify a transfer by its transaction hash. 2026-11-04 · FinCEN · Control-layer, on-chain measurement · 5 recommendations No. 26 SEC Transfer Agent Rules The proposal lets a crypto network carry the master securityholder file, the authoritative record of who owns an issuer's securities, but does not say which record governs when a token and the off-chain file disagree, does not require that a token's supply be reconciled to the file, and does not define exclusive control for a ledger the transfer agent shares with everyone else on the network. Gaps: no rule says which record governs when a token and the off-chain file disagree; on-chain supply is never reconciled to the file, so tokens minted beyond it are neither a record difference nor an overissuance; exclusive control is undefined for a shared ledger, and viewing a public ledger counts as independent access; the rule text records the results of a token's privileged functions but never the functions or who holds them; the 15-day handover moves records but not keys; the annual report transfer agents file never links an issue to the network that carries its record. 2026-11-03 · SEC · Control-layer · 7 recommendations No. 25 SEC: temporary exemptions for trading tokenized stock Several of the Order's conditions turn on facts the public ledger already shows, but the Order collects them as descriptions of procedure, only for a venue's own contracts, or not at all; a five-year exemption meant to inform rulemaking should collect them from its first day, in a form checkable against the ledger.
