Federal Comment Letters: eighteen letters, eight agencies.

April 10 to August 21, 2026 · GENIUS Act and related rulemakings, plus the SEC draft strategic plan.

Thirteen dockets, thirteen rulemakings plus the SEC draft strategic plan, three frameworks applied. The Federal Comment Letter Program used CLII (operator regulatory intensity), MVEP (institutional product diligence), and the control-layer view across the GENIUS Act implementation cycle and the Commission's strategic plan. Ninety-seven recommendations across thirteen principal letters, with three supplements: two June FinCEN supplements that add first-party empirical evidence, and a June 22 NCUA actionability supplement. Each letter summarizes what the proposed rule or plan does, identifies gaps, and proposes specific recommendations.

1

Structural thesis: regulate the operator, audit the product. The program's central analytical move separates two questions that rulemaking practice has been collapsing. The CLII scores regulatory intensity at the operator level. The MVEP scores institutional diligence at the product level. Applied across eight rulemakings, this separation surfaces gaps that neither frame alone would catch: collateral interoperability on Binance; the interface-layer asymmetry exposed by the SEC CUIP statement; the custody-concentration channel documented during SVB.

2

First statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation. The joint FinCEN-OFAC rulemaking (FINCEN-2026-0100) operationalizes the GENIUS Act Section 1033.240 block/freeze/reject technical capability, applied to both primary and secondary market activity. The program's comment on that docket is the most framework-intensive letter in the set, applying all three frameworks in depth and engaging 50 of 58 questions in the Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (86% coverage).

3

Principal substantive contribution: MVEP-gated conditional deposit insurance pass-through. Recommendation 7 of the closing AG19 letter proposes that the FDIC replace §330.11(a)(3)'s categorical foreclosure of pass-through insurance with a conditional framework gated on MVEP qualification across six pass-through-critical categories. Pass-through attaches to the reserve deposit at the IDI, not to the payment stablecoin itself; consistent with 12 U.S.C. 5903(e)(1) and (e)(2). Makes pass-through harder to achieve than the current market state, rewarding only operators whose architectures provide genuine structural equivalents of deposit protection.

The eighteen letters

Each card leads with the substantive finding. Click through for the full rule summary, gaps, and proposed recommendations. Three are supplements: two June deadline-day FinCEN supplements that strengthen April recommendations with first-party evidence, and a same-day NCUA actionability supplement that routes the principal NCUA comment.

1. OCC PPSI MVEP (anchor) Three tokenized U.S. Treasury products under three regulatory regimes receive comparable collateral treatment on Binance through Ceffu, with no counterparty disclosure about which instrument backs any given trade. Gaps: collateral interoperability is unregulated; MSB capital runs roughly 22-to-1 below the banking-law equivalent for a $40B issuer. 2026-04-10 · OCC · MVEP · 3 recs 2. OCC Supplemental (control-layer) A user transacting in a PPSI-issued stablecoin through an SEC-CUIP-covered interface receives none of the twelve interface-conduct conditions; the protection gradient is inverted. Gaps: interface-layer asymmetry between crypto-asset-securities and payment-stablecoin transactions on the same interface; no third-party diligence framework attaches. 2026-04-13 · OCC · Control-layer + CLII · 1 rec 3. Treasury GENIUS (state-similarity) Treasury must evaluate fifty state regulatory frameworks as substantially similar to the federal GENIUS Act framework; the proposed principles frame this as section-by-section assessment against statutory text. Gaps: text-matching cannot detect functional-dimension drift across reserve transparency, capital adequacy, activity scope, insolvency, and interface-layer conduct. 2026-04-15 · Treasury · Control-layer + CLII + MVEP · 6 recs 4. FDIC AG20 (application) The AG20 application is the FDIC's first and foundational opportunity to map a prospective PPSI's control-layer topology, shaping both the approval decision and the baseline for ongoing supervision. Gaps: §303.252 filing content does not surface the structural features (operator, custodian, interface, sweep) that are consequential for either decision. 2026-04-18 · FDIC · MVEP + CLII · 4 recs 5. FinCEN AML/CFT NPRM AML regulation has always been control-layer regulation: the Bank Secrecy Act attaches obligations to intermediaries, not to transactions or instruments. Gaps: tokenized-finance intermediation disaggregates across issuer, custodian, interface, router, sweep, and hold-and-freeze layers; the proposed risk-based directive does not articulate this compatibility. 2026-04-18 · FinCEN · Control-layer + CLII · 7 recs 6. FinCEN-OFAC PPSI (sanctions) The joint NPRM operationalizes the first statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation: GENIUS Act §1033.240's block, freeze, and reject technical capability across primary and secondary market activity. Gaps: implementing technical capability without explicit MVEP-mapped diligence categories leaves both compliance evidence and supervisory observability under-specified. 2026-04-18 · FinCEN + OFAC · CLII + MVEP + Control-layer · 10 recs, 50 of 58 NPRM Qs 7. Banking agencies AML (joint) Banks increasingly perform control-layer functions in tokenized finance: PPSI reserve custody, tokenized deposit issuance, tokenized securities custody, Federal qualified PPSI operation, and correspondent services to issuers and interfaces. Gaps: 2026 re-issuance dropped the Federal Reserve Board from the 2024 proposal; the resulting consistency gap is addressed by Recommendation 6. 2026-04-18 · OCC + FDIC + NCUA · CLII · 7 recs 8. FDIC AG19 PPSI activities (closing) Recommendation 7 proposes an MVEP-gated conditional deposit insurance pass-through replacing §330.11(a)(3)'s categorical foreclosure, with pass-through attaching to the reserve deposit at the IDI rather than the payment stablecoin itself. Gaps: categorical foreclosure forecloses incentive-aligned design; current market-state pass-through claims would not qualify under MVEP-gated assessment. 2026-04-20 · FDIC · MVEP + CLII · 12 recs 9. FinCEN AML/CFT supplemental A deadline-day supplement strengthening Recommendation 4: the United States based AML/CFT officer should hold effective supervisory control, not a nominal title. New evidence: in a 52-protocol study of token-governed systems, effective voting control diverged from the nominal locus of holdings in 13 of 18 protocols, by 2.5x to 25.6x. 2026-06-09 · FinCEN · Control-layer · supplements FINCEN-2026-0034-0008 10. FinCEN-OFAC PPSI supplemental A deadline-day supplement strengthening Recommendation 6: PPSI risk assessments must address reserve-custody concentration. New evidence: a within-unit event study of the SVB shock shows a 9.77-point USDC depeg whose recovery broke at the federal backstop announcement (Chow F = 7.41, p = 0.0012). 2026-06-09 · FinCEN + OFAC · Control-layer · supplements FINCEN-2026-0100-0005 11. NCUA GENIUS stablecoin (completes coverage) Stablecoin issuance standards for NCUA-licensed credit unions, the credit-union analogue of the OCC rule. Completes coverage of every GENIUS-implementing agency. Distinctive contribution: cross-agency parity, flagging where the NCUA proposal and the OCC and FDIC rules diverge in ways that invite charter arbitrage. 2026-06-22 · NCUA · MVEP + CLII · 11 recs 12. NCUA GENIUS supplemental A same-day actionability supplement to the NCUA comment of record: a routing table keyed to the questions, a cross-agency alignment map, and suggested rule text for the five highest-priority asks. Adds no new recommendations; it lowers the cost for agency staff to route and act on the eleven. 2026-06-22 · NCUA · Routing + suggested text · supplements NCUA-2026-1024 13. FDIC AG29 BSA and sanctions (PPSI) The FDIC's supervision-and-coordination layer over a PPSI AML and sanctions program works only when the FDIC and FinCEN read the same measured control posture of the same operator. Gaps: the effective-program disposition is left attestation-shaped; nothing keys the FDIC-to-FinCEN consultation to a shared, reproducible control-posture record. 2026-06-30 · FDIC · MVEP + CLII · 6 recs 14. SEC Draft Strategic Plan (first SEC comment) The plan's digital-asset objectives turn on a measurement capability the plan does not yet name: where a framework depends on whether a system is decentralized, that decentralization must be measured against a reproducible standard, not asserted. Gaps: naive holder lists inflate measured concentration 2.3x median (up to ~18x); launch allocation does not predict where control settles (r = 0.09). 2026-07-02 · SEC · CLII + MVEP · 4 recs 15. OCC AF55 BSA and sanctions (PPSI) The OCC's supervision-and-coordination layer over a PPSI AML and sanctions program works only when the OCC and FinCEN read the same measured control posture of the same operator, extended to reserve assets, entity types, and alignment with the parallel FDIC rule. Gaps: the effective-program disposition is attestation-shaped; nothing keys the OCC-to-FinCEN consultation to a shared control-posture record; the OCC and FDIC consultation thresholds diverge (assets against outstanding issuance value), inviting arbitrage. 2026-07-23 · OCC · MVEP + CLII · 8 recs 16. FinCEN AB74 PPSI customer identification program The account definition is doing two different jobs in the GENIUS Act framework, and this proposal codifies only one of them while disclaiming the other in a footnote; the disclaimer belongs in regulatory text, where it will bind. Gaps: a narrow codified account definition sits beside a codified lawful-order cross-reference with only footnote 57 between them; the population estimate counts on-chain counterparties while the obligation attaches to formal relationships; the government-list comparison obligation has no designated lists. 2026-08-21 · FinCEN (joint with OCC, Board, FDIC, NCUA) · Control-layer + on-chain measurement · 8 recs 17. FDIC AG21 resolution submissions The rule narrows what covered institutions must submit for resolution planning, and the content it removes is disproportionately the content that predicts a run. Gaps: uninsured-deposit concentration and key-depositor detail, the variables March 2023 showed to be decisive, would be deleted while coverage is keyed to asset size alone; the receivership perimeter does not identify tokenized-deposit or stablecoin-reserve-custody arrangements or the operational controls at that boundary. 2026-08-24 · FDIC · Run-risk measurement + control layer · 6 recs 18. FDIC AG27 assessments The cohort the assessment rule reclassifies carries measurably higher run risk than the cohort it is priced against, and the framework prices size and resolvability but not novel-activity exposure. Gaps: reclassified banks show higher uninsured and insured brokered deposit shares than the comparison cohort; no indicator reaches crypto-asset or stablecoin-reserve-custody exposure; a voluntary rate-incentivized readiness mechanism selects for the banks least likely to fail, so its loss-reduction rationale is weakest in the non-electing tail. 2026-08-24 · FDIC · Run-risk measurement · 4 recs

International standard-setter responses

A distinct series from the federal comment-letter program above: responses to international standard-setting bodies. The first extends the program's frameworks to AI infrastructure, and does not change the federal count.

FSB: Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of AI A response to all seven consultation questions, anchored by the first control-layer measurement of live agentic payments: a market where autonomous agents already execute irreversible payments through an unsupervised settlement layer no institution governs. Contribution: two uncovered risk areas (cross-operator composition; integrity of AI-adoption statistics), a measured nonbank case study, three practice additions, and two glossary refinements. 2026-07-01 · Financial Stability Board · MVEP-AI + CLII-AI · first international filing

Program timeline

Submission channels: six via regulations.gov; three via Comments@fdic.gov email (FDIC does not use regulations.gov or a website filing form for these dockets); one joint filing submitted concurrently at three agency dockets with mixed channels; one via the SEC internet comment form.

Program context

Six dockets opened simultaneously in April in the GENIUS Act implementation cycle: three AML rulemakings published April 10, 2026 (FinCEN 0034, FinCEN-OFAC 0100, banking joint 91 FR 18304), two FDIC PPSI rulemakings (AG20, AG19), the OCC PPSI rule already in comment period, and Treasury's state-similarity rulemaking. Filing as one coordinated program let the recommendations cross-reference: the OCC MVEP anchor (cited by five subsequent filings) seeded the diligence vocabulary; the OCC Supplemental introduced the control-layer frame; later letters extended both to new domains. The four AML comment periods closed June 9, 2026; on that deadline the program filed two supplements to the FinCEN dockets, adding first-party evidence to Recommendations 4 and 6. The NCUA published its own GENIUS implementing proposal on May 18, 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 28956); the program filed its principal NCUA comment and a same-day actionability supplement on June 22, 2026, completing coverage of every GENIUS-implementing agency. On June 30, 2026 the program filed its third FDIC comment, on the FDIC Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions compliance standards for PPSIs (RIN 3064-AG29, 91 Fed. Reg. 34171). On July 2, 2026, the comment-period close date, it filed its first SEC comment, on the Draft Strategic Plan for Fiscal Years 2026 through 2030 (File Number DSP-3), extending it from the GENIUS-implementing banking agencies to the securities regulator. On July 23, 2026 the program filed its third OCC comment, on the OCC Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions compliance standards for OCC-supervised PPSIs (RIN 1557-AF55, Docket OCC-2026-0463, 91 Fed. Reg. 37840), the OCC-side twin of the FDIC AG29 rule, bringing the program to its fifteenth letter, across ten dockets and seven agencies. On August 21, 2026, the closing day of that comment window, the program filed its fifth FinCEN comment, on the permitted payment stablecoin issuer customer identification program (RIN 1506-AB74, Docket FINCEN-2026-0101, 91 Fed. Reg. 37234), a rule FinCEN proposed jointly with the OCC, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the FDIC, and the NCUA, bringing the program to eighteen letters across thirteen dockets and eight federal agencies. The Board of Governors is the eighth: an agency counts as engaged once the program has filed in a proceeding to which it is a party, whether as the agency addressed or as a co-issuer of a joint rulemaking, and each agency counts once however many comments it later receives. That is the convention the ledger had already been applying to NCUA and OFAC, written down when this filing made it decide something. The GENIUS Act statutory rulemaking deadline of July 18, 2026 governs the PPSI implementing rules.

Framework deployment (thirteen principal letters). CLII applied in 11 of 13; MVEP in 9; control-layer five-element in 4. The two June FinCEN supplements both apply the control-layer frame, to effective compliance-officer control and to reserve-custody concentration respectively; the NCUA supplement is a routing-and-suggested-text supplement. The SEC strategic-plan letter adds the corrected concentration methodology from the 52-protocol governance audit (B2) as a third instrument class. Per Frameworks: CLII is the continuous-monitoring drift detector, MVEP is the entry gate, the control-layer framework is the structural spine both operate on.

Related research. The letter program is the applied-regulatory expression of the Track A papers: Routing the Dollar (CLII), MVEP, Seven Dollars (independence of the two frameworks), Three Tokenization Strategies. See also Overview: Federal Comment Letter Program.
