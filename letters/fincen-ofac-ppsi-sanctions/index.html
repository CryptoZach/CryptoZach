FinCEN-OFAC PPSI comment: the first statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation

FINCEN-2026-0100 (RIN 1506-AB73) · regulations.gov · Comment period: April 10, 2026 to June 9, 2026

The joint FinCEN-OFAC NPRM operationalizes the first statutory control-layer mandate in U.S. financial regulation. GENIUS Act §1033.240 requires Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers to have technical capability to block, freeze, and reject transactions and to comply with any lawful order, applied to both primary and secondary market activity. The letter engages 50 of 58 NPRM questions (86%), applies all three program frameworks, and proposes ten specific recommendations.

What the rule proposes

Under 31 U.S.C. 5312(a)(2)(Y), FinCEN designates PPSIs as financial institutions subject to the BSA. GENIUS Act proposed 31 CFR 1033.240 requires PPSIs to have technical capability to block, freeze, and reject transactions and to comply with any lawful order, applied to both primary and secondary market activity. Proposed §502.201(b) sets out a five-element sanctions compliance program (senior management commitment, risk assessment, internal controls, testing/auditing, training). Proposed §1010.100(ff)(8)(iii) removes PPSIs from the MSB definition, creating a clean partition: PPSIs under this rule; non-PPSI stablecoins remain under FINCEN-2026-0034's MSB framework.

Gaps identified

  • Technical capability cannot be presumed from existence of smart-contract functions. Without operational verification, block/freeze/reject obligations risk being paper-only.
  • "Account" in §1010.100(rrr) may be read to exclude wallet addresses. If so, lawful orders against specific wallets may fail for insufficient particularity, disabling the routing-layer capability the Act mandates.
  • SAR framework for secondary market is underspecified. Blanket secondary-market SAR obligation would be over-inclusive; no obligation under-inclusive.
  • Reserve custody relationships unaddressed in PPSI risk assessments. Concentration, jurisdiction, and operational risk need first-order treatment.
  • Sanctions compliance program integration with AML/CFT program needs structural clarification at both governance and operational levels.
  • Information sharing framework for interface operators is absent.
  • AML/CFT officer felony conviction prohibition scope needs clarification.
  • Effective date and phased implementation need calibration to operational-verification readiness.

Recommendations

  1. Control-layer function mapping in PPSI risk assessments. Operational topology as the unit of AML analysis.
  2. Operational verification of block/freeze/reject capability through independent testing for both primary and secondary market transactions. Preserve the NPRM's flexibility on technical methods; add a verification requirement.
  3. "Account" in §1010.100(rrr) must include wallet addresses. Structural necessity for lawful-order execution against routing-layer capability.
  4. Sanctions compliance program integration with AML/CFT program at governance and operational levels.
  5. Graduated secondary market SAR framework tied to 314(a) requests and executed block/freeze/reject actions, with explicit SAR-to-capability interaction rules.
  6. Reserve custody relationships in PPSI risk assessments: concentration, jurisdictional, operational risk.
  7. AML/CFT officer felony conviction prohibition scope clarification.
  8. Information sharing framework for interface operators: the PPSI channel for interface-layer engagement.
  9. Coordination with FINCEN-2026-0034 and 91 FR 18304 (three-rulemaking coordination; FRB non-participation gap).
  10. Effective date and phased implementation calibrated to operational-verification readiness.

MVEP mapping preamble (Questions 8-19). Five MVEP categories map to specific provisions of proposed §1033.210 and related sections: Rights Parity, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture, Settlement Finality, Governance and Change Control.

Program role

Most framework-intensive letter in the program. All three program frameworks applied (CLII, MVEP, control-layer five-element). The "first statutory control-layer mandate" framing makes this letter the apex of the program's five federal-applications control-layer synthesis. Engages 50 of 58 NPRM questions (86% coverage).
