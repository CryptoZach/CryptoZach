FinCEN AB74 comment: codify the account boundary the footnote states, and count the population the rule actually reaches

RIN 1506-AB74, Docket FINCEN-2026-0101 (91 Fed. Reg. 37234, June 22, 2026) · Filed at regulations.gov, Comment Tracking Number mt3-sunw-w2rq · Comment period: June 22, 2026 to August 21, 2026

The account definition is doing two different jobs in the GENIUS Act framework, and this proposal codifies only one of them while disclaiming the other in a footnote. Proposed 31 CFR 1033.100(a) defines an account for customer identification purposes as a formal relationship, expressly excluding bare ownership or control of an issuer's stablecoins. Separately, the GENIUS Act conditions the lawful-order obligation at 12 U.S.C. 5903(a)(6)(B) on a definition of lawful order that is itself framed in terms of accounts at 12 U.S.C. 5901(16)(B). Footnote 57 says the customer identification definition is not meant to reach that obligation, and it says so nowhere in the regulatory text, while proposed Section 1033.220(c) codifies the cross-reference without carrying the disclaimer with it. If the narrow definition is read into the preserved obligation, a blocking order directed at a wallet address would be directed at something the regulation says is not an account. Eight recommendations address that boundary, the object the population estimate measures, and how identity is verified.

What the rule proposes

The AB74 rule establishes a customer identification program for permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs). It is a joint proposal: FinCEN issued it with the OCC, the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, the FDIC, and the NCUA, so one rule carries five agency identifier sets (FinCEN Docket FINCEN-2026-0101 and RIN 1506-AB74; OCC Docket OCC-2026-0331 and RIN 1557-AF53; Board Docket R-1885 and RIN 7100-AH18; FDIC RIN 3064-AG28; NCUA Docket NCUA-2026-0793 and RIN 3133-AG09). The rule works through three proposed definitions at 31 CFR 1033.100 (account, customer, and digital asset service provider) and a customer identification program at proposed 31 CFR 1033.220. Where the companion rulemaking at 91 Fed. Reg. 18582 sets what a PPSI's anti-money-laundering and sanctions programs must contain, this one sets something narrower and prior to it: who the issuer must identify, and what relationship triggers the duty to identify them. Every other obligation in the PPSI framework that operates on a customer or an account inherits the boundary these definitions draw.

The problem identified

Three problems, each an instance of the same concern with what is observable and what is merely asserted. First, the account boundary: the disclaimer that keeps the customer identification definition out of the lawful-order obligation lives in a preamble footnote, while both the narrow definition and the lawful-order cross-reference live in codified text. Second, the measured population: the regulatory impact analysis derives its affected-population estimate from on-chain minting and redemption activity, while the rule's obligation attaches to contractually formalized relationships. Those are different objects, and independent measurement of the contractual perimeter finds counts three orders of magnitude below the analysis mean. Third, verification and screening: the rule permits reliance on digital identity credentials without saying what makes one trustworthy (a signature proves who issued a credential, not what that issuer checked), and it imposes a government-list comparison obligation for lists that have not yet been designated.

Recommendations

  1. Codify the account-definition boundary (Questions 2 and 3; proposed Sections 1033.100(a) and 1033.220(c)). Move the footnote 57 disclaimer into regulatory text, so the customer identification definition of account cannot be read into the lawful-order obligation that Section 1033.220(c) preserves. Carries forward the position already on the record at FINCEN-2026-0100 (wallet address as account for lawful orders) by citation rather than re-argument. The signature multi-docket contribution of a commenter present on both dockets.
  2. Observable hallmarks of a formal relationship (Questions 2 and 3; proposed Section 1033.100(a)(1)). Retain "formal relationship" but state its hallmarks as observable attributes, stated non-exclusively: a contractual right of redemption against the issuer, an onboarding record, membership on an authorized-participant or minter list, a custody agreement. Reconcile with the mutual fund CIP "contractual or other business relationship" formulation that the proposal's own footnote 59 flags. A relationship test that turns on the issuer's characterization of it is not examinable.
  3. Conform the preamble to the codified account text (Question 2; proposed Section 1033.100(a)(1)(v)). The preamble at 91 Fed. Reg. 37239 describes subparagraph (a)(1)(v) with three qualifiers; the codified text at 91 Fed. Reg. 37270 reads bare, "Providing services of a digital asset service provider." Resolve in favor of the codified text and correct the preamble: the qualified reading would relieve an issuer of customer identification duties precisely where its activities were unauthorized.
  4. Redemption-only relationships (Question 4; proposed Sections 1033.100(b)(2)(iv) and 1033.220(a)(2)(ii)). Confirm that a redemption-triggered account may be verified within a reasonable period after opening, so the rule does not operate as a gate on the one exit path that leads to par, with risk-based pre-completion verification preserved for unusual size or pattern.
  5. The population estimate measures a different object than the rule reaches (Additional Questions 1, 4, 8, and 9; Question 1). State which object the population estimate measures, and re-derive the affected-population and burden estimates on the account-holder object the rule defines. The program's strongest empirical fit to an explicit agency data request: the analysis counts on-chain counterparties, the obligation attaches to formal relationships, and the two have been measured separately.
  6. Reliance asymmetry, and the threshold that determines which side an issuer sits on (Question 7; Additional Questions 5 and 12; proposed Section 1033.220(a)(6)). Address the asymmetry the preamble itself identifies, under which a state qualified issuer may rely on a federally regulated PPSI but not the reverse, and reconcile the measurement basis of the ten-billion-dollar threshold that determines an issuer's position. The same numeral is now measured on a third basis (consolidated total outstanding issuance at 12 U.S.C. 5903(c)) against 10 billion dollars in assets at the OCC (AF55) and 10 billion dollars in outstanding issuance value at the FDIC (AG29).
  7. Digital identity and verifiable credentials (Questions 5, 6, and 8; proposed Section 1033.220(a)(2)(ii)). Do not name digital identity tools in regulatory text. State the four observable properties by which a credential supports a reasonable belief: the evidence and assurance level the credential issuer collected, the binding of the credential to the presenter, status and revocation checkability at the time of reliance, and how failures and indeterminate results are handled.
  8. List comparison with no designated lists (Question 8; proposed Section 1033.220(a)(4)). The government-list comparison obligation currently has no designated lists. State the designation mechanism and the notification timing, and confirm whether existing OFAC sanctions screening discharges the obligation in whole, in part, or not at all. An obligation whose object does not yet exist cannot be examined for effectiveness, and cannot be costed.

Question coverage. All eight questions in Section VI are addressed, along with six of the sixteen Additional Requests for Comment in Section VIII.I (AQ1, AQ4, AQ5, AQ8, AQ9, AQ12). The remaining ten are deliberately not commented on for lack of program evidence, and the letter says which and why rather than filling the space.

A note on scope and evidence

Where the comment draws on the program's empirical work, that work studies stablecoin issuers and decentralized-finance protocols as they operate today, not permitted payment stablecoin issuers, a category that has no occupants yet. Findings are offered to illustrate a measurement method and to supply data the Agencies expressly requested, not as claims about any future PPSI or as allegations about any named entity, and the letter flags the qualification at each point of use. Two externally sourced figures recur (882 verified redemption accounts at Tether and 1,834 Circle Mint accounts); both come from published legal scholarship, the Circle figure carries an as-of date of March 2025 in that source, and both are flagged with those provenance limits wherever they appear.

Program role

AB74 is the program's fifth FinCEN comment, after the general anti-money-laundering rulemaking (FINCEN-2026-0034) and the joint FinCEN-OFAC PPSI program rulemaking (FINCEN-2026-0100) and their June 9 supplements. It is the program's sixteenth federal comment letter, and the filing that made the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System the eighth federal agency the program has engaged. The Board is a party to this joint rulemaking (Docket No. R-1885, RIN 7100-AH18) without being the agency this comment is addressed to, which is the case that forced the counting rule to be written down: an agency is engaged once the program files in a proceeding it is party to, as addressee or as co-issuer, and counts once thereafter. Filed August 21, 2026 at regulations.gov on Docket FINCEN-2026-0101, Comment Tracking Number mt3-sunw-w2rq, on the closing day of the comment window. Because the rule is joint, the recommendations are written to coordinate with the sibling dockets rather than to duplicate them, and the adjacency traps are worth naming: FINCEN-2026-0101 is not FINCEN-2026-0100, FDIC RIN 3064-AG28 here is not AG29, OCC RIN 1557-AF53 is not AF55, and Board Docket R-1885 is not R-1835.
