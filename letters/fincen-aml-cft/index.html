FinCEN AML/CFT NPRM comment: AML regulation as structurally a control-layer regime

FINCEN-2026-0034 (RIN 1506-AB72) · regulations.gov · Comment period: April 10, 2026 to June 9, 2026

AML regulation has always been control-layer regulation: the Bank Secrecy Act attaches obligations to intermediaries, not to transactions or instruments. The FinCEN NPRM is the first structural AML reform since the AML Act of 2020. In tokenized finance, intermediation can be disaggregated across several layers (issuer, custodian, interface, router, sweep, hold and freeze). The letter argues the proposed rule's risk-based resource allocation directive is structurally compatible with control-layer-level risk assessment, and will be more effective if it articulates this compatibility explicitly.

What the rule proposes

The FinCEN NPRM reforms AML/CFT programs across eleven financial-institution categories (banks, casinos, MSBs, broker-dealers, mutual funds, insurance, FCMs and IBCs, DPMSJs, credit card system operators, loan and finance companies, housing GSEs) in the first structural reform since the AML Act of 2020. It operationalizes the Act's risk-based, effectiveness-focused supervision directive, requires a US-based AML/CFT officer (31 U.S.C. 5318(h)(5)), mandates consideration of the AML/CFT Priorities, and introduces a risk assessment process requirement. The rule does not yet address PPSIs as a distinct category; PPSI-specific AML obligations live in FINCEN-2026-0100.

Gaps identified

  • Risk assessment is instrument-class-scoped, not control-layer-scoped. The "business activities" requirement permits but does not require financial institutions to evaluate at control-layer granularity (issuer, custodian, interface, router, sweep, hold-and-freeze).
  • No disqualifying-activities analog for tokenized finance operators. The SEC Division of Trading and Markets' April 13 CUIP statement articulates twelve conditions for securities interfaces; no parallel exists for the broader set of tokenized-finance operators.
  • US-based AML/CFT officer requirement lacks tokenized-finance specification. Much technical infrastructure operates across borders; the officer's supervisory control over US-nexus operations needs clarification.
  • AML/CFT Priorities do not yet address PPSI-related risks. Custodial concentration channel (Du/Sonawane/Watsky FEDS Note); financial stability considerations (Carapella/Lubis/Vardoulakis FEDS Note).
  • Federal Reserve Board non-participation in the parallel banking-agency NPRM creates a coordination gap. Three rulemakings; one agency absent.
  • Independent testing for control-layer operators needs calibration. Testing must evaluate demonstrated on-chain capability, not inferred capability.

Fixes proposed

  1. Clarify that risk assessment processes for tokenized finance must evaluate control-layer exposure. Business activities should explicitly include the control-layer functions the institution performs.
  2. Articulate a disqualifying-activities analog for tokenized-finance operators, paralleling the SEC CUIP twelve conditions.
  3. Integrate PPSI-related risks into the AML/CFT Priorities update, including the custodial concentration channel and financial-stability considerations.
  4. Preserve and clarify the US-based AML/CFT officer requirement for tokenized finance, ensuring effective supervisory control over US-nexus operations.
  5. Calibrate independent testing requirements for control-layer verification: on-chain activities, enforcement capability functionality, identity verification coverage across all control-layer functions.
  6. Apply the supervision and enforcement consultation framework beyond banks, with priority for large MSBs operating in tokenized finance.
  7. Coordinate the final rule with FINCEN-2026-0100 and 91 FR 18304, with specific attention to the FRB non-participation coordination gap.

Program role

First of three AML-batch letters filed April 18, 2026. Sets out the structural control-layer observation across eleven FI categories before the PPSI-specific FINCEN-2026-0100 letter applies all three frameworks in depth.
