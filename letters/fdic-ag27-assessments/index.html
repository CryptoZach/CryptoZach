FDIC AG27 comment: price the run risk the assessment framework is about to stop pricing

RIN 3064-AG27 (91 Fed. Reg. 39794, June 30, 2026) · Comments@fdic.gov email and the Federal eRulemaking Portal · Comment period: June 30, 2026 to August 31, 2026

The AG27 rule reprices deposit insurance assessments, and the cohort it reclassifies carries measurably higher run risk than the cohort it is priced against. Reclassified banks show higher uninsured (34.8 percent versus 32.6 percent) and insured brokered (8.3 percent versus 6.1 percent) deposit shares than the $1 billion to $10 billion institutions Table 1 compares them to. The framework also prices size and resolvability but not crypto-asset, stablecoin-reserve-custody, or novel-activity exposure, the channel that transmitted a failure across sectors in March 2023. Four recommendations address both, and ask that the Resolution Readiness Adjustment be calibrated jointly with the companion resolution proposal rather than separately.

Recommendations

  1. A run-risk factor for the reclassified cohort (Questions 1 and 2; the $10 billion to $30 billion threshold and Table 1). Retain a run-risk-sensitive factor for reclassified banks, or condition the reclassification on a measured uninsured-deposit-concentration or rapid-growth trigger.
  2. A novel-activity and concentration risk indicator (Question 12; Section IX). Add, or solicit comment on adding, a novel-activity and concentration risk indicator to the assessment framework, so the pricing reaches the exposure channel March 2023 demonstrated.
  3. A Resolution Readiness Adjustment keyed to a current demonstration (Questions 13, 17, 18 and 25; proposed Section 327.18). Preserve the operational test the proposed text already defines, state the sufficiency standard, reconcile the seven-year data-access reengagement interval with the three-year retest, calibrate the adjustment jointly with the companion AG21 resolution proposal, and support observable partial credit.
  4. Reserve-ratio slippage and the distribution of relief (Question 12; material economic effects). Weigh the reductions' effect on the fund's recovery and their distribution against the fairness rationale, and state the reasoning: the roughly $4.0 billion per year reduction delays the fund reaching its 2 percent designated reserve ratio to 2035.

The problem identified

Assessment pricing should track the risks that actually drive loss to the fund. Two are mispriced under the proposal. First, the reclassified cohort's run-risk profile is above the cohort it is being priced against, on the FDIC's own Table 1 data. Second, the framework contains no indicator for novel-activity or crypto-asset and stablecoin-reserve-custody exposure, which is the specific channel that transmitted a bank failure across sectors in March 2023. A third issue is structural rather than a mispricing: a voluntary, rate-incentivized readiness mechanism selects for the banks least likely to fail, so its loss-reduction rationale is weakest exactly in the non-electing tail where readiness matters most.

What the rule proposes

The AG27 rule revises the FDIC's deposit insurance assessment thresholds, rate schedules, and adjustments, and introduces a Resolution Readiness Adjustment at proposed 12 CFR 327.18 that reduces assessments for institutions demonstrating resolution readiness. It was published the same day as the companion resolution-submissions proposal (RIN 3064-AG21, 91 Fed. Reg. 39546), and the Resolution Readiness Adjustment depends on evidence that rule governs.

Program role

AG27 is the program's fifth FDIC comment and the companion to its comment on the resolution-submissions proposal (AG21), filed the same day on the same FDIC rulemaking issue. Prior FDIC comments addressed the prudential activities rule (AG19), the application-process rule (AG20), and the BSA and sanctions compliance rule (AG29). It is the program's eighteenth federal comment. Filed August 24, 2026 by email to Comments@fdic.gov with RIN 3064-AG27 in the subject line, seven days before the August 31 deadline. Anchored in the FDIC's own Options for Deposit Insurance Reform, the GAO March 2023 review, and the bank cross-section literature.
