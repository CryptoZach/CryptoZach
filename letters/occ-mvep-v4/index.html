OCC PPSI MVEP comment: applying the Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack to the Permissible Payment Stablecoin Issuer rule

3 min read·March 2026·Zach Zukowski

OCC-2025-0372-0057 (RIN 1557-AF41) · regulations.gov · Comment period: March 11, 2026 to May 10, 2026

Three tokenized U.S. Treasury products under three regulatory regimes receive comparable collateral treatment on Binance, through Ceffu, with no counterparty disclosure about which instrument backs any given trade. The OCC's PPSI rule regulates the issuer, not the collateral pools in which PPSI-issued stablecoins circulate. This letter applies the Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack (MVEP) ten-category diligence framework to the proposed rule and identifies three implementable fixes.

Framework: MVEP Paper: MVEP (A2)

What the rule proposes

The OCC's PPSI NPRM implements the GENIUS Act for payment-stablecoin issuers under OCC jurisdiction. Its headline provisions: reserve composition must be cash, demand deposits, and short-term Treasuries at federally insured institutions; federal supervision extends to PPSI issuers currently regulated as state money transmitters; three PPSI categories are differentiated (Federal qualified, State qualified with joint supervision above $10 billion, and IDI subsidiary). The rule targets the issuer, not the interfaces or the collateral pools in which PPSI-issued stablecoins circulate.

Gaps identified

  • Collateral interoperability is unregulated. Three tokenized Treasury products under three regimes (U.S. mutual fund law, BVI partnership law, Cayman-Bermuda offshore fund law) receive comparable collateral treatment on Binance through Ceffu with no counterparty disclosure. No regulator supervises the seam where instruments are combined.
  • Legal defects in holder rights are undisclosed. The UCC Article 12 qualifying-purchaser defect (thieves cannot create an interest in property, so take-free protection fails) is documented, acknowledged, and intentionally unrepaired. No issuer discloses it.
  • Owner-vs-controller ambiguity for custodial wallets. The GENIUS Act defines payment stablecoins by the issuer's obligation to redeem but does not specify whether that obligation runs to the owner of the token or the person who controls it.
  • MSB capital framework is structurally insufficient. MSB minimum capital for a $40 billion issuer is approximately $216 million (0.54%); banking-law capital for $40 billion in deposits is approximately $4.8 billion (12%). A 22-to-1 gap, with no stress testing, no deposit insurance, and no resolution framework.
  • Super-priority risks administratively insolvent debtors. Holder super-priority under GENIUS Act §11 ranks ahead of trustee fees, potentially making reorganization infeasible and trapping the holders the Act was designed to protect.

Fixes proposed

  1. Require PPSIs to publish a holder-rights analysis under applicable commercial law, specifically addressing the UCC Article 12 qualifying-purchaser defect and the owner-vs-controller ambiguity, with compensating measures disclosed (insurance, make-whole commitments, recovery procedures).
  2. Coordinate interagency on the collateral eligibility boundary. The OCC should coordinate with the SEC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, FinCEN, and Treasury on whether tokenized instruments from different regulatory regimes should receive equivalent collateral treatment without a standardized equivalence assessment.
  3. Adopt a standardized equivalence assessment as a supervisory tool. MVEP or a comparable framework, producing reproducible assessments that distinguish instruments with different risk profiles. A quarterly or semiannual update cadence reviewed by OCC examiners during regular supervision; estimated cost $5,000 to $15,000 annually per PPSI.
