Comment to the OCC on its GENIUS Act application forms: ask, on the form, for the facts the deemed-approval periods turn on

Docket summary

The OCC's notice of October 23, 2026 submitted to the Office of Management and Budget the two forms an applicant uses under the OCC's GENIUS Act final rule: the License Application, for a permitted payment stablecoin issuer the OCC would supervise, and the Registration Application, for a foreign issuer seeking access to the United States market. Under the final rule an applicant "must submit all the information required by the form," a substantially complete license application is deemed approved on the 120th day after the OCC receives it unless the OCC denies it first, and a registration is deemed approved on the 30th day. The notice answered the two comments on its 60-day notice and declined to change the forms for issuance by several affiliates. Comments were due to the OCC by November 23, 2026, and to reginfo.gov within 30 days of publication.

Where this letter fits

Filed the same day as the program's comment on the OCC's GENIUS Act reporting forms, after its comments on the OCC's proposed rule, with a supplement, and on the FDIC's application rule. The function-by-entity table it asks of the OCC's forms is the structure the program recommended to the FDIC for that rule. The reporting forms comment asks the public quarterly report to name each institution holding more than 5 percent of reserves; this comment asks the application to name the reserve institutions and their planned shares before issuance begins.

Under the OCC's final rule the application form sets what an application must contain, and a substantially complete license application is deemed approved on the 120th day after the OCC receives it, a registration on the 30th, so what the forms ask largely decides what the OCC holds when those periods begin to run. The forms list only the functions an applicant outsources or receives from affiliates, ask for lawful-order policies but not the capability the Act makes a condition of issuance, and ask how reserve concentration is managed but never where reserves will sit. Five of the six recommendations ask for facts the Act, the rule or a review factor already turns on: who performs each function, whether the issuer can carry out a lawful order on each blockchain, where its reserves sit and in what shares, how its customers redeem in a surge, and, for a foreign issuer, what its United States operations are. The sixth corrects the forms and the burden estimate.

Framework: Form-to-rule mapping (each review factor and statutory condition set against the form item that collects it) Paper: Seven Dollars Evidence: the notice and its supporting statement, the two posted forms, the OCC's final rule and Bulletin 2026-51, Federal Reserve Board staff's analysis of the March 2023 USDC run, and an October 2025 on-chain overmint

Recommendations

  1. Map each function to the entity that performs it. Add one table to both forms naming who performs issuance and burning, redemption and onboarding, reserve management, reserve deposits and custody, custody of customers' stablecoins, administration of the token on each blockchain, Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions compliance, and the execution of lawful orders, with each entity's location and affiliation. The forms now list only functions outsourced to third parties or provided by affiliates.
  2. Ask whether the applicant can comply with a lawful order. Add an item to both forms asking, for each blockchain on which the stablecoin is or will be issued, the token's contract address, which of the Act's seize, freeze, burn, prevent-transfer, block and reject actions the contract supports, who executes each and with what approvals, the expected time from an order to execution, and any network on which an action is not available. The Act allows issuance only if the issuer has the technological capability to comply with lawful orders, and the forms ask only for policies.
  3. Name the reserve institutions and their planned shares. Ask, in Reserve Assets, for each institution expected to hold reserve deposits or reserve assets in custody when issuance begins, naming any fund's custodian and any affiliation, the planned share at each, and how the applicant measures the risk that one or a few of them cannot return reserves. Ask a foreign issuer which of the rule's four ways of showing that its U.S. reserves suffice it relies on, the U.S. institutions, and the share of its stablecoins held in the United States.
  4. Reach all of the redemption rule. Cite the whole redemption rule and ask how onboarding procedures avoid undue delay in times of stress, with the applicant's expected daily capacity to onboard customers and process redemption orders, and how it will detect redemption requests above the 10 percent threshold and give the 24-hour notice. Ask a foreign issuer how a customer in the United States redeems: through which entity, paid from which U.S. institution, and in what time.
  5. Give the registration factors their items. Add Registration Application items for the United States operations and their resources, the illicit finance risk the issuer presents to the United States and the controls that address it, and risk management under the home-country regime; move the test of whether the issuer's home country is under comprehensive U.S. sanctions from Reserve Assets to Eligibility; and ask for the country of domicile and regulation. Three of the six registration review factors have no item, and a registration is deemed approved 30 days after the OCC receives it.
  6. Correct the forms and the burden estimate. Cite the rule's risk management standard, not the OCC's general rule on licensing decisions, in the risk management item; name the Corporate Background and Financial Report the rule requires; place in this collection anything the unpublished GENIUS Act manual will require; use one name for the filing system; state the basis for the 125 hours per license application or revise it against the OCC's own 250 hours for charter documents; reconcile the supporting statement's 40 hours per registration with the form's own 125; and count the biographical reports here or in the OCC's Licensing Manual collection.

The problem identified

Under the final rule the form sets what an application must contain: an applicant "must submit all the information required by the form," and the 120-day and 30-day deemed-approval periods turn on a filing that contains it. The OCC also "generally expects to grant requests for waivers" from the Prior Approval Requirement for existing issuers with a substantially complete application pending, so the forms decide what the OCC holds while those issuers keep issuing. Yet the forms list only the functions an applicant outsources or receives from an affiliate, never who performs the rest. They ask for Bank Secrecy Act, sanctions and technology policies, never whether the token's contract on each blockchain can carry out a lawful order, which the Act makes a condition of issuance, though the notice says an issuer "may issue the same brand across multiple blockchains without a separate application."

Reserves and redemption have the same gap. The rule requires an issuer to measure and manage the risk of concentrating reserves at one or a few institutions; the License Application asks how that duty is met, never where the reserves will sit or how much at each. In March 2023, Federal Reserve Board staff report, significant USDC redemptions followed the receivership of Silicon Valley Bank, which held about 8 percent of USDC's reserves, even before Circle's announcement that it could not withdraw them. The license form cites three paragraphs of the redemption rule and skips the two on onboarding in times of stress and the 24-hour notice; the registration form's whole redemption item is one line, and three of the six registration review factors have no item at all.

The burden estimate gives each license application 125 hours, half the 250 hours the OCC estimates for charter documents in its Licensing Manual collection, and neither the notice nor the supporting statement gives a basis for the figure. The supporting statement's 40 hours per registration differs from the registration form's own statement of 125.

A note on scope and evidence

The comment treats the forms as a sound base and asks the OCC to add to them, not replace them. None of its requests adds a standard: where a request asks for a figure, such as the time to carry out an order or a daily capacity to onboard customers, the figure is the applicant's own description of its operations, offered for review, not a proposed limit, and an applicant may ask for confidential treatment under the forms' existing procedure. Each request can be met within this collection, by adding an item to the posted forms or correcting the supporting statement, without any change to the rule. The October 2025 overmint the comment cites, 300 trillion tokens minted and burned 22 minutes later, is offered to show how the control works, not as a finding about the issuer, which the comment does not name; USDC was not a payment stablecoin issued under the Act.
