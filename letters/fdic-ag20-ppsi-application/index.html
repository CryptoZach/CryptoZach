FDIC AG20 comment: the application as the FDIC's first control-layer topology map

3 min read·April 2026·Zach Zukowski

RIN 3064-AG20 (90 Fed. Reg. 59409) · Comments@fdic.gov email (FDIC does not accept this docket via regulations.gov or a website filing form)

The FDIC AG20 application rule is the FDIC's first and foundational opportunity to map the control-layer topology of a prospective Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuer. Filing content shapes both the approval decision and the baseline for ongoing supervision. Four recommendations restructure §303.252 filing content to surface structural features that are consequential for both.

What the rule proposes

The FDIC AG20 application rule implements GENIUS Act Section 5, establishing the procedural pathway for an insured depository institution to obtain FDIC approval for a subsidiary PPSI. Proposed §303.252 specifies filing content: description of proposed activities, reserve assets and composition plan, customer agreements, BSA/AML/OFAC compliance, and related materials. The information the FDIC collects at the application stage becomes both the approval-decision basis and the baseline for ongoing supervision under Section 4 and Section 6.

Gaps identified

  • §303.252(d)(1) activity description is underspecified. Requires "identities, roles, and responsibilities of entities involved" but no structured mapping to GENIUS Act Section 4 activity categories.
  • Reserve composition is tracked; custody concentration is not. §303.252(d)(2) requires a composition plan but nothing on where reserves are held. The SVB episode showed concentration as the transmission channel.
  • Customer agreements omit rights-parity disclosure. §303.252(d)(4) requires agreements but nothing ensures they characterize the legal nature of the holder's claim, especially the PPSI-vs-IDI distinction where holders may assume deposit-like protection.
  • Enterprise-wide AML/CFT program integration is not expressly evaluated. A subsidiary PPSI's AML program (under FINCEN-2026-0100) and parent IDI's program (under 91 FR 18304) integrate at the FDIC supervisory layer, but §303.252(d)(4) does not require the applicant to describe how.
  • §5(c)(5) factors deferred without flexibility preservation. Rule declines to add factors now but does not preserve flexibility to add them as substantive rulemakings surface relevant considerations.

Fixes proposed

  1. Structured section-4-activity mapping under §303.252(d)(1): two-axis matrix of (a) activity categories 4(a)(7)(A)(i) through 4(a)(7)(B) against (b) performing entity (PPSI, parent IDI, affiliate, third-party, combined). Coordinates with parallel FinCEN 0100 topology mapping.
  2. Preserve §5(c)(5) flexibility. Final rule preamble should state that declining to add factors now does not foreclose later additions. Four candidate considerations for future factors: control-layer observability; smart contract immutability; public blockchain dependencies; reserve custody concentration beyond composition.
  3. Expand §303.252(d)(2) asset management plan to include named custodians, per-institution concentration limits as percentage of total reserves, diversification rationale with single-institution failure analysis, and specific treatment of parent-IDI-as-custodian concentration.
  4. Customer agreements must include a rights-parity assessment under §303.252(d)(4): (a) contractual-claim-not-deposit-claim characterization; (b) redemption limit circumstances including UCC Article 12 qualifying-purchaser scenarios; (c) Article 12 asymmetric-effects disclosure; (d) PPSI-IDI solvency relationship disclosure.

Structural observation (Q11). §303.252(d)(4) should signal that the FDIC will evaluate enterprise-wide AML/CFT program integration between the subsidiary PPSI (under FINCEN-2026-0100) and the parent IDI (under 91 FR 18304): governance, information sharing, unified testing, enterprise-wide risk assessment.
