Treasury GENIUS Act state-similarity comment: six fixes for the substantial-similarity assessment

TREAS-DO-2026-0232 (RIN 1505-AC90; 91 FR 16844) · regulations.gov

Fifty state regulatory frameworks need to be evaluated as substantially similar to the federal GENIUS Act framework across dozens of specific requirements. The proposed principles frame substantial similarity as section-by-section assessment against statutory text. This letter proposes a functional-dimension diagnostic framework (MVEP) and six specific fixes covering reserve transparency, capital adequacy, activity scope, insolvency, and interface-layer conduct.

What the rule proposes

Treasury's proposed §1521 principles frame the evaluation of whether a state regulatory regime is "substantially similar" to the federal GENIUS Act framework, enabling state-qualified PPSIs to operate under state regimes that "meet or exceed" the federal baseline. Proposed §1521.2(b)(1) requires that implementation of each state-calibrated requirement "leads to regulatory outcomes that are at least as stringent and protective as the Federal regulatory framework." §1521.5 addresses custody and insolvency separately from §1521.3's reserve and capital standards. §1521.6 permits states to impose additional requirements beyond the federal framework.

Gaps identified

  • Section-by-section assessment misses cross-cutting weaknesses. Holder-protection failures cluster: rights parity, insolvency posture, and settlement finality fail together because they trace to the same weak legal claim on reserves.
  • Statutory text alone is the wrong baseline. The most consequential gaps arise from implementation choices within statutory authorization (Circle restricting direct redemption to 1,834 accounts; BUIDL's USDC-dependent redemption; Binance collateral interoperability), not from statutory text.
  • Custody and insolvency under §1521.5's weaker standard. Both are among the three most consequential holder-protection dimensions; evaluating them under a general-consistency standard while reserves get "consistent in all substantive respects" leaves the wrong gap open.
  • Two-business-day redemption standard is pathway-blind. Circle suspended USDC primary-market redemptions for 64 hours in March 2023; the standard assumes the pathway is functional.
  • Some federal protections are structurally unreplicable at the state level. Bankruptcy priority (Section 507 amended by GENIUS §11); UCC Article 12 take-free variance by state; OCC examination authority over nationally chartered custodians.
  • Activities that functionally defeat the Act. Section 4(a)(7)(B) permits state discretion that could license activity mixes producing holder-protection outcomes inconsistent with the Act's intent.

Fixes proposed

  1. Adopt a standardized diagnostic framework (MVEP or comparable) for the substantial-similarity assessment, structured around functional holder-protection dimensions rather than statutory sections.
  2. Incorporate the three-category reserve disclosure (legal claim, composition, location/custody) as uniform cross-state data fields for Stablecoin Certification Review Committee comparability.
  3. Require disclosure of redemption pathway dependencies. A regime meeting two-business-day redemption but not disclosing when the sole redemption route depends on another tokenized instrument (as BUIDL depends on USDC) leaves holders exposed.
  4. Permit states to narrow but not expand permitted reserve assets. Narrowing strengthens holder protection; expanding enables arbitrage.
  5. Operational backstop should include custodial diversification. The SVB episode indicates concentration, not operating-capital inadequacy, as the transmission channel.
  6. Explicitly recognize interface-layer conduct standards under §1521.6 as permissible and encouraged additional state requirements, preserved from future federal preemption under section 7(f)(4) consumer-protection carve-out.

Program role
