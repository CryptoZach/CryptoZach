October 19, 2026 7 recommendations

SEC File No. 4-927 comment: collect from day one the facts tokenized NMS stock rulemaking will need, in a form checkable against the public ledger

Docket summary

The SEC's exemptive order of September 17, 2026 (Release No. 34-106402) grants two temporary, conditional exemptions under Exchange Act section 36(a)(1) that run until September 17, 2031. The TSV Exemption relieves a Tokenized Securities Venue, which trades Tokenized NMS Stock through AMM Liquidity Pools against a non-security crypto asset such as a stablecoin or against a tokenized money market fund, from the definition of "exchange" in section 3(a)(1). Its conditions include that the TSV's smart contracts be auditable, public and deployed on a public, permissionless distributed ledger; that it verify each Tokenized NMS Stock gives holders the same rights and privileges as traditional NMS stock of an equivalent class; that it describe its arrangements item by item in a Notice; that it publish U.S. dollar transaction data within ten minutes; and that its trading, aggregated with that of affiliated TSVs, stay within two tiers of volume and symbol limits. The Covered Firm Exemption relieves certain liquidity providers from the definition of "dealer" in section 3(a)(5) while the Commission evaluates the scope of that term more broadly. The Order requests comment on all aspects of the exemptions, poses ten numbered questions and sets no comment deadline. The SEC's press materials call it the Innovation Exemption; the Order itself uses the names TSV Exemption and Covered Firm Exemption.

Macro context

The program's third comment to the SEC, after its July 2, 2026 comment on the Draft Strategic Plan (File Number DSP-3) and its comment on proposed Regulation Crypto Assets (File No. S7-2026-27). Recommendation 2 applies the DSP-3 comment's recommendation of reproducible on-chain measurement of control, through an inventory of privileged functions, and Recommendation 6 is consistent with the effective-control positions the program has taken before FinCEN and the FDIC. The comment also refers, without restating them, to the program's comments to the OCC on Docket OCC-2025-0372 and to the CFTC on Docket CFTC-2026-1685.

Several of the Order's conditions turn on facts the public ledger already makes observable, but the Order collects those facts as descriptions of procedure, collects some of them only for a Tokenized Securities Venue's (TSV's) own smart contracts rather than for the assets traded in its pools, and does not collect others at all. The Order is designed to produce evidence for rulemaking over a five-year term, so the comment asks it to collect, from its first day, the facts that rulemaking will need, in a form a reviewer can check against the ledger rather than accept on representation. The Order's own condition that a TSV's smart contracts be auditable, public and deployed on a public, permissionless ledger is what makes the seven recommendations inexpensive: most of the facts they ask for are already public on that ledger.

Recommendations

  1. Verify equivalence through the custody chain and the holder's position if the tokenizer fails (Section II.E; Notice items (h), (i) and (cc); Questions 1, 3 and 5). State that the Section II.E verification, and the Notice item (i) description of it, must address five things: the tokenization model; which record controls if the ledger and the off-chain record disagree, and how often token supply is reconciled to the shares held for holders; the holder's position if the tokenizer, custodian or any other intermediary fails; how corporate actions are applied to the token, and by whom; and who may convert the token into the share and back, and on what terms. The four rights the Order names run against the issuer, while the Commission's staff has identified a further exposure that none of them reaches, to the third party itself, "such as bankruptcy." Under item (cc), the TSV should name which of three approaches to corporate actions under around-the-clock trading it uses (halting trading during corporate-action processing windows, treating the ledger as the authoritative record, or synchronizing two records), when an ex-date adjustment takes effect on the token, and how a dispute about it is resolved.
  2. Extend the change-control disclosure from the TSV's own contracts to every asset in its pools (Section II.A; Notice items (h), (n) and (dd); Questions 1, 4 and 5). Add a Notice item listing, for each Tokenized NMS Stock and each paired asset, every function by which any person can mint, burn, freeze, block, force the transfer of, pause, restrict the transfer of, or upgrade or replace the code governing the asset; the address at which it resides; who can exercise it; and whether its exercise requires approval by more than one person or is subject to a delay, revised within five business days of any change. Treat an exercise as a significant operational event under Section II.I when it freezes, blocks, burns or forces the transfer of units held by a pool the TSV provides, or pauses transfers of an asset in that pool, or when the TSV or a Covered Firm supplying the pool holds the function, and confirm that "auditable" in Section II.A includes the authority to replace the audited code. Item (n) asks these questions only of the TSV's own applications, and the program's verification of four major tokenized Treasury products, as documented in March 2026, found no timelock in any administrative control chain.
  3. State, for each leg of a trade, when the transfer is final and who bears the loss if it is reversed or frozen (Notice item (y); Questions 4, 5 and 7). Require Notice item (y) to state, for each asset in a trading pair, the ledger event at which the TSV treats the transfer as final; the legal effect of that event under the terms governing the asset, including whether it transfers the security or security entitlement or instructs an off-chain record; and how loss is allocated if a completed transfer is reversed by a reorganization of the ledger or the asset is frozen after execution. Settlement is "near instantaneous" only if the ledger event is the legal transfer for both assets. Every fiat-backed stablecoin in the MVEP's sample failed its settlement-finality category for want of a finality analysis comparable to UCC Article 4A, and because a pool holds every liquidity provider's stablecoin at one contract address, a block on that address would freeze the stablecoin side of the whole pool.
  4. Add the ledger transaction identifier and the U.S. dollar conversion rate to each transaction record (Section II.G; Questions 3 and 5). Add to the Section II.G minimum fields (vi) the identifier of the transaction on the distributed ledger and (vii), for any pair whose other asset is not denominated in U.S. dollars, the conversion rate used to state the price in dollars and the source of that rate; the TSV already holds both when it computes the dollar price. Without the rate, a reader cannot tell a move in the stock from a move in the stablecoin it traded against: in the March 2023 banking stress one stablecoin fell 10.4 percent below par on an hourly decentralized-exchange volume-weighted basis and 13 percent at the centralized-exchange intraday low, so the choice of conversion source alone moved the measured price by more than two and a half percentage points. Without the identifier, no reported print can be reconciled to the ledger.
  5. Ask Covered Firms for their control over the parameters of pools they supply and their share of each pool's liquidity (Section IV.A; Questions 8 and 10). Add to the Covered Firm notification, and to the records a Covered Firm must keep, any key, role or governance right held by the firm or its affiliates that can set or alter the parameters, fees, code or pause state of an AMM Liquidity Pool it supplies, together with its share of each such pool's liquidity at the end of each calendar month. The Order names control over pricing as the conduct that may make liquidity provision dealing, and on a ledger that control is exercised through acts the Order itself lists. A supplier that trades against a pool's price does not set it; one that also holds the pool's parameter, fee or pause controls is in a different position, and the two facts distinguish one from the other.
  6. Determine affiliation for the volume and symbol limits by who can exercise a pool's privileged functions (Section II.F; Question 5). Confirm that control "by contract, or otherwise" in the Order's definition includes the ability to exercise the privileged functions described in Recommendation 2, so that, for the aggregation conditions, TSVs are affiliated where the same person, or persons acting together, can exercise those functions over an AMM Liquidity Pool that each provides. On a ledger the decisive power over a pool can sit with a shared key holder across entities that are not affiliated in the corporate sense, as with the single multisignature wallet the program found controlling several tokenized Treasury products. The reading adds no new category of affiliation and no disclosure beyond Recommendation 2, and it does not reach a shared software vendor, data provider or node operator that holds no such function.
  7. Address the GENIUS Act's July 18, 2028 offer-and-sale restriction before it applies to a TSV's pairing of a payment stablecoin (footnote 24; Question 4). With Treasury, state before July 18, 2028 how section 3(b)(1) of the GENIUS Act applies to a TSV's pairing of a payment stablecoin, and in the meantime require the Notice to identify, for each paired payment stablecoin, whether its issuer is a permitted payment stablecoin issuer. From that date, subject to exceptions, section 3(b)(1) makes it unlawful for a digital asset service provider to offer or sell a payment stablecoin to a person in the United States unless a permitted payment stablecoin issuer issued it. The date falls inside the Order's term, which runs to September 17, 2031, and footnote 24 leaves open whether a TSV is a digital asset service provider. The comment does not ask the Commission to limit paired assets by type.

The problem identified

The Order is designed to produce evidence. The Commission anticipates that experience with the exemptions, which are time-limited and conditional, will help inform its future actions in this area, including rulemaking, and Commissioner Peirce describes their purpose as an opportunity "to observe how tokenized NMS stocks are used and traded in different onchain contexts and how onchain and traditional markets interact with one another." Several of the conditions turn on facts the public ledger already makes observable, but the Order collects them as descriptions of procedure, collects some only for the TSV's own smart contracts rather than for the assets traded in its pools, and does not collect others at all.

Three examples show the gap. The Order requires a TSV to name who can upgrade, modify, suspend, override or cease its own smart contracts, but asks no equivalent question about the token contracts of the Tokenized NMS Stock, stablecoin or tokenized money market fund in each pool, although those contracts commonly carry functions to mint, freeze, force a transfer, pause or replace their own code. It deems a token to carry the same rights as the share by reference to four rights a shareholder holds against the issuer, while the exposure that third-party tokenization adds, to the tokenizer itself, sits outside all four. And it requires transaction prices in U.S. dollars but not the rate used to convert a stablecoin price into dollars, so a reader of the data cannot tell a move in the stock from a move in the stablecoin it traded against.

A note on scope and evidence

The comment addresses Questions 1, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10 and the operational part of Question 7. It takes no position on the duration or permanence of either exemption (Questions 2 and 9), on the calibration of the Tier 1 and Tier 2 limits (Question 6), on Regulation NMS relief for broker-dealer participants, on whether TSVs should trade securities other than NMS stock, or on the issuer notice and objection mechanics, because the program has no evidence to offer on them.

Because Tokenized NMS Stock was not yet trading under the Order when the comment was filed, where a finding comes from other products the comment says so at the point of use and offers it for what it shows about the control layer of tokenized assets generally. The MVEP's subject is tokenized money and collateral products and the governance audit concerns governance tokens, so both are offered as method; the program's on-chain measurements are described without naming the products measured; and the comment states, as a limitation of the program's own index, that the Control Layer Intensity Index scores whether a capability exists, not the quality of the controls around it. None of the recommendations requires a program instrument: any reviewer may determine the facts they ask for by its own method. Every quotation was checked against an archived copy of its source and every Federal Register pin cite against the page it names, and statements of the Commission's staff are attributed to the staff, not the Commission.

Status note

Two temporary, conditional exemptions under Exchange Act section 36(a)(1), in effect from September 17, 2026 until September 17, 2031: the TSV Exemption, from the definition of exchange, for venues that trade Tokenized NMS Stock through AMM Liquidity Pools, and the Covered Firm Exemption, from the definition of dealer, for certain liquidity providers. The Order requests comment on all aspects of the exemptions and sets no comment deadline; the Commission may modify the exemptions.
