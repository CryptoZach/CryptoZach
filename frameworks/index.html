Featured framework

MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack)

Ten-category institutional diligence standard for tokenized money and securities. Tests three equivalence claims: legal, operational, economic.

  1. Rights Parity
  2. System of Record
  3. Records and Reconciliation
  4. Custody and Customer Property
  5. Insolvency Posture
  6. Settlement Finality
  7. Reorg and Exception Handling
  8. Governance and Change Control
  9. Disclosures
  10. Collateral Operability

For tokenization issuers and custodians

  • Gate timingPre-launch or pre-committee approval; re-runs on material product change, custodian enforcement action, or reserve-composition shift beyond pre-disclosed band.
  • ArtifactsEach category pairs to a named-owner artifact (counsel sign-off, custody agreement, bankruptcy-priority memo, scenario analysis). Missing artifacts or failed tests block the gate.
  • Stress envelopeThree liquidation paths, four path constraints, and seven stress scenarios specified per tokenized Treasury (MVEP Section 2.3).

For regulators and policy researchers

  • Capital gapApproximately 22-to-1 between bank-chartered (~12% CET1) and money-transmitter-chartered (~0.54% tangible net worth) issuers at $40B scale.
  • Rulemaking footprintCited across four of eight federal comment letters (OCC, FDIC, Treasury).
  • Production testCircle BUIDL-to-USDC redemption-pool incident, March 27, 2025 (23 hours of limited availability; Category 10 composed-product cascade).

Framework stack

Two parallel sequences. Both share control-layer and stress-first logic; the diligence artifacts differ.

Track ADigital money sequence

  1. 1

    Define the object

    Payment stablecoin, tokenized deposit, deposit token, or yield wrapper. Each starts a different rulebook (Seven Dollars).

  2. 2

    Test the product at launch

    MVEP. Ten diligence categories, each with a named artifact and a pass-or-fail test.

  3. 3

    Monitor the control layer after approval

    CLII. Approval starts the monitoring cycle; CLII is the drift detector.

  4. 4

    Map balance-sheet and system-level consequences

    Credit Migration Model. Where deposits move, how funding quality changes, which banks absorb the shift.

  5. 5

    Identify who controls the operating layer

    Regime Dashboard. Routing defaults, sweep ownership, hold authority, and which entities recur across strategies (Three Strategies).

Track BToken-economy sequence

  1. 1

    Classify the payer base

    Burn Mechanism Classification. S2R (Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio, the fiscal-sustainability measure) and HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the standard market-concentration measure) separate carrier-contract from subscription designs.

  2. 2

    Demonstrate the integrated mechanism

    Adaptive-Tokenomics Pattern Map. Stress-tests the full stack (staking, governance, burn-mint, and emissions controlled by a PID loop, the proportional-integral-derivative feedback architecture used to stabilize systems against disturbance) across multi-year scenarios.

  3. 3

    Test operational resilience under stress

    Operational Risk Taxonomy. 74 vectors mapped to 64 controls across a 624-scenario battery. Economic-only simulations miss what infrastructure failures actually change.

  4. 4

    Diagnose realized governance concentration

    Governance-Concentration Diagnostic. Sector, insider retention, and delegation patterns predict concentration; initial allocation alone does not.

Classification frameworks

Two frameworks for naming what you are looking at before testing it. Classification precedes diligence: a Seven Dollars product profile or a Three Strategies position determines which diligence pack and monitoring posture apply downstream.

Comparative Framework for Dollar Products

Classification · Dollar-denominated products

What it does. Positions seven dollar-denominated digital products (USDC, USDT, USDG, PYUSD, DAI, USDe, OUSG) across two independent dimensions: operator regulation and product equivalence. Each requires a structurally different evidence package.

Why it matters. Operator regulation and product equivalence are separate dimensions. Conflating them produces the diligence error that allowed Circle (CLII 0.92) to break during the SVB (Silicon Valley Bank) failure in March 2023 while less-regulated operators held.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new product class, a regulatory regime change that re-positions an issuer, or a structural shift in how an existing product is collateralized or wrapped.

Tokenization Infrastructure Map

Classification · Institutional tokenization

What it does. Classifies institutional US-securities tokenization as three strategies: a regulated collateral circuit (tokenized Treasuries + bank-issued deposit tokens), a multi-issuer yield stack (five fund issuers with a smart redemption router), and tokenized equity rails on Nasdaq and NYSE. Maps which entities recur across strategies.

Why it matters. System-level risk lives in the entities that connect the strategies, not in the strategies themselves. Securitize, an SEC-registered transfer agent, sits in all three; any new strategy that depends on the same transfer agent, custodian, or redemption router inherits that concentration by default.

What triggers re-evaluation. A fourth strategy emerging, a structural change at a cross-strategy entity, or a regulatory change that re-partitions which entity types can participate.

Track A frameworks: digital money and market infrastructure

Seven frameworks for testing stablecoin issuers, tokenized deposits, deposit tokens, yield wrappers, and the control-layer operators that sit above them. Includes MGCPT and TCRD as derivative frameworks operationalizing MVEP categories.

MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack)

Diligence · Pre-launch entry gate

What it does. Tests three equivalence claims (legal, operational, economic) across ten diligence categories: Rights Parity, System of Record, Records and Reconciliation, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture, Settlement Finality, Reorg and Exception Handling, Governance and Change Control, Disclosures, and Collateral Operability. For tokenized Treasuries, the paper identifies three liquidation paths, four constraints on path availability, and seven stress scenarios.

Why it matters. A product that cannot demonstrate equivalence across all three claims is not ready for institutional adoption. MVEP is the entry gate before launch or committee approval; missing artifacts or failed tests block the gate.

What triggers re-evaluation. A missing artifact, a failed test, a failure sequence with no documented recovery plan, or an unclear loss-absorption path.

MGCPT (MVEP-gated Conditional Pass-Through) Preliminary

Derivative on MVEP · Pass-through gating

What it does. Conditions FDIC pass-through deposit insurance eligibility on MVEP qualification across six pass-through-critical categories (Rights Parity, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture, Settlement Finality, Governance and Change Control, Disclosures). Statutory anchor: 12 U.S.C. § 5903(e)(1) and (e)(2); IOLTA (Interest on Lawyer Trust Accounts), Section 529 plans, and custodial brokerage accounts as direct analogies.

Why it matters. The FDIC's proposed § 330.11(a)(3) categorically forecloses pass-through coverage for PPSI-related deposits. AG19 letter Recommendation 7 proposes MGCPT as the substantive alternative: condition pass-through on MVEP qualification, retaining categorical foreclosure for non-qualified PPSIs. Preserves the FDIC's prudential interest while honoring the GENIUS Act's intent that qualified PPSIs be treated as bank-deposit-equivalent when their architecture supports it.

What triggers re-evaluation. FDIC AG19 final-rule disposition (statutory deadline window ~2026-07-18), a change in any of the six pass-through-critical MVEP categories' artifacts, or a new statutory regime affecting 12 U.S.C. § 5903(e).

TCRD (Three-Category Reserve Disclosure)

Derivative on MVEP · Reserve disclosure

What it does. Customer-facing disclosure template derived from MVEP Categories 1, 4, and 5 (Rights Parity, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture). Surfaces three substantive disclosure items at the point-of-use: legal claim (the holder's claim on the reserve), reserve composition (what the reserves are made of), and reserve location and custody (where the reserves sit, with which custodian).

Why it matters. MVEP scores the artifact (rights-parity memo with counsel sign-off, segregation model with custody agreement, bankruptcy-priority memo with scenario analysis); TCRD is the displayable summary surfacing that artifact's substantive content where holders actually see it. MVEP without TCRD leaves the diligence content invisible to holders; TCRD without MVEP leaves the displayable summary unanchored to verifiable artifacts. Cross-letter operationalized across OCC supplemental, Treasury Section II, and FDIC AG19 Recommendations 1 to 6.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in the underlying MVEP Categories 1, 4, or 5 artifacts; a new disclosure rule in PPSI rulemaking; or new cross-letter operationalization that extends the template.

CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index)

Monitoring · Post-approval drift detection

What it does. Scores each gateway across five regulatory dimensions: licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions. Weighted 0-to-1 score per gateway.

Why it matters. A high CLII score means more regulatory obligations, not stress resilience. Circle (CLII 0.92) and Coinbase (CLII 0.85) had near-identical scores but showed sharply different stress retention during SVB. Stress depends on reserve-bank exposure, counterparty concentration, and other conditions outside what CLII measures.

What triggers re-evaluation. A tier crossing (for example Tier 1 to Tier 2), a significant drop in any single dimension, a jump in volume concentration through one gateway, or a new dependency where multi-gateway traffic routes through one.

Credit Migration Model In development

Deposit analysis · Funding-structure shift

What it does. Tracks where deposit balances sit and how they move as stablecoin reserve growth scales: which banks gain or lose funding, how funding quality changes, and whether reserves reduce, recycle, or restructure deposits.

Why it matters. Bank funding can become more concentrated (fewer banks hold larger shares), more skewed toward institutional depositors than retail, and more vulnerable to rapid withdrawal as stablecoin-serving banks absorb reserves. That is the mechanism the Federal Reserve highlights in its December 2025 note. The essay's term "deposit mutation" is the author's synthesis; the Fed describes the underlying mechanism as deposits being reduced, recycled, or restructured.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in which assets issuers hold as reserves, concentration of reserves at fewer banks, a shift in how redemptions are processed, or a change in which banks absorb new reserve inflows.

Regime Dashboard

Control layer · Operating-regime mapping

What it does. Tracks who controls the operating layer right now: interface ownership, default routing, sweep ownership, hold authority, fee structures, and disclosure obligations.

Why it matters. CLARITY-style provisions (House-passed form analyzed here) attach compliance where it becomes operational: at the interface, in routing defaults, in hold-and-release workflows, and in audit logs. The question is whether the entity that bears compliance also captures the economics (fees, float, data), or whether the two end up at different points in the stack.

What triggers re-evaluation. A shift in front-end interface ownership, default routing control, where yield is allowed to live, or where hold and reporting obligations attach.

Tokenized Dollar Regime Classifier

Market structure · Equilibrium classification

What it does. Classifies the U.S. dollar operating-layer equilibrium along two axes, each scored 0 to 6 via three components. ControlScore = obligated-interface share of flows + default sweep and wrapper penetration + holds-or-freeze usage as operating norm. IssuerScore = settlement-object concentration + reserve carry retention + direct mint and burn authority. Classification rule (3-month rolling average, weekly in stress): Control-dominant (ControlScore ≥ 4 and beats IssuerScore by ≥ 2), Issuer-dominant (mirror test), or Split / Contested otherwise.

Why it matters. Names which layer captures the economics, who bears the regulatory burden, and whether policy is shifting the equilibrium. Tracks structural power allocation, not safety or solvency.

What triggers re-evaluation. Three or more of five high-beta policy signposts moving the same direction in a quarter (GENIUS implementation rulemaking; CBDC (central bank digital currency) posture; bank deposit-token launches; offshore perimeter tightening; committee leadership plus markup calendar changes), or two or more component scores flipping in a quarter.

Track B frameworks: tokenomics and DePIN

Four frameworks for testing token-economy mechanism design, operational resilience, governance concentration, and burn-driven fiscal sustainability across DePIN (Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks, where token incentives coordinate real-world hardware deployment) and adjacent token systems. They extend the same control-layer and stress-first logic from Track A to the protocol layer.

Operational Risk Taxonomy

Diligence · Operational resilience

What it does. Maps 74 risk vectors to 64 testable controls and 31 program objectives across a 624-scenario simulation battery. Calibrated from Ethereum, Helium, GEODNET, and Filecoin production data using ValOS (Validator Operating System) and NORS (Node Operator Risk Standard) frameworks.

Why it matters. Economic-only simulations miss the infrastructure failures that change token-economy behavior. Reputation systems gated by hard thresholds can become unworkable under routine operational risk; adaptive emission control can be more resilient than static designs under the same stress.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new failure pattern, a node-operator concentration shift, a reputation-system threshold crossing, or a previously unmodeled correlation between economic and operational stress.

Burn Mechanism Classification

Classification · Fiscal sustainability

What it does. Classifies token networks by payer concentration using S2R (Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio, the fiscal-sustainability measure) and HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the standard market-concentration measure). Differentiates carrier-contract burn models from subscription models.

Why it matters. Burn-mechanism design, not network size, drives sustainability. Helium's carrier-contract model: HHI 0.22, Top-5 demand sources roughly 90% of burn volume. GEODNET's subscription model: HHI 0.055, Top-5 = 40.4%. The burn-mint equilibrium (BME) crossing requires both fee growth and supply compression, not either alone.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in S2R trajectory (not just level), an emission halving or other supply-compression event, a shift in demand concentration, or BME parity crossing.

Governance-Concentration Diagnostic

Diagnostic · Governance design

What it does. Tests whether initial token allocation predicts realized governance concentration using a 40-protocol cross-section (15 DePIN, 15 DeFi (decentralized finance), 8 L1/L2 (Layer 1 and Layer 2 blockchain infrastructure) protocols, 2 social or stagnated). Lead correlation is null (Pearson r = 0.18, p = 0.28).

Why it matters. Sector membership predicts concentration (Mann-Whitney p = 0.014); insider retention amplifies it (Spearman rho = 0.54); delegation amplifies token concentration into voting concentration by 3 to 6 times in DePIN. Applies a five-lens framework (Kantian publicity, Rawlsian legitimacy, Pettit non-domination, Ostromian polycentricity, Hayekian knowledge-use) with a Blockscout-verified exclusion of 69 protocol-controlled addresses across 20 protocols.

What triggers re-evaluation. A sector-membership shift, a change in insider retention, a delegation-pattern change, or a new dataset that revises the institutional-design controls.

Adaptive-Tokenomics Pattern Map

Stress design · Mechanism demonstration

What it does. Integrates productive staking, reputation-weighted governance, burn-mint value capture, constitutional governance, and PID-controlled adaptive emissions (PID is a proportional-integral-derivative feedback controller, a standard control architecture used to stabilize systems against disturbance), then stress-tests the full stack across five years of adverse scenarios.

Why it matters. Integrated design surfaces structural properties that no single-mechanism analysis can. Results are mechanism demonstrations under stated assumptions, not deployment forecasts. MeshNet is a fictional but empirically parameterized DePIN network used to illustrate the framework.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new stress scenario, a parameter range not previously modeled, or a real-deployment data point that contradicts the simulation.

How the work runs

Four operating principles behind every framework on this page.

Evidence-led

Principle · Source discipline

Every claim ties to a source, a threshold, or a tested condition. No claim ships without one.

Stress-first design

Principle · Stress-first orientation

Each framework names where stress enters first, which thresholds force re-evaluation, and what evidence should change the view.

Named decision owners

Principle · Accountability

Every framework points to who approves, who monitors, who escalates, and what event forces action.

Versioned and replicable

Principle · Reproducibility

Frameworks publish when the evidence is strong enough to support real operating decisions. Manuscripts and code travel with them when possible.

Built so allocators, regulators, and operators can trace every framework decision back to its trigger, its owner, and its evidence.
