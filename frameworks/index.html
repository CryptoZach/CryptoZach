FRAMEWORKS

Frameworks for tokenized infrastructure: classification, diligence, and monitoring.

Digital money is programmable money. Each framework names what to test, what should change the view, and who acts when conditions deteriorate. Two of them, CLII and MVEP, extend to AI operators.

Unifying thesis:

Who earns the token matters more than who holds it. Classify the token, but regulate and audit the operator.

What are you evaluating? One framework per step.

Classify the token. Audit the operator. Test the system. Every tokenized product is evaluated in the same order.
Each step names the question it settles and the framework that answers it; the field guide walks the sequence in full.

    What exactly is the token? The dollar-product and infrastructure maps name the class, and the class decides which rulebook applies.

Featured framework

MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack)

Ten-category institutional diligence standard for tokenized money and securities. Tests three equivalence claims: legal, operational, economic.

  1. Rights Parity
  2. System of Record
  3. Records and Reconciliation
  4. Custody and Customer Property
  5. Insolvency Posture
  6. Settlement Finality
  7. Reorganization and Exception Handling
  8. Governance and Change Control
  9. Disclosures
  10. Collateral Operability

For tokenization issuers and custodians

  • Gate timingPre-launch or pre-committee approval; re-runs on material product change, custodian enforcement action, or reserve-composition shift beyond pre-disclosed band.
  • ArtifactsEach category pairs to a named-owner artifact (counsel sign-off, custody agreement, bankruptcy-priority memo, scenario analysis). Missing artifacts or failed tests block the gate.
  • Stress envelopeThree liquidation paths, four path constraints, and seven stress scenarios specified per tokenized Treasury (MVEP Section 2.3).

For regulators and policy researchers

  • Capital gapApproximately 22-to-1 between bank-chartered (~12% Common Equity Tier 1 / CET1, the Basel bank-capital benchmark) and money-transmitter-chartered (~0.54% tangible net worth) issuers at $40B scale.
  • Rulemaking footprintCited across the federal comment letter program (OCC, Treasury, FDIC, FinCEN, OFAC, NCUA, SEC, Board of Governors, CFTC).
  • Production testCircle BUIDL-to-USDC redemption-pool incident, March 27, 2025 (23 hours of limited availability; Category 10 Collateral Operability composed-product cascade).

The MVEP scorecard: fourteen products the paper scored, and yours

Exhibit 1 of the MVEP paper scores fourteen tokenized dollar products on the ten categories and the Cash Settlement Asset companion test: 154 verdicts, each tied to the place in the paper that establishes it. Exhibit 3 turns each column into a rating by its weakest cell. Both are loaded here, unchanged.

1

Read a product the paper scored. Choose it from the list. Each category shows the paper's verdict and its source; the rating panel shows the three equivalence dimensions, what binds, and the strength score.

2

Score your own product. Choose "Your own product", record a verdict per category in the paper's vocabulary, and the paper's rule rates it beside a comparator you pick. Nothing leaves your browser; "Copy link" carries your verdicts to a colleague.

Pass Partial Ambiguous Fail Not disclosed Unassessed

Not disclosed is an assessed verdict: a documented search found no published issuer artifact for the category. Unassessed means no verdict of record exists. A category's rating tier is set by its weakest cell on the ladder Pass, Partial, Ambiguous, Not disclosed, Fail; the strength score is the mean rung (1.0 to 5.0) and orders products within a tier without ever lifting it.

  1. 1 Rights Parity pass-through-critical

    Verify the tokenized representation preserves legal rights equivalent to the underlying instrument.

    Artifact: rights-parity memo with counsel sign-off. Pass: claim holder enjoys the same redemption, recourse, and information rights as a holder of the underlying. Fail: any right is degraded, gated, or contingent on operator discretion.

    Your verdict for Rights Parity

  2. 2 System of Record

    Identify the authoritative ledger of ownership and the procedure that reconciles on-chain state with off-chain books.

    Artifact: system-of-record designation memo plus reconciliation cadence policy. Pass: a single authoritative record exists with a documented reconciliation procedure. Fail: conflicting records or undefined reconciliation.

    Your verdict for System of Record

  3. 3 Records and Reconciliation

    Verify mechanism for reconciling the on-chain token supply against the off-chain reserve or underlying.

    Artifact: reconciliation procedure document plus attestation cadence. Pass: continuous or daily reconciliation with auditor sign-off. Fail: reconciliation gap, manual override path, or missing attestation.

    Your verdict for Records and Reconciliation

  4. 4 Custody and Customer Property pass-through-critical

    Verify segregation model for reserves or underlying, custody agreement terms, and customer-property characterization under stress.

    Artifact: segregation model plus custody agreement plus customer-property memo. Pass: reserves are bankruptcy-remote and segregated under enforceable agreement. Fail: commingling, undocumented custody chain, or customer-property uncertainty.

    Your verdict for Custody and Customer Property

  5. 5 Insolvency Posture pass-through-critical

    Verify what happens to token holders if the issuer, custodian, or operator enters insolvency.

    Artifact: bankruptcy-priority memo with scenario analysis. Pass: clear priority class for holders, with worked stress scenarios. Fail: undefined priority, subordination risk, or no scenario analysis.

    Your verdict for Insolvency Posture

  6. 6 Settlement Finality pass-through-critical

    Identify when a token transfer is final under the chain's consensus and under applicable law.

    Artifact: settlement-finality memo addressing both layers. Pass: documented finality at both layers with reorganization handling. Fail: finality gap between layers, or chain-reorg risk without recovery plan.

    Your verdict for Settlement Finality

  7. 7 Reorganization and Exception Handling

    Verify procedures for chain reorganizations, smart-contract exploits, validator collusion, custody breach, and other exception conditions.

    Artifact: exception-handling runbook with decision authority. Pass: documented runbook covering material exception classes. Fail: any failure mode with no documented recovery path.

    Your verdict for Reorganization and Exception Handling

  8. 8 Governance and Change Control pass-through-critical

    Verify governance authority for protocol upgrades, parameter changes, and emergency interventions.

    Artifact: governance charter plus change-control procedure plus emergency-pause documentation. Pass: clear authority chain with procedural gates. Fail: undocumented upgrade authority or unilateral emergency power.

    Your verdict for Governance and Change Control

  9. 9 Disclosures pass-through-critical

    Verify substantive disclosure of reserve composition, custody, redemption rights, governance, and stress posture at the point-of-use.

    Artifact: disclosure register mapped to MVEP categories. Pass: holder can answer the key diligence questions from disclosed materials. Fail: critical fact buried, omitted, or only available on request.

    Your verdict for Disclosures

  10. 10 Collateral Operability

    If the tokenized product will serve as collateral on derivatives venues or repo markets, verify collateral framework treats the token equivalently to its underlying.

    Artifact: collateral acceptance documentation per venue. Pass: collateral haircuts and acceptance criteria are transparent and equivalent. Fail: opaque eligibility or differential haircuts across functionally-equivalent tokenized products.

    Your verdict for Collateral Operability

  11. + Cash Settlement Asset (companion test)

    Verify the asset the product subscribes and redeems in. A product whose redemption routes through another token inherits that token's verdicts: the paper's recursive test, and the row where otherwise comparable products separate. RLUSD is the only column in the paper that passes it.

    Your verdict for Cash Settlement Asset (companion test)

Track B sequence: token economies

Token economies and physical networks (DePIN) are a different subject from tokenized financial products, so they keep their own four-step order. Tokenized products follow the decision map above.

Track B Token-economy sequence

  1. 1

    Classify the payer base

    Burn Mechanism Classification. S2R (Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio, the fiscal-sustainability measure) and HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the standard market-concentration measure) separate carrier-contract from subscription designs.

  2. 2

    Demonstrate the integrated mechanism

    Adaptive-Tokenomics Stress Audit. Stress-tests the full stack (staking, governance, burn-mint, and emissions controlled by a PID loop, the proportional-integral-derivative feedback architecture used to stabilize systems against disturbance) across multi-year scenarios.

  3. 3

    Test operational resilience under stress

    Operational Risk Taxonomy. 74 vectors mapped to 64 controls across a 624-configuration battery. Economic-only simulations miss what infrastructure failures actually change.

  4. 4

    Diagnose realized governance concentration

    Governance-Concentration Diagnostic. Sector and insider retention are associated with post-launch concentration, delegation can amplify voting power beyond holdings, and initial allocation is uninformative.

Classification frameworks

Two frameworks for naming what you are looking at before testing it. Classification precedes diligence: a Seven Dollars product profile or a Three Strategies position determines which diligence pack and monitoring posture apply downstream.

Comparative Framework for Dollar Products

Classification · Dollar-denominated products

What it does. Positions seven dollar-denominated digital products (USDC, USDT, USDG, PYUSD, DAI, USDe, OUSG) across two independent dimensions: operator regulation and product equivalence. Each requires a structurally different evidence package.

Why it matters. Operator regulation and product equivalence are separate dimensions. Conflating them produces the diligence error that allowed Circle (CLII 0.92) to break during the SVB (Silicon Valley Bank) failure in March 2023 while less-regulated operators held.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new product class, a regulatory regime change that re-positions an issuer, or a structural shift in how an existing product is collateralized or wrapped.

Tokenization Infrastructure Map

Classification · Institutional tokenization

What it does. Classifies institutional US-securities tokenization as three strategies: a regulated collateral circuit (tokenized Treasuries + bank-issued deposit tokens), a multi-issuer yield stack (five fund issuers with a smart redemption router), and tokenized equity rails on Nasdaq and NYSE. Maps which entities recur across strategies.

Why it matters. System-level risk lives in the entities that connect the strategies, not in the strategies themselves. Securitize, an SEC-registered transfer agent, sits in all three; any new strategy that depends on the same transfer agent, custodian, or redemption router inherits that concentration by default.

What triggers re-evaluation. A fourth strategy emerging, a structural change at a cross-strategy entity, or a regulatory change that re-partitions which entity types can participate.

Track A frameworks: programmable money and market infrastructure

The two frameworks the decision map leans on most: MVEP at Compare and Stress, CLII at Map and Decide.
The five narrower Track A tools follow under their own heading.

MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack)

Diligence · Pre-launch entry gate

What it does. Tests three equivalence claims (legal, operational, economic) across ten diligence categories: Rights Parity, System of Record, Records and Reconciliation, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture, Settlement Finality, Reorganization and Exception Handling, Governance and Change Control, Disclosures, and Collateral Operability. For tokenized Treasuries, the paper identifies three liquidation paths, four constraints on path availability, and seven stress scenarios.

Why it matters. Under MVEP, a product is not ready for institutional adoption until it demonstrates equivalence across all three claims. MVEP is the entry gate before launch or committee approval; missing artifacts or failed tests block the gate.

What triggers re-evaluation. A missing artifact, a failed test, a failure sequence with no documented recovery plan, or an unclear loss-absorption path.

Open Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack →

CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index)

Monitoring · Post-approval drift detection

What it does. Scores each gateway (a stablecoin issuer or major distributor routing dollars between on-chain and off-chain rails; e.g., Circle, Coinbase, Tether) across five regulatory dimensions: licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions. Weighted 0-to-1 score per gateway.

Why it matters. A high CLII score means more regulatory obligations. Stress retention is a separate reading. Circle (CLII 0.92) and Coinbase (CLII 0.85) sat close on CLII and far apart in stress retention during SVB. Circle's SVB exposure, a banking tie outside the five dimensions, is the leading explanation.

What triggers re-evaluation. A tier crossing (for example Tier 1 to Tier 2), a significant drop in any single dimension, a jump in volume concentration through one gateway, or a new dependency where multi-gateway traffic routes through one.

Five tools that answer one step in more depth: pass-through conditions, reserve disclosure, deposit migration, operating regimes, and dollar-regime classification. Start with the decision map; open these when a step needs a finer instrument.

MGCPT (MVEP-gated Conditional Pass-Through) Preliminary

Derivative on MVEP · Pass-through gating

What it does. Conditions FDIC pass-through deposit insurance eligibility on MVEP qualification across six pass-through-critical categories (Rights Parity, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture, Settlement Finality, Governance and Change Control, Disclosures). Statutory anchor: 12 U.S.C. § 5903(e)(1) and (e)(2); IOLTA (Interest on Lawyer Trust Accounts), Section 529 plans, and custodial brokerage accounts as direct analogies.

Why it matters. The FDIC's proposed § 330.11(a)(3) categorically forecloses pass-through coverage for deposits held by PPSIs (Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers under the GENIUS Act). AG19 letter Recommendation 7 proposes MGCPT as the substantive alternative: condition pass-through on MVEP qualification, retaining categorical foreclosure for non-qualified PPSIs. Preserves the FDIC's prudential interest while honoring the GENIUS Act's intent that qualified PPSIs be treated as bank-deposit-equivalent when their architecture supports it.

What triggers re-evaluation. FDIC AG19 final-rule disposition under the July 18, 2026 GENIUS Act statutory deadline (tracked live on the GENIUS Act rulemaking tracker), a change in any of the six pass-through-critical MVEP categories' artifacts, or a new statutory regime affecting 12 U.S.C. § 5903(e).

3CRD (Three-Category Reserve Disclosure)

Derivative on MVEP · Reserve disclosure

What it does. Customer-facing disclosure template derived from MVEP Categories 1, 4, and 5 (Rights Parity, Custody and Customer Property, Insolvency Posture). Surfaces three substantive disclosure items at the point-of-use: legal claim (the holder's claim on the reserve), reserve composition (what the reserves are made of), and reserve location and custody (where the reserves sit, with which custodian).

Why it matters. MVEP scores the artifact (rights-parity memo with counsel sign-off, segregation model with custody agreement, bankruptcy-priority memo with scenario analysis); 3CRD is the displayable summary surfacing that artifact's substantive content where holders actually see it. MVEP without 3CRD leaves the diligence content invisible to holders; 3CRD without MVEP leaves the displayable summary unanchored to verifiable artifacts. Cross-letter operationalized across OCC supplemental, Treasury Section II, and FDIC AG19 Recommendations 1 to 6.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in the underlying MVEP Categories 1, 4, or 5 artifacts; a new disclosure rule in PPSI rulemaking; or new cross-letter operationalization that extends the template.

Credit Migration Model In development

Deposit analysis · Funding-structure shift

What it does. Tracks where deposit balances sit and how they move as stablecoin reserve growth scales: which banks gain or lose funding, how funding quality changes, and whether reserves reduce, recycle, or restructure deposits.

Why it matters. Bank funding can become more concentrated (fewer banks hold larger shares), more skewed toward institutional depositors than retail, and more vulnerable to rapid withdrawal as stablecoin-serving banks absorb reserves. That is the mechanism the Federal Reserve highlights in its December 2025 note. The essay's term "deposit mutation" is the author's synthesis; the Fed describes the underlying mechanism as deposits being reduced, recycled, or restructured.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in which assets issuers hold as reserves, concentration of reserves at fewer banks, a shift in how redemptions are processed, or a change in which banks absorb new reserve inflows.

Regime Dashboard

Control layer · Operating-regime mapping

What it does. Tracks who controls the operating layer right now: interface ownership, default routing, sweep ownership, hold authority, fee structures, and disclosure obligations.

Why it matters. CLARITY Act provisions (the House-passed digital-asset market-structure bill analyzed here) attach compliance where it becomes operational: at the interface, in routing defaults, in hold-and-release workflows, and in audit logs. Tokenized Dollar Regime Classifier (TDRC)

Market structure · Equilibrium classification

What it does. Classifies the U.S. tokenized-dollar market by which layer dominates, in income and in control: the apps, wallets, and routers users go through, or the issuers. ControlScore (0 to 6) measures the app layer: how much flow must pass an app with a legal duty, how far automatic sweeps and yield wrappers have spread, and how routine it is to hold or freeze a transfer. IssuerScore (0 to 6) measures the issuer: how concentrated the settlement dollar is, how much of the interest on reserves the issuer keeps, and whether the issuer still decides who can mint and redeem. Each score is three parts, and each part runs from 0 to 2. The reading uses a three-month average, and weekly readings in a stress. Control-dominant means ControlScore is at least 4 and leads IssuerScore by at least 2. Issuer-dominant is the same test in the other direction. Otherwise neither side leads.

Why it matters. It names who keeps the income and who carries the compliance duty, and whether policy is moving that split. Safety and solvency sit outside this score. CLII, on its own 0-to-1 scale, scores how regulated a gateway is.

What triggers re-evaluation. Three or more of six leading policy signposts moving the same direction in a quarter (GENIUS implementation rulemaking; CBDC (central bank digital currency) posture; bank deposit-token launches; offshore perimeter tightening; committee leadership plus markup calendar changes; SEC-CFTC 2026 Joint Final Rule on Crypto Taxonomy), or two or more component scores flipping in a quarter.

Track B frameworks: tokenomics and DePIN

Four frameworks for testing token-economy mechanism design, operational resilience, governance concentration, and burn-driven fiscal sustainability across DePIN (Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks, where token incentives coordinate real-world hardware deployment) and adjacent token systems. They extend the same control-layer and stress-first logic from Track A to the protocol layer.

Operational Risk Taxonomy

Diligence · Operational resilience

What it does. Maps the ValOS taxonomy (74 risk vectors, 64 testable controls) and the NORS standard (36 control objectives) onto seven simulatable parameters, tested across a 624-configuration simulation battery. Calibrated from Ethereum, Helium, GEODNET, and Filecoin production data using ValOS (Validator Operation Standard) and NORS (Node Operator Risk Standard) frameworks.

Why it matters. Economic-only simulations miss the infrastructure failures that change token-economy behavior. Reputation systems gated by hard thresholds can become unworkable under routine operational risk; adaptive emission control can be more resilient than static designs under the same stress.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new failure pattern, a node-operator concentration shift, a reputation-system threshold crossing, or a previously unmodeled correlation between economic and operational stress.

Burn Mechanism Classification

Classification · Fiscal sustainability

What it does. Classifies token networks by payment architecture and payer concentration using S2R (Subsidy-to-Revenue ratio, the fiscal-sustainability measure) and HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the standard market-concentration measure). Differentiates direct-payment architectures, where the customer base is observable on-chain, from buy-and-burn, single-relayer, and credit-intermediated models, where a treasury, relayer, or credit contract signs in place of the customer and hides the customer base from on-chain view.

Why it matters. Payment architecture determines whether a network's demand concentration can be observed on-chain at all. Helium's direct-burn model: burn-signer HHI 0.27, Top-5 demand sources roughly 90% of burn volume. Livepeer's direct-fee model: fee-payer HHI 0.31. GEODNET's Foundation buy-and-burn relocates demand off-chain behind a single treasury signer. Helium's burn-mint parity crossing was driven jointly by the August 2025 emission halving and the full carrier-revenue burn policy.

What triggers re-evaluation. A change in S2R trajectory (as distinct from level), an emission halving or other supply-compression event, a shift in demand concentration or payment architecture, or a fiscal-parity crossing.

Governance-Concentration Diagnostic

Diagnostic · Governance design

What it does. Tests whether initial token allocation predicts realized governance concentration using a 52-protocol cross-section corrected for protocol-controlled addresses (PCAs) across DePIN, DeFi (decentralized finance), L1/L2 (Layer 1 and Layer 2) infrastructure, and social tokens. Lead correlation is a bounded null (insider allocation Pearson r = 0.09, p = 0.55, N = 50).

Why it matters. Sector membership predicts concentration (DePIN more concentrated than DeFi after correction, Mann-Whitney p = 0.028, Cohen's d = 0.65); insider-wallet retention is the operating mechanism (Spearman rho = 0.44, p = 0.005); delegation amplifies voting concentration above holdings in 13 of 18 protocols. The methodological core is a generalizable five-class protocol-controlled-address exclusion typology that removes 133 addresses across 38 protocols and corrects a median 2.3x (maximum roughly 18x) inflation in prior holder-list studies.

What triggers re-evaluation. A sector-membership shift, a change in insider retention, a delegation-pattern change, or a new dataset that revises the institutional-design controls.

Adaptive-Tokenomics Stress Audit

Stress design · Mechanism demonstration

What it does. Integrates productive staking, reputation-weighted governance, burn-mint value capture, constitutional governance, and PID-controlled adaptive emissions (PID is a proportional-integral-derivative feedback controller, a standard control architecture used to stabilize systems against disturbance), then stress-tests the full stack across five years of adverse scenarios.

Why it matters. Integrated design surfaces structural properties that no single-mechanism analysis can. Results are mechanism demonstrations under stated assumptions, not deployment forecasts. MeshNet is a fictional but empirically parameterized DePIN network used to illustrate the framework.

What triggers re-evaluation. A new stress scenario, a parameter range not previously modeled, or a real-deployment data point that contradicts the simulation.

Extending the frameworks to AI operators

CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index) and MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack) carry from stablecoin gateways to AI deployments without losing their structure. The CLII-AI and MVEP-AI extension tracks transfer the same control-layer vocabulary (routing defaults, hold authority, monitoring hooks, exception handling, and non-compensatory gates) to AI operators, deployment classes, and autonomous payment rails. First external deployment: the FSB Sound Practices for Responsible Adoption of AI consultation response (July 2026).

CLII-AI (Control Layer Intensity Index for AI Operators)

AI operator monitoring · 5 regulatory-posture dimensions

What it does. Extends the continuous-monitoring index to AI operators across five regulatory-posture dimensions: Regulatory Licensing, Evaluation Transparency, Access Gating, Compliance Infrastructure, and Geographic Restrictions. Like the stablecoin CLII, it measures who is supervised and what controls attach at the operator layer. Whether a model is safe is a separate question.

Why it matters. An advisory warning states intent, whereas an armed gate enforces execution. On the live Agent Infrastructure fleet, the highest-volume advisory warning repeated in 84% of workspaces after firing, whereas armed hard gates refused the prohibited action 199 times in 200 (13,098 of 13,156 attempts across both machines). In agentic payments (Who Controls Agentic Payments?), facilitator concentration across x402 on Base, Solana, and Polygon clears merger-guidelines thresholds on every chain.

MVEP-AI (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack for AI Operators)

AI deployment gate · 10-category diligence standard

What it does. Applies the ten-category diligence-at-decision-point methodology to AI deployment classes: enterprise APIs, frontier chat interfaces, composed AI products, open-weights releases, and agentic systems. Tests whether an AI operator preserves contractual rights, system-of-record integrity, change control, and exception escalation under stress.

Why it matters. Before an institution relies on an AI deployment or delegates execution to an agentic workflow, it must verify what happens when an operator silently changes a deployed model, deprecates an interface, or reclassifies outputs, and confirm that shared records, task leases, and verification checks fail closed rather than passing vacuously.
