### How I Help Teams Make Decisions (3-block)

**Block 1: Explain the system in plain English:** I translate policy, market structure, and technical design into questions non-specialists can follow: who issues the product, where the reserves sit, how money moves, who can pause or route flows, and what changes under stress.

**Block 2: Test whether a tokenized product is institution-ready:** I focus on custody, redemption, settlement, finality, concentration, disclosures, and whether the tokenized version is truly equivalent to the traditional product it claims to represent.

**Block 3: Turn research into action:** I build decision frameworks: if-then scenario maps, evidence checklists, monitoring thresholds, and committee-ready memos. The goal is simple: move forward, ask for more proof, redesign the product, or walk away.

### Selected Research (homepage)

**Heading:** Selected research
**Intro:** A few starting points for readers who want substance without a wall of jargon.

**Routing the Dollar:** A plain-English question sits underneath this paper: when digital dollars move, which institutions actually control the route? This research argues that the important unit of analysis is often the gateway, not just the token.

**Minimum Viable Equivalence Packs:** Before an institution treats a tokenized product as equivalent to its traditional version, that equivalence should be proven under stress. This framework shows what evidence needs to exist before that claim is credible.

**Dollar v3 / The Control Layer War:** This work explains how laws and policy proposals show up in real operations: interfaces, holds, custody, reporting duties, and the firms that end up carrying compliance and control.
