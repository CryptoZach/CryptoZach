Tokenomics & physical network systems (DePIN)

Three papers exploring token-economic simulation and modeling, physical networks that depend on geography, and what makes those systems credible to users and institutions. Read the full tokenomics research overview.

  • SSRN ~6,500 words Mar 5, 2026

    Operational Risk in Token Economies

    What it means: When infrastructure fails, adaptive emission controllers get more stable, but reputation-based governance drifts toward plutocracy, or rule by the biggest holders.

    Why it matters: Emission control and reputation governance need different failure-handling architectures because the same shock strengthens one and degrades the other.

    Who it's for: Issuers, network operators, startup founders.

  • SSRN ~20,900 words Mar 1, 2026

    Adaptive Tokenomics

    What it means: A complete token economy built from scratch, then stress-tested across 240 simulations under four adversarial scenarios.

    Why it matters: Slashing drives supply dynamics more than emission policy under ordinary shocks; emission model choice matters mainly in tail scenarios that single-seed simulations miss.

    Who it's for: Issuers, network operators.

    Why it matters: Helium's transition from subsidy dependence to self-funding operation provides the clearest empirical case for how these transitions work.

    What it means: Token systems are rarely judged by standards of legitimacy. DePIN protocols concentrate governance power roughly twice as much as comparable DeFi systems.
