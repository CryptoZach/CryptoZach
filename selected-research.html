Tokenomics and physical network systems (DePIN)

Research on incentive design, growth mechanics, governance, and the stress dynamics of tokenized physical and digital networks.

Start here if your question is about token incentive design, physical-network economics, validator/participant behavior, or simulation-driven mechanism design.

  • SSRN ~6,500 words Mar 2026

    Operational Risk in Token Economies

    What it is: Simulations calibrated to real failure rates from four major protocols reveal an asymmetry: adaptive emission controllers compress variance after correlated infrastructure shocks while reputation-weighted governance drifts toward concentration as the same shocks penalize smaller operators disproportionately.

    Why it matters: Adaptive controllers interpret supply shocks as signals and adjust emissions to stabilize the network. Reputation systems respond to the same shocks by concentrating influence among operators with the most redundancy, which are typically the most capital-intensive. The result is a strong case for separate failure-handling architectures: one for emission control, one for governance.

    Who it's for: Protocol teams, token designers, network operators.

  • SSRN ~20,900 words Mar 2026

    Adaptive Tokenomics

    What it is: A complete token economy designed from first principles and stress-tested across hundreds of simulations covering four adversarial scenarios: prolonged bear markets, competitor entry, regulatory shocks, and combined stress. Parameters calibrated to real hardware economics, collateral ratios, and governance data from twelve DePIN protocols.

    Why it matters: The drivers of survival change with shock severity. Under ordinary conditions, slashing dominates supply dynamics. Under sustained contraction, adaptive emissions compress worst-case outcomes dramatically, sustaining roughly eight times more active participants than static policy at the bottom of the distribution. The engineering premium emerges only under stress; bull markets produce identical outcomes. Reputation-weighted governance meaningfully reduces voting-power concentration. Pre-planned guardrails are essential: without them, the controller can destabilize the system it is designed to protect, reinforcing the case for simulation-first design methodology.

    Who it's for: Protocol teams, token designers, network operators, investors.

  • Final draft ~13,200 words Mar 2026 Targeting Blockchain: Research and Applications

    GeoDePIN

    What it is: Geographic DePIN protocols face place-specific design challenges: credible proof that coverage is real, coordination between local supply and demand, and incentive systems that support operators whose economics depend on location. A taxonomy of design patterns addresses coverage incentives, spatial verification, and geographic governance, grounded in a longitudinal Helium case study spanning nearly three years of network evolution.

    Why it matters: Helium's transition from subsidy-led growth to usage-supported operation was driven by three forces working together: growing real demand contributed most, followed by declining token price making each revenue dollar remove more tokens from circulation, and emission halvings reducing new supply. The decomposition turns that experience into replicable design patterns. Every empirical claim in the paper is traceable to primary sources through an accompanying validation bundle.

    Who it's for: Protocol teams, network operators, geographic infrastructure founders, academic reviewers.

  • Final draft ~10,058 words Mar 2026 Targeting Frontiers in Blockchain (Hypothesis & Theory)

    Umbrella Thesis: Tokenomics as Distributed Institutional Design

    Tokenomics enables a new class of distributed institution in which programmable enforcement and the dissolution of the capital-labor boundary reshape how ownership is allocated, governed, and legitimized.

    What it is: Token systems are rarely evaluated against standards of institutional legitimacy. Five normative traditions (Kantian publicity, Rawlsian fairness, republican non-domination, Ostromian polycentricity, Hayekian knowledge-use) are operationalized into testable hypotheses and applied to a 12-protocol governance cross-section. DePIN protocols concentrate governance power roughly twice as much as comparable DeFi systems.

    Why it matters: The concentration gap traces to hardware capital barriers: earning tokens requires deploying physical infrastructure, which concentrates emission rewards among well-funded operators who can scale node fleets. Reputation-weighted governance, implemented as non-transferable soulbound tokens with quarterly decay, reduces voting inequality. Earned governance should replace purchased access.

    Who it's for: Policy designers evaluating token governance legitimacy, protocol teams choosing governance mechanisms, and researchers bridging institutional economics with mechanism design.
