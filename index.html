Signature frameworks

Overview defines the objects. Frameworks defines the tools. Research shows the evidence.

Track A Digital Money, Tokenization, and Market Infrastructure

CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index)

Measures how heavily regulated each stablecoin issuer, exchange, or protocol is across five dimensions: licensing, reserve transparency, freeze capability, compliance infrastructure, and geographic restrictions. Each entity receives a weighted score from 0 to 1. A high score means more regulatory obligations. Stress performance is a separate question: Circle and Coinbase had near-identical scores but showed sharply different outcomes during SVB because their banking exposures differed.

View CLII framework

MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack)

Asks three questions about any tokenized product: does the holder have the same legal rights as the traditional version? Does the infrastructure perform the same functions? Does the product behave the same way under stress? Each question is tested through nine categories, each requiring specific evidence (legal opinions, custody attestations, reconciliation logs) and a pass/fail determination. MVEP is the entry gate before launch; CLII monitors whether equivalence holds after approval.

View MVEP framework

Credit Migration Model

Tracks where deposit balances move as stablecoin reserves grow: which banks gain funding, which lose it, and whether the deposits that remain become more concentrated and more vulnerable to rapid withdrawal. The core question: as dollars move from bank deposits into stablecoin reserves, does the funding base that monetary policy operates through change in ways that matter for financial stability? The essay's term "deposit mutation" is the author's synthesis of the Federal Reserve's December 2025 finding that stablecoins can reduce, recycle, or restructure deposits.

View Credit Migration Model

Regime Dashboard

Classifies who controls the operating layer right now: who owns the interface, who sets the default routing, who holds the authority to freeze or delay transactions, and who bears the compliance obligations. Tracks routing defaults, sweep ownership, hold authority, fee structures, and disclosure obligations as they change. The central question is whether the entity responsible for compliance is the same one earning the fees, float, and data.

Tokenomics & DePIN

Who earns the token matters more than who holds it.

Track B asks whether token economies reward productive contribution or concentrate into extraction. Each paper specifies the mechanism, stress-tests it under adversarial conditions, and asks whether the design survives contact with real participant behavior.

The anchor study, Adaptive Tokenomics, is a 240-run Monte Carlo simulation testing whether adaptive emission mechanisms sustain participation under adversarial conditions where static schedules mask path-dependent tail risk.

Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack

A diligence framework that asks three questions about any tokenized product: does the holder have the same legal rights as the traditional version? Does the infrastructure perform the same functions? Does the product behave the same way under stress? Each question is tested through nine categories with specific evidence requirements (legal opinions, custody attestations, reconciliation logs) and a pass/fail determination. During the SVB weekend, Circle's $3.3 billion reserve exposure broke all three claims at the same time, and tier labels, attestation reports, and "institutional grade" designations all failed to surface the risk. This framework replaces pitch-deck review with a repeatable, category-by-category standard.

Dollar v3

Public

2025 · SSRN · Tokenomics / DePIN / Operational Risk

Operational Risk in Token Economies

A 624-configuration simulation using real outage rates from Ethereum, Helium, Geodnet, and Filecoin. Adaptive emission controllers become more resilient under infrastructure shocks (the counterintuitive result: failures strengthen the controller's advantage over static policy). Reputation-weighted governance moves in the opposite direction, and the outcome traces to one design parameter: the minimum uptime a node must maintain to recover its standing. At 99%, real-world outage rates make recovery nearly impossible and reputation scores collapse to 0.18 on a 5.0 scale by year five. At 95%, the system stabilizes. The paper also translates blockchain-specific failures into established operational risk categories that a traditional risk team already works with.
