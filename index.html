Operating model

Ten years in digital assets, first as the founding hire at Borderless Capital, a Web3-focused RIA, where I built the research operating system from scratch, led a four-analyst team, and supported underwriting across roughly 250 investments (~$500M AUM, ~10 institutional funds). I have evaluated 1,500+ opportunities, advised on 100+ tokenomics engagements, and led diligence on 100+ tokenization products.

The work spans payment safety and efficiency, bank funding and liquidity transmission, operational resilience, and financial stability. The analytical commitment across all of it: regulate and audit the operator and the routing layer, not the token or mechanism.

That commitment is operationalized in three frameworks. The CLII (Control Layer Intensity Index) scores custody segregation, reconciliation integrity, and concentration risk across gateway models. The MVEP (Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack) defines nine evidence categories as an institutional gate for tokenization proposals. The five-lever credit-migration framework tracks how balances and credit move across bank and nonbank balance sheets as policy changes.

Research system

Governance. Research agendas are ranked by cost of being wrong. Quality standards are enforced through automated checks: XML validation, cross-reference testing, and claim-to-source tracing. Publication is tied to submission readiness, not calendar dates. Decision logs record what was deferred, why, and what would cause it to be reopened.

Measurement. The focus is on metrics that change decisions: redemption latency and reliability (P99 targets, failure rates under stress), settlement exception rates and resolution times, intraday liquidity constraints at custody and reserve points, and subsidy-to-revenue trajectories that distinguish self-funding networks from inflation-dependent ones.

Control-layer metrics. Screening coverage, hold frequency and duration, routing-default concentration, disclosure speed under stress, recordkeeping integrity, and gateway dependency are tracked continuously. A hold rate crossing 0.5% of transactions signals a regime shift. Volume that doubles while the counterparty base shrinks is the early warning pattern for infrastructure concentration.

User and operator outcomes. Error-resolution performance is measured against defined SLAs. Complaints and appeals are tracked through disposition. Governance participation is measured by stakeholder class. Outcomes reporting distinguishes acknowledgment from remediation.

The preference is always measurable guardrails with named thresholds and assigned owners. Tradeoffs documented before decisions, not rationalized afterward. The work avoids advocacy dressed as analysis, category errors that collapse payment stablecoins into tokenized deposits, and hype cycles that outrun operational evidence.

Private money, bank funding, and credit migration

Stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and tokenized cash-management products differ in liability design, reserve composition, custody, and redemption operations. The central question is not which product wins but how balances and credit move across bank and nonbank balance sheets during adoption and stress. When balances migrate onto Treasury-backed rails designed as narrow money, credit does not disappear; it reroutes. Banks tend to shorten duration and tighten underwriting while nonbank originators expand where they hold a structural advantage. The five-lever framework in Dollar v3 tracks where that migration concentrates and which policy choices accelerate or restrain it.

Settlement and market infrastructure

Settlement finality means different things depending on what is being measured: block confirmation, economic value integrity, or convertibility under stress. The SVB weekend exposed the gap. USDC continued to settle on-chain while the economic value of those settlements diverged by 13 percent. This work covers intraday liquidity mobilization, custody segregation, reconciliation architecture, and the short-term funding infrastructure (reserve eligibility, collateral haircuts, cross-system settlement timing) that becomes binding as tokenized instruments scale. The system-of-record decision is treated as foundational because it constrains everything downstream. The MVEP and CLII frameworks operationalize these issues: nine evidence categories as an entry gate, three scoring dimensions as a continuous drift detector.

The control layer

The control layer is where policy becomes operating reality. CLARITY Section 302 defines the interface boundary. Section 305 makes holds and case-file obligations routine. Section 404 distinguishes permissible activity-based rewards from prohibited interest on the money object. Section 304 creates offshore reporting duties that can become de facto gateway controls. Each provision maps to a specific infrastructure node (interface operators, routing defaults, sweep engines, hold/freeze mechanisms) and to a specific shift in compliance cost, liability, or value capture. The regime classifier tracks ten signals with quantitative thresholds: wrapper sweep share above 50%, regulated interface share above 70% of flows, hold frequency above 0.5% of transactions. When three or more signals flip within a quarter, the regime label updates. Concentration risk, third-party dependency, and failure-mode testing are monitored continuously, not reviewed on a fixed calendar.

Governance, incentives, and stress dynamics

Stress in stablecoin markets propagates vertically through code-based dependencies, not just horizontally across gateways. MakerDAO's Peg Stability Module during the SVB episode is the clearest documented case. In DePIN networks, hardware capital requirements concentrate governance power: measured DePIN governance HHI (0.19, range 0.037 to 0.388) materially exceeds DeFi benchmarks (0.09, range 0.028 to 0.174) across a 12-protocol cross-section. Infrastructure failures create a further asymmetry: adaptive emission controllers compress reward variance after shocks, while reputation systems drift toward plutocracy as correlated downtime penalizes smaller operators disproportionately. These findings translate into operating thresholds and escalation rules: CLII score below 0.50 triggers re-diligence, governance HHI above 0.40 triggers concentration review, bear-scenario PID deviation above 40% activates anti-windup safeguards. Monitoring updates when conditions change, not when the reporting cycle comes due.

Track A · Stablecoin & dollar infrastructure

  • SSRN · FRBNY under review ~20,000 words Mar 12, 2026

    Routing the Dollar: Gateway Infrastructure, Monetary Policy Transmission, and the Dollar's International Functions

    Stablecoin policy usually focuses on the token. This paper argues that the decisive layer is the gateway infrastructure connecting issuers, custodians, and settlement rails, and introduces a Control Layer Intensity Index (CLII) to score custody segregation, reconciliation integrity, and concentration risk across gateway models. The empirical base includes Johansen cointegration tests on USDC/USDT peg dynamics (with regime-dependent rank concentrated in tightening episodes), the SVB stress event tracing vertical contagion through MakerDAO's Peg Stability Module, a 51-address Ethereum counterparty registry, and a multi-chain settlement-flow decomposition. The concentration finding is stark: total dollar volume doubled between 2023 and 2025 while unique counterparties declined 25%, cross-gateway bridging entities halved, and a single market-making firm's share rose from 1.4% to 19.9% (gateway HHI 7,361). Routing architecture, not token design alone, shapes monetary-policy transmission, stress resilience, and the regulatory leverage points that matter most.

    Minimum Viable Equivalence Packs: An Institutional Diligence Framework for Tokenized Products

    Before an institution treats a tokenized product as equivalent to its traditional counterpart, that equivalence should be proven under stress. This paper defines nine evidence categories (rights parity, custody segregation, recordkeeping integrity, reconciliation frequency, finality, insolvency posture, reorg/exception handling, governance and change control, and disclosures) and turns them into a versioned diligence pack. The USDC/SVB depeg anchors the framework: Circle's $3.3 billion reserve exposure at SVB broke all three equivalence dimensions simultaneously. Legal equivalence failed when rights parity and claim priority depended on a single custodian's solvency. Operational equivalence failed when monthly attestation cycles could not detect a crisis moving in hours. Economic equivalence failed when USDC traded at $0.87 while on-chain settlement continued at face value. The MVEP gives committees a pass/fail gate to apply before any tokenization proposal moves forward.

    Dollar v3 Follow-Up: The Control Layer War

    This follow-up maps the Dollar v3 taxonomy onto CLARITY and GENIUS. It argues that the real policy battle is not over labels but over where compliance is embedded and which firms are required to enforce it. Track B · DePIN & tokenomics

  • SSRN ~6,500 words Mar 5, 2026

    Operational Risk in Token Economies: How Infrastructure Failures Strengthen Adaptive Controllers and Break Reputation Systems

    What happens to token mechanisms when the underlying infrastructure fails? In a 624-run simulation calibrated to observed failure rates from Ethereum, Helium, Geodnet, and Filecoin, this paper finds an asymmetry: PID emission controllers compress reward variance after infrastructure shocks (the adaptive mechanism becomes more stabilizing), while reputation-weighted governance drifts toward plutocracy as correlated downtime disproportionately penalizes smaller operators who cannot absorb extended outages. The implication is direct: emission control and reputation governance require different failure-handling architectures, because the same shock that strengthens one mechanism degrades the other.

  • SSRN ~20,900 words Mar 1, 2026

    Adaptive Tokenomics: A Systems Engineering Approach to Programmable Incentive Design

    This paper builds a complete token economy for a fictional DePIN network (MeshNet) using a systems engineering lifecycle, then stress-tests it with a 240-run Monte Carlo ensemble (30 seeds across 8 configurations) under four adversarial scenarios (bull, bear, competitor entry, regulatory shock). Twenty-one exhibits document the results, supported by sensitivity sweeps across 60 PID gain configurations, 40 slashing parameter settings, and 16 evaluation-interval variants. Key findings: slashing, more than emission policy, drives supply dynamics under ordinary shocks; PID-controlled emissions compress reward variance but accumulate integral wind-up under sustained demand contraction (bear scenario: 44.6% deviation from node-count target); and the emission model choice becomes decisive mainly in tail scenarios that single-seed simulations miss entirely. The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck price process is fitted directly to HNT daily returns (May 2023 through February 2026), and MeshNet's parameters are calibrated against observed Helium and Filecoin ranges to operate within the plausible DePIN design space rather than favorable assumptions.

  • Final draft ~13,200 words Mar 2026

    GeoDePIN: Design Patterns for Decentralized Geographic Infrastructure

    Geo-dependent DePIN networks face problems that purely digital systems do not: they must verify physical coverage, respond to locally heterogeneous demand, and support operators whose economics depend on place-specific hardware deployment. This paper develops a taxonomy for those design problems, tracks Helium's subsidy-to-revenue ratio from 0.013 to 2.06 across 34 months of on-chain data, and decomposes the transition from subsidy dependence to net-deflationary operation: fee growth accounts for 58% of the 12-month improvement (weekly Data Credit burns surging 20-fold), HNT price decline for 25%, and the HIP-147 emission halving for 17%. Twelve tables tie each design claim to validated data sources (29 of 29 paper claims verified in the data bundle, 24 exact matches and 5 defensible close matches). Practitioner design tools cover coverage incentives, spatial verification, and geographic governance. Journal submission pending.

  • Final draft ~11,500 words Mar 2026

    Umbrella Thesis: Tokenomics as Distributed Institutional Design

    Token design borrows from economics and mechanism design, but it is rarely judged by standards of legitimacy. This paper operationalizes five normative traditions (Kantian publicity through on-chain verifiability, Rawlsian fairness through emission allocation design, republican non-domination through veto rights and power balance, Ostromian polycentricity through tiered committee governance, Hayekian knowledge-use through PID control using local price and coverage signals) into testable hypotheses, then grounds them in a governance concentration cross-section of twelve protocols (seven DeFi, five DePIN) using on-chain holder data from Dune Analytics and Cosmos LCD snapshots. The empirical result: DePIN governance concentration (mean HHI 0.19, range 0.037 to 0.388) materially exceeds DeFi benchmarks (mean HHI 0.09, range 0.028 to 0.174), with token inequality Gini coefficients of 0.67 to 0.98 near-universal across the sample. The gap reflects hardware capital barriers: earning tokens requires deploying physical infrastructure, which concentrates emissions among operators who can scale node fleets. The normative framework and the empirical finding converge on the same design prescription: earned access over purchased access. Journal submission pending.

What I Build

Decision frameworks for digital money: clearer questions, stronger evidence, measurable thresholds, and defined escalation paths.

Research agendas and scenario architecture

Identify the questions that block a decision and rank them by the cost of being wrong. Translate market-entry sequences, liquidity shocks, and infrastructure failures into decision trees with named thresholds and assigned owners. If gateway concentration rises above 0.60, review exposure. If redemption queues exceed 48 hours, reallocate reserves. Leadership gets numeric tripwires and defined responses, not slide decks hedged with qualifiers.

Simulation and empirical evidence

Build agent-based models calibrated to observed data (Helium attrition, Filecoin sector faults, Ethereum fee dynamics), not assumptions. Run hundreds of stress paths under adverse scenarios to expose failure modes that single-path analysis cannot reach. Tie every empirical claim to a versioned query, API pull, or replication script. A new analyst should be able to regenerate every table and exhibit without the original author.

Taxonomies, scoring, and diligence frameworks

Build classification and scoring systems (gateway-risk taxonomies, tokenization-equivalence checklists, governance concentration indices) that produce repeatable, auditable results. Design pre-committee gate checks specifying required evidence bundles, redemption-stress scenarios, and breach thresholds. Missing evidence sends a proposal back with a defined deficiency list; a declining CLII or HHI score triggers escalation and counterparty review.

Policy-to-operations mapping

Trace proposed legislation to the settlement rails, custody workflows, and compliance obligations it reshapes, and to the shifts in liability and value capture it produces. Identify which issuers, custodians, and interface operators gain or lose as each provision takes effect, making the distributional consequences visible before commitments are made. The output is an operational impact grid: build, defer, or stop.

Control-layer audit and measurement

Audit frameworks that target the operator and the routing layer instead of the token. Locate where liability, compliance, and value capture concentrate; identify single points of failure; produce key-control inventories, reconciliation-point maps, and escalation protocols that regulators and counterparties can audit. Every metric has a source, update interval, designated owner, and threshold tied to a specific response. If a metric does not trigger action, it does not stay on the dashboard.

Escalation workflows and board materials

Assign named owners and defined procedures for when a threshold is breached or evidence deteriorates: memo, data request, supervisory inquiry, policy option set. Decision memos, option sets, and "what would change the view" one-pagers give committees actionable input instead of raw research. Every recommendation ties to the evidence, the conditions that would reverse it, and the owner of the next step.
