Routing the Dollar

Why gateways often matter more than the token itself.

Dollar stablecoins move $60.6 trillion across nine blockchains, but the same token inherits different compliance, monetary, and safe-haven properties depending on which gateway routes it. Introduces the Control Layer Intensity Index (CLII), scoring custody segregation, reconciliation integrity, and concentration risk across 19 entities and 51 addresses. During the SVB crisis, capital rotated across dollar gateways rather than exiting dollar stablecoins; Tier 1 share collapsed from 63% to 13% over a weekend.

What I work on

The recurring problems are structural: they show up in reserves, settlement, routing, concentration, and accountability.

Private money, bank funding, and credit migration

Stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and tokenized cash-management products: liability design, reserve custody/composition, redemption operations, and how balances and credit migrate across bank and nonbank balance sheets under adoption and stress.

Settlement & market infrastructure

Settlement finality, how cash and collateral move within a trading day, custody segregation and reconciliation, and the short-term funding infrastructure that becomes binding as tokenized instruments scale.

The control layer

Where policy becomes operational reality: screening, holds, routing defaults, disclosures, and recordkeeping. Focus areas include concentration risk, third-party dependency, failure-mode testing, and whether hold/freeze/appeal workflows actually work as designed.

Governance, incentives & stress dynamics

Bank-run and redemption-run dynamics, incentive misalignment, and operational/cyber concentration. I translate mechanisms into scenarios, thresholds, and escalation playbooks that keep views current as conditions change.

What I build

Each deliverable is built for a team to make a decision, run a check, or escalate a risk.

Scenario architecture

Priority-ranked decision trees, stress paths, and named thresholds that define what changes the view. 1,880-run Monte Carlo ensembles calibrated to real hardware economics and observed failure rates.

Product checklists

Pass-fail diligence across custody, settlement, finality, insolvency posture, and governance controls. Nine categories structured around what risk committees ask, backed by audit logs of observed use and tested controls.

Regulation to product spec

Legislation mapped to specific infrastructure nodes: which section attaches to which operator, what compliance it compels, and where liability concentrates. Operating playbooks tied to specific sections, operators, and compliance primitives.

Decision memos and monitoring frameworks

Recommendations clear enough for a committee vote, with quantitative thresholds, named triggers to move forward, redesign, or stop, and CLII-based drift detection tracking whether the control surface holds post-approval.

How the research program is run

Evidence-led

Every claim traces to a primary source, a validated dataset, or a stress simulation with named parameters. Replication packages accompany each paper so the work can be rerun and challenged.

Action-linked thresholds

A metric earns its place when it changes a decision, assigns an owner, or triggers escalation. Monitoring systems include named triggers that guide whether to proceed, redesign, or stop.

Published on readiness

Work ships when the evidence is mature and the checks are complete, with versioned manuscripts, frozen checksums, and public replication code.

If a claim cannot be tied to a source, threshold, or tested condition, it does not belong in the final deliverable.
