Agent infrastructure

We are building an operating layer for AI agent fleets.

Teams are beginning to run many AI coding agents against the same codebase. At that scale, three problems compound: agents overwrite one another's work, record false claims that later sessions accept as fact, and rely on safeguards without measuring whether those safeguards change behavior.

We built an operating layer to address those failures. It has run on our fleet since March 2026, and we judge each control by what happens after it fires. Warnings that only advised the agent often appeared again in the same workspace, indicating that advice alone was not reliably changing behavior.

The operating layer also preserves identity, provenance, and attribution for work that enters the shared record: which agent and session produced it, what evidence and prior state it relied on, and the exact commit that wrote it into that record.

About Tokenization Systems

An independent research, advisory, and infrastructure practice for digital financial systems: stablecoins, tokenized deposits, the settlement rails that move value between institutions, and token-economy design. The work holds itself to the standard it applies to the systems it studies: claims trace to sources, frameworks are tested against adversarial conditions, and boundaries are named explicitly.

FounderZach Zukowski. Six years as Senior Investment Analyst and founding research hire at Borderless Capital, a Web3-focused registered investment adviser.

FrameworksControl Layer Intensity Index (CLII), a 0-to-1 score for how heavily regulated a gateway is, where a gateway is any issuer or distributor routing dollars between on-chain and off-chain rails. Minimum Viable Equivalence Pack (MVEP), a ten-category diligence standard for tokenized money and securities, run by the institution taking the risk rather than paid for by the issuer. MVEP is the entry gate, checked once before you commit; CLII is the ongoing check that flags when a product drifts away from what it cleared on.

ProgramEight working papers on SSRN and eighteen federal comment letters across the GENIUS Act and related rulemaking dockets, organized in two tracks:
Track A (stablecoins, gateways, and settlement) and Track B (tokenomics; DePIN, meaning decentralized physical infrastructure networks; and protocol-network governance).

Patent-pending systemsThirty filed USPTO applications across twenty families. Most describe infrastructure for running many AI agents on one shared codebase at the same time: keeping their edits from overwriting each other, requiring every output to point at the evidence behind it, keeping a tamper-evident record of what changed and who changed it, and governing when an agent may start, stop, or hand unfinished work to another. The rest cover measurement itself, and the attribution of control in pseudonymous on-chain systems. One describes the combination: how those mechanisms compose into one architecture.
