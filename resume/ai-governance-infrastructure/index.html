Supporting research

Five papers that ground this profile in empirical work and committee-grade frameworks.

Public

Routing the Dollar

What it is: A data-driven study of 19 gateway entities and 51 wallet addresses showing that the same dollar token inherits different regulatory obligations, different Fed-policy sensitivity, and different crisis behavior depending on who routes it. Introduces the CLII, which measures how heavily regulated each gateway is across five dimensions.

Why it matters: Moves analysis from token labels to operators, reserve chains, and routing networks, with named metrics for concentration and stress behavior.

Who it's for: Policy, central-bank, market-structure, and infrastructure readers building transferable analogies to AI routing and operator accountability.

Prepared for the FRBNY Dollar Conference.

Under review

Governance Concentration Beyond Token Allocation

What it is: Cross-sectional analysis of 40 protocols (15 DePIN, 15 DeFi, 8 L1/L2 infrastructure, 2 social or stagnated). Allocation design does not predict governance concentration (Pearson r = 0.19, p = 0.25, N = 37); sector membership does (Mann-Whitney p = 0.031, Cohen's d = 0.96). Five-lens institutional-design framework (Kantian publicity, Rawlsian legitimacy, Pettit non-domination, Ostromian polycentricity, Hayekian knowledge-use) and a Blockscout-verified exclusion of 69 protocol-controlled addresses across 20 protocols.

Why it matters: Gives the program a criteria layer for evaluating protocol governance alongside simulation results, connecting token and infrastructure design to standards of institutional legitimacy that AI governance work also needs. Lead-finding framing is "allocation null plus four supporting findings", not "no covariate predicts concentration."

Who it's for: Governance designers, DAO researchers, protocol teams, and AI governance readers who need explicit fairness and accountability criteria.

Under review at Frontiers in Blockchain. SSRN 6599278.

Public

Who Burns the Tokens?

What it is: A within-sector GeoDePIN study of Helium and GEODNET: Subsidy-to-Revenue, burn-mint equilibrium, and demand concentration by burn-mechanism design (not network size). Uses the Governance Concentration exclusion methodology and Ostromian polycentricity framing; fiscal trajectory and the HIP-147 halving in Helium’s BME path.

Why it matters: Pairs the cross-sectional protocol governance in Governance Concentration with a vertical, operator-scale story about who pays, how fast sustainability arrives, and how design choices split concentration.

Who it’s for: DePIN, protocol, and tokenomics readers who need governance criteria plus measured fiscal and burn-side mechanics.

SSRN 6483619. Submission to Blockchain: Research and Applications (Elsevier) pending.

Public

Tokenized Equity Vehicles

What it is: Comparative launch analysis of two tokenized-equity architectures (Robinhood Stock Tokens and Backed xStocks): legal form, investor claim, custody chain, liquidity architecture, and corporate-action treatment.

Why it matters: Shows how custody, settlement, and counterparty choices concentrate operational responsibility and failure modes across products that look similar on the surface, with diligence questions that translate cleanly to AI deployment reviews.

Who it's for: Asset managers, market-infrastructure leads, and product strategists who need a shared vocabulary with policy and risk teams, including AI deployment teams that mirror custody and counterparty questions.
