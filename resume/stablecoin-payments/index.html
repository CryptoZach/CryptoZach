Can the product move money under stress?

Reserve location, redemption logic, custody dependencies, and cross-provider movement determine whether users can actually exit, settle, or reroute when conditions tighten. The stress test: a redemption request arrives at 2am Saturday. The token settles in seconds. The underlying reserve will not mobilize until Monday. The product must document how this gap is managed.

Who actually controls failure handling?

Holds, routing defaults, partner dependencies, and application-layer obligations reveal which team or outside institution really owns the problem when something breaks. The compelled operator is typically the interface, router, or default owner. Required artifacts: screening, policy routing, audit logs, and hold workflows.

What should change the launch plan?

Named thresholds should trigger redesign, slower rollout, tighter reserve standards, or stronger reporting before the product scales into avoidable operational risk. In the Adaptive Tokenomics simulations, PID emission control functions as downside insurance (compressing worst-case variance by 6x), but the advantage is conditional: under sustained demand contraction, the controller can underperform because it applies a supply-side lever to a demand-side problem.
